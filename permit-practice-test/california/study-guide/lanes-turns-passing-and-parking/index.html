California Permit Study Guide: Lane Markings, Turns, Merging, Passing and Parking

Section 6 of the California Driver’s Handbook, Navigating the Roads, is full of the details knowledge tests love: which lines you may cross, how far you may drive in a turn lane, and how many feet to stay away from a fire hydrant. This lesson goes through it in order.

Lane markings

  • Single solid yellow line: the center of a two-way road. If your direction has only one lane and the solid yellow line is on your side, do not pass.
  • Double solid yellow lines: never pass over them. Stay to their right, except to use an HOV lane whose entrance is on the left, when signs or construction send you to the other side, or to turn left across one set of them into or out of a driveway or private road, or to make a U-turn.
  • Two sets of double yellow lines two feet or more apart form a barrier: it must not be driven on or crossed for a left turn or U-turn, except where an opening is provided.
  • Broken yellow line: you may pass when the broken line is on your side, if it is safe.
  • Single solid white line: separates lanes going the same way, including on one-way streets.
  • Double solid white lines: a barrier between a regular lane and a special one such as a carpool lane, also seen near freeway ramps. Never change lanes across them; wait for a single broken white line.
  • Broken white lines: separate lanes going the same way on roads with two or more such lanes.
  • Large broken lines usually mean your lane is ending or will exit the freeway: watch for the sign telling you to exit or merge.
  • Yield line: a row of solid white triangles pointing at you, showing where to yield or stop.

Choosing and changing lanes

Lanes are numbered from the left: the far-left (fast) lane is the Number 1 Lane, the next one the Number 2 Lane, and so on. Passing and left turns belong in the left lane; entering or leaving traffic, and pulling out from the curb or shoulder, in the right lane.

Before changing lanes, signal, check your mirrors and the cars around and behind you, turn your head toward the lane you want, check your blind spots for cars, motorcycles and bikes, and make sure there is room. You do not need to slow down to change lanes. Stay in one lane as much as you can and do not weave. Once you enter an intersection, keep going; once you start a turn, finish it. If you miss your turn, drive on until you can turn around safely and legally.

Special lanes

  • Passing lane: on a multilane road, the far-left lane next to the center divider.
  • Carpool (HOV) lanes are marked with a diamond and the words Carpool Lane. You may use one if you carry the minimum number of people posted (signs also give the hours), if you drive a low or zero emission vehicle with its special DMV decal, or if you ride a motorcycle (unless signs say otherwise). Use the marked entrances and exits; never cross double solid lines to get in or out.
  • Center left turn lane: in the middle of a two-way street, edged on each side by a solid outer line and a broken inner line. Use it only to start and make a left turn or U-turn, never to pass or travel, and for no more than 200 feet. Watch for oncoming drivers using the same lane, signal, check your blind spot, move all the way into it, and turn when it is safe.
  • Turnouts on two-lane roads let slower vehicles pull aside. You must use one when you are driving slowly, passing is unsafe, and five or more vehicles are lined up behind you.
  • Bicycle lanes usually have a solid white line and signs, sometimes green paint. You may drive in one only to park where allowed, to enter or leave the road, or to turn within 200 feet of an intersection. Check your blind spot first. Other bike facilities include buffered lanes, bike routes, bicycle boulevards, separated (protected) bikeways and shared-lane markings that tell you a cyclist may use the whole lane.

Turns

When you stop for a turn, stop at the limit line, a wide white line before the intersection or crosswalk. With no limit line, stop before the crosswalk; with no crosswalk, stop before the intersection.

Right turns

Move close to the right edge (enter a right turn lane at its opening; you may enter a bike lane within 200 feet of the turn after checking for cyclists). Watch for people walking, cycling or riding between you and the curb. Start signaling roughly 100 feet ahead of the corner, glance back over your right shoulder and slow down, stop at the limit line, look left, right and left again, turn when it is safe, and finish in the right lane without swinging wide.

  • On a red light you may turn right after a full stop unless a NO TURN ON RED sign is posted.
  • On a red arrow you may not turn right: wait for green.
  • You may not drive, stop or park in a lane marked for buses only, but you may cross it to turn right.
  • A dedicated right turn lane that does not merge into another lane lets you turn without stopping, even when through traffic has a red light, unless a light or sign at the right curb says otherwise. Always yield to people in the crosswalk.

Left turns

Get next to the center line or into the left turn lane (through its opening, never across a solid line; a two-way center turn lane may be entered once you are 200 feet or less from the corner, yielding to anyone already using it). Signal 100 feet ahead of the corner, glance over your left shoulder, slow down, stop at the limit line, look left, right and left again, and turn when it is safe, ending in the left lane. Keep your wheels pointed straight until you start turning: if a car hits you from behind while your wheels are turned left, the impact could shove you into oncoming traffic. Speed up smoothly and let the wheel straighten in the new lane.

You may turn left on a red light only from a one-way street onto another one-way street, if no sign forbids it, after yielding to everyone with a green light.

Which lane to end in

  • Two-way street to two-way street: start and end in the left lane nearest the middle of the road.
  • Right turn: start and end in the lane nearest the right edge.
  • Onto a one-way street, or from one one-way street to another: start from the far-left lane (the far-right lane for a right turn). With three or more lanes going your way, you may end in any open lane (any lane, if safe, for a right turn between one-way streets).
  • Leaving a one-way street for a two-way street: finish in the left lane closest to the center line.
  • Where a one-way street ends at a two-way street (a T intersection), cars going straight on the through street go first, and you may turn left or right from the middle lane.

U-turns

Signal, and turn from the left turn lane or the far-left lane. U-turns are allowed:

  • over a double yellow line;
  • in a residential district when no vehicle is approaching within 200 feet;
  • at an intersection with a green light or green arrow, unless a sign forbids U-turns;
  • through an opening in the divider of a divided highway.

They are forbidden:

  • where a sign prohibits them;
  • on or at railroad tracks;
  • across the middle of a divided highway, whether the divider there is two sets of double yellow lines, a curb or a strip of land;
  • when your view is shorter than 200 feet in either direction;
  • on one-way streets;
  • in front of a fire station (and its driveway is never a place to turn around);
  • in business districts, other than at an intersection or a divider opening.

Braking, merging and exiting

To stop, take your foot off the accelerator, let the car slow, and press the brake gently until you stop. Do not cross a limit line, and when stopping behind a car leave enough room to see its rear tires.

Highway traffic has the right-of-way when you merge. Get into the right lane of the on-ramp, reach the speed of traffic, and merge into a gap big enough for your car without stopping unless you really must. Use mirrors and signals, glance over your shoulder, leave three seconds of space to the car ahead, never cross solid lines, and cross several lanes one at a time, checking blind spots each time.

To exit, know where your exit is, change lanes one at a time, and once in the right lane signal five seconds (about 400 feet) before the exit, at a safe speed, without crossing solid lines.

To cross or join traffic from a stop, you need a gap of about half a block (150 feet) on city streets and a full block (300 feet) on highways. Do not start across on a green light if people or cars block your way. When turning left, do not assume an oncoming car with its right signal on will turn before reaching you: wait until it starts turning.

Passing

Decide whether there is room before you pass whenever you approach oncoming traffic or a cyclist, or a hill, curve, intersection or obstruction: you want a hill or curve to be one-third of a mile or more ahead. Do not pass:

  • near a hill or curve when you cannot see whether traffic is coming;
  • when you are 100 feet or less from an intersection, a bridge, a tunnel, a railroad crossing or another dangerous spot;
  • where crossroads or driveways meet the road;
  • without enough room to get back in your lane.

To pass on an open highway, signal, check your blind spot, move into the passing lane, speed up, then signal and return to your lane. You may pass on the right only on an open highway with two or more lanes your way, when the driver ahead is turning left and there is room, or on a one-way street; never pass on the left of a driver signaling a left turn, and never leave the pavement to pass. When someone passes you, keep your lane and your speed.

Parking

Parallel parking

  1. Look for a gap three feet or more longer than your car, and signal.
  2. Pull up beside the car in front of the space, about two feet away, and stop when your rear bumper lines up with the front of the space. Keep signaling.
  3. Check your mirrors and look over your shoulder for traffic and pedestrians.
  4. Back in at about a 45-degree angle.
  5. Once the rear wheel gets to about 18 inches from the curb, steer the other way to straighten out, pulling forward and back if needed, until the car is parallel to the curb and no more than 18 inches from it.

Then turn off the engine, set the parking brake and check for bikes, motorcycles and cars before opening your door.

Straight-line backing (a drive test skill): signal as you pull to the curb, check traffic and blind spots, then back up in a straight line for three car lengths while staying within three feet of the curb, slowly and smoothly.

Parking on a hill

Set the parking brake and leave the car in park (or in gear with a manual transmission). Then turn the front wheels:

  • Downhill: into the curb (or toward the side of the road).
  • Uphill: away from the curb, toward the center of the road, letting the car roll back a few inches until a wheel gently rests against the curb.
  • No curb, uphill or downhill: to the right, so the car would roll off the road rather than into it.
  • Sloping driveway: so the car would not roll into the street.

Colored curbs

  • White: stop only to pick up or drop off passengers.
  • Green: limited-time parking (time on a sign or the curb).
  • Yellow: loading and unloading passengers or freight, no longer than posted; drivers of noncommercial vehicles usually must stay with the car.
  • Red: you may not stop, stand or park there; only buses may stop in a red zone marked for them.
  • Blue: disabled parking, for a person with a disabled placard or plate or someone driving them.

Where you may never park

Never leave your car wherever a NO PARKING sign says so; on any crosswalk, painted or not; on a sidewalk or partly over one; across a driveway; closer than three feet to a sidewalk ramp for people with disabilities, or in front of a curb cut for wheelchairs; on the striped (crosshatched) area beside a disabled space; in a charging or fueling space for zero-emission vehicles unless yours is one; in a tunnel or on a bridge unless signs allow it; closer than 15 feet to a fire hydrant or the driveway of a fire station; closer than 20 feet to a crosswalk (15 feet where the curb sticks out into the street); between a safety zone and the curb; double parked; facing the wrong way; or on a freeway, unless there is an emergency, an officer tells you to stop, or stopping there is specifically allowed. If you have to stop on a freeway, pull fully off the pavement, stay inside and lock the doors. A car left on a freeway longer than four hours may be towed away.

Driving green

Speed up and slow down gently, keep a steady speed, keep tires inflated, change the oil and filters on time, and carry no extra weight: you save fuel and cut emissions.

If an officer stops you

Turn on your right signal to show you have seen the officer, pull completely onto the right shoulder (even from the carpool lane), in a well-lit spot if you can, and turn off the radio. Stay inside unless told to get out, lower your window before the officer reaches you, and keep everyone’s hands in view. You must show your license, proof of insurance and registration. The officer must tell you the reason for the stop before asking questions about a crime or a traffic violation (unless holding it back is needed to protect life or property), and it must appear on the ticket or report.

You may say no when an officer asks permission to do something such as search your car, but you may not interfere if the officer goes ahead; the officer may have legal authority to search in some cases. Passengers may decline to give their names, though in some situations they must identify themselves. In California, only federal officers may ask about immigration status, and you may refuse to answer a state or local officer who asks. You may generally record the stop in public if you say so right away and do not interfere or reach into hidden places without permission. Never resist physically, even if you think your rights are being violated: object in words, and file a complaint with the officer’s agency afterward, which no government employee may punish you for.

Remember for the test

  • Double solid yellow: never pass over it (exceptions: HOV entrance on the left, signs or construction, left turn into a driveway or a U-turn across one set).
  • Center left turn lane: at most 200 feet. Bike lane: only within 200 feet of your turn.
  • Signal 100 feet before turning; five seconds (about 400 feet) before a freeway exit.
  • Merging gap: 150 feet in the city, 300 feet on the highway. No passing within 100 feet of an intersection, bridge, tunnel or railroad crossing.
  • U-turn in a residential area only if no vehicle is within 200 feet; never where you cannot see 200 feet each way.
  • Hills: downhill wheels to the curb, uphill wheels away, no curb wheels to the right.
  • Parking: 15 feet from a hydrant, 20 feet from a crosswalk, three feet from a sidewalk ramp. Red curb: no stopping. White: passengers. Green: limited time. Yellow: loading. Blue: disabled.
