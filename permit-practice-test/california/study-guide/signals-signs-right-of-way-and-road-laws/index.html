Section 7 of the handbook, Laws and Rules of the Road, is the longest part of the knowledge test material. Learn it in four blocks: signals and signs, right-of-way, sharing the road, and the special limits and penalties.

Traffic lights

Whatever the light says, you still yield to people walking or cycling and to vehicles that have the right-of-way.

Light What you do
Solid red Stop. You may then turn right unless a NO TURN ON RED sign is posted, after stopping at the limit line and yielding to pedestrians.
Red arrow Stop and do not turn until a green light or green arrow comes on.
Flashing red Stop, then go when it is safe, like a stop sign.
Solid yellow Red is coming next: stop if you can do it safely; if not, cross carefully.
Yellow arrow Your protected turn is ending. If you cannot stop safely or are already in the intersection, finish the turn carefully, and watch the next signal.
Flashing yellow Slow down and be alert; you do not have to stop.
Flashing yellow arrow You may turn left, but it is not protected: yield to oncoming traffic first.
Solid green Go, but only when the way is clear, and stop for anyone still in the intersection. Do not enter if you cannot get all the way across before the light turns red.
Green arrow Go in the arrow’s direction: a protected turn, with oncoming traffic held by a red light.
Light not working Treat the intersection as an all-way stop, then go carefully.

Pedestrian signals

A WALK signal or walking person means people may cross; DON’T WALK or a raised hand means they may not. A flashing DON’T WALK or hand means do not start crossing, but drivers must still yield to people who are crossing. Some signals count down the seconds left, some intersections let people cross diagonally in every direction at once, and beeps, chirps or voice messages help blind pedestrians. Where there is no pedestrian signal, pedestrians follow the vehicle lights.

Signs

Obey every warning sign, whatever its shape or color.

  • STOP sign: come to a complete stop at the limit line or before the crosswalk (before the intersection if there is neither), then check every direction before going.
  • Red YIELD sign: ease off and be prepared to let any vehicle, bicyclist or person on foot go first.
  • Red and white signs are rules: DO NOT ENTER means just that. If DO NOT ENTER and WRONG WAY signs face you, you are going against traffic: back out or turn around when it is safe. At night, road reflectors that shine red in your headlights also tell you that you are going the wrong way.
  • A red circle with a red line across it forbids whatever the picture shows.
  • Round yellow-and-black or X-shaped (crossbuck) signs: a railroad crossing ahead. Look, listen, slow down and be ready to stop for trains. A blue-and-white sign at many crossings says what to do in an emergency or if your car stalls on the tracks.
  • Five-sided sign: you are close to a school; go slowly and stop for any child in the crosswalk.
  • Diamond sign: a warning about road conditions or dangers ahead (curves, merging traffic, lane ends, signals ahead and so on).
  • White rectangular sign: an important rule you must follow.

Right-of-way: who goes first

The first vehicle to reach an intersection has the right-of-way, and everyone else waits. Never assume others will yield to you, and give up your right-of-way whenever it helps avoid a collision. People walking always have the right-of-way.

  • No STOP or YIELD signs: first to arrive goes first. If you arrive at the same time as a vehicle, bicyclist or pedestrian, yield to the one on your right. At an all-way stop, stop first, then apply the same rule.
  • T intersection with no signs: traffic on the through road goes first.
  • Turning left: yield to people walking and to any oncoming vehicle close enough to be a danger.
  • Turning right: look for people crossing and for bikes and motorcycles beside you.
  • Green light: go carefully; pedestrians still come first.
  • Entering traffic: yield to the vehicles already in the lanes. Never stop in or block an intersection you cannot clear before the light turns red: it is illegal.

Roundabouts

Traffic flows one way, counterclockwise, around a central island. Slow down as you approach, yield to everyone already circling, and enter to the right when the gap is big enough. Follow the signs and lane markings, do not stop or pass inside, signal when you change lanes or leave, and if you miss your exit, go around again. In a roundabout with two lanes: to turn right, use the right lane and exit from it; to go straight, use either lane and exit from the lane you entered; to turn left, enter and keep circling until you reach the exit you want.

Pedestrians

Pedestrians (vulnerable road users) include people on foot, people on skates or skateboards, and people with disabilities using a tricycle, quadricycle or wheelchair. They must obey traffic rules too, but whenever someone is crossing, with or without a crosswalk, slow down or stop so they can finish safely.

  • Crosswalks may be marked with white lines, or yellow near schools, or not marked at all; people walking have the right-of-way in all of them. Stop at the limit line if there is one.
  • Never pass a car stopped at a crosswalk: it may be hiding someone who is crossing.
  • A person who makes eye contact with you is about to cross: yield. Give older people, parents with small children and people with disabilities extra time.
  • At crosswalks that have flashing lights, look for people crossing even when the lights are off.
  • People with a guide dog or white cane always have the right-of-way. Never stop your car inside a crosswalk (they could be forced to walk around it into traffic), never honk at them, and take extra care when turning or backing in a quiet hybrid or electric car. A blind person who pulls in the cane and steps back from the corner is usually telling you to go ahead.

Mountain roads

When two vehicles meet on a steep, narrow road with no room to pass, the driver going uphill goes first. The downhill driver has better control when reversing up the hill, so that driver backs up until the other can get by.

Sharing the road

Trucks and large vehicles

Big rigs have large blind spots, the No Zones: when the truck’s side mirrors are out of your sight, you are out of the driver’s sight. Trucks also need far more room to stop: a typical car at 55 mph stops within about 300 feet, while a large vehicle may need up to 400 feet, and a loaded truck needs more than an empty one. So never cut in front of a truck and then slow down or stop.

On turns, a truck’s rear wheels follow a shorter path than its front wheels, which is why trucks swing wide; check a truck’s turn signals before passing, because it may be swinging the opposite way to make its turn. On divided highways with four or more lanes in one direction, trucks may use the two right lanes. Do not change lanes right in front of a truck, do not ride alongside one longer than necessary (pass on the left, then get ahead of it), do not tailgate, and do not underestimate a truck’s size and speed: it often looks slower than it is.

Buses, streetcars and light rail

Safety zones for people waiting for buses, streetcars and trolleys are marked by raised buttons or markers: never drive through one. You may pass a bus, streetcar or trolley that has stopped at a safety zone or a traffic light, but at 10 mph or less. Overtaking a streetcar or light rail vehicle on its left side, whether it is moving or stopped, is not allowed unless the tracks are too close to the right side to pass there, you are on a one-way street, or an officer directs you. Light rail vehicles have the same rights as other vehicles; never turn in front of an approaching one, keep a safe distance, check for one before turning across the tracks, and turn only when the light allows it.

Motorcycles

Motorcyclists have the same rights and duties as drivers but disappear easily in blind spots. Check for them when changing lanes or entering a road, keep a three-second following distance, give them the full lane when you can (lane splitting is legal in California), never try to pass a motorcycle in its own lane, check before opening your door, and when a rider wants to pass, shift toward one side of your lane. Road conditions can make riders change speed or direction suddenly.

Emergency vehicles

When a police car, fire engine, ambulance or other emergency vehicle comes with its siren on and red lights flashing, yield: pull over to the right edge and stop, and wait there until it has gone by. If you are already in an intersection, keep going through it, then pull right and stop. For a stopped emergency vehicle or tow truck with flashing lights, or a stopped road work vehicle with flashing or amber lights, move over and slow down. Obey police officers and firefighters even when their orders conflict with signs or signals. Following within 300 feet of an emergency vehicle with its siren or lights on is illegal, and driving to the scene of a fire, crash or disaster to watch can get you arrested.

Slow vehicles, animals and bicycles

  • Slow-moving vehicles often carry an orange and red triangle; road maintenance vehicles usually travel at 25 mph or less. Neighborhood electric vehicles and low-speed vehicles (signs: NEV USE ONLY, NEV ROUTE) reach 25 mph at most and may not use roads with limits above 35 mph. Scooters and golf carts may also be on the road.
  • Horse riders and horse-drawn vehicles may share the road; scaring horses or livestock on purpose is against the law. Near animals, slow down or stop and follow the directions of whoever is in charge of them.
  • Bicyclists have the same rights and duties as drivers. They may use some freeway sections with no alternative route, move left to avoid hazards, ride near the left edge of a one-way street, or walk across a crosswalk. They must ride with traffic, obey signs and signals, yield to pedestrians, wear a helmet if under 18, have working brakes, and at night carry a white front lamp visible from 300 feet, a red rear reflector or light visible from 500 feet, and pedal (or shoe or ankle) reflectors visible from 200 feet.
  • Passing a cyclist: change lanes if you can. If you cannot, leave at least three feet between your car and the bicycle, and if three feet is not possible, wait. Merge behind cyclists before turning, and do not move into a bike lane until you are 200 feet or less from your turn.

Work zones

Slow down, leave extra space, expect sudden stops and lane changes, avoid distractions, follow the cones, drums and flaggers, and merge early without crossing the cones. A ticket in a work zone can cost $1,000 or more, assaulting a highway worker can bring a fine of up to $2,000 and up to one year in jail, and fines double in Safety Enhanced-Double Fine Zones and in construction or maintenance zones while workers are present.

Hazardous loads, towing and slow driving

Trucks with diamond-shaped hazardous load placards must stop before railroad tracks. In heavy traffic or bad weather you must slow down, but driving too slowly to block normal traffic can also get you a ticket; if you want to drive slower than others, use the right lane, and move right when someone behind wants to go faster. When towing a vehicle or trailer or driving a truck with three or more axles, drive in the rightmost lane, or in a lane signed for slow vehicles; with four or more lanes your way and no markings, stay in the two right lanes.

Special speed limits

  • Schools: 25 mph when you are 500 feet or less from a school while children are out of class or crossing; a few school zones are posted as low as 15 mph. Obey crossing guards and safety patrols.
  • School buses: flashing yellow lights mean the bus is preparing to stop: slow down. Flashing red lights mean stop, from either direction, and stay stopped until the children have crossed and the lights stop. Failing to stop can cost up to $1,000 and a one-year suspension. The exception: a bus on the far side of a divided highway, or of a highway with two or more lanes in each direction; there, you do not stop.
  • Blind intersections (no stop signs at any corner and a blocked view): 15 mph, creeping forward until you can see.
  • Alleys (roads no wider than 25 feet giving access to the back or side of buildings): 15 mph.
  • Railroad crossings: 15 mph within 100 feet of a crossing where you cannot see 400 feet down the tracks both ways, unless gates, a signal or a flagger control it. When the lights flash or a train is coming, stop 15 feet or more from the closest rail and stay there until the lights stop flashing, even if the gate goes up. Never go around or under gates; if they are down and no train comes, call the posted railroad number or 911. A train can come on any track, at any hour, from either side, so never stop on the tracks.
  • Business and residential districts: 25 mph unless posted otherwise.

Other laws to know

  • Smoking with a minor in the car is against the law.
  • Dumping or abandoning an animal on a highway is a crime: up to $1,000 in fines and up to six months in jail, or both.
  • Do not drive so loaded that you cannot control the car or see ahead and to the sides, or with an unsecured load. Nothing may stick out past the left fenders or more than 6 inches past the right fenders of a passenger car. A load sticking out more than 4 feet behind the rear bumper needs a 12-inch red or fluorescent orange flag, and two red lights at night.
  • People may ride in the back of a pickup only with secure seats and seat belts; animals there must be secured.
  • A video screen visible to the driver may show only vehicle information, navigation, a media player or the radio.
  • Never throw a lit cigarette or anything burning from a car.
  • Keep the windshield and front side windows clear: nothing hanging from the mirror. Stickers are allowed only in a 7-inch square at the lower corner of the passenger side of the windshield or rear window, a 5-inch square at the lower corner of the driver’s window, a 5-inch square at the top center of the windshield for a toll device, and on side windows behind the driver.
  • Do not cut into a funeral procession (led by an officer, cars with headlights on and windshield markers).
  • Keep your license plate readable and unaltered.
  • Fleeing from the police is a misdemeanor (up to one year in county jail); causing serious injury during a pursuit can mean up to seven years in state prison, and manslaughter during a pursuit 4 to 10 years. A car driven by an unlicensed person may be impounded for 30 days. Reckless driving or a speed contest that injures someone can bring jail, a fine or both.

Points, suspensions and teen rules

When you sign a ticket, you promise to appear in court; failing to appear (FTA) goes on your record and the DMV may suspend you until you appear. Convictions and collisions remain on your driver record for at least 36 months, depending on the offense. An adult may be suspended for 4 points within 12 months, 6 within 24 months or 8 within 36 months. For a one-point violation, a judge may let you attend traffic violator school once in any 18-month period to keep the ticket from your insurance company (it stays on your record). Too many points makes you a negligent driver: the DMV may put you on probation, suspend or revoke your license, and you have the right to a hearing. Hit-and-run or reckless driving causing injury means revocation, and you need proof of financial responsibility (SR 22/SR 1P) to get a license back afterward.

Provisional (teen) drivers who have collisions or violations in the first 12 months may be restricted or suspended:

  • two at-fault collisions or convictions (or one of each): 30 days of driving only with a licensed adult aged 25 or older;
  • three: six months suspended, then one year of probation;
  • a conviction for alcohol or drugs at ages 15 to 20: a one-year suspension or a one-year delay before you can apply.

Turning 18 does not wipe out restrictions, suspensions or probation. To contest a DMV action, you must ask for a hearing no later than 10 days after being served, or 14 days after the notice was mailed; you may bring evidence and witnesses, testify, have a representative at your own cost and question the evidence. Anyone can ask the DMV to reexamine a driver they believe is no longer safe. Your driver record is public except for physical or mental conditions, your address and your social security number.

Remember for the test

  • Flashing red = stop, then go. Flashing yellow = slow down, no stop. Red arrow = no turn. Dead traffic light = all-way stop.
  • Five-sided sign = school. Round yellow or X-shaped sign = railroad crossing. Red reflectors at night = wrong way.
  • Same-time arrival: yield to the right. Uphill driver has the right-of-way on narrow mountain roads.
  • Pass a stopped bus or streetcar at a safety zone at no more than 10 mph. Stay 300 feet behind emergency vehicles. Three feet to pass a cyclist.
  • Speed limits: 25 mph near schools and in business or residential districts; 15 mph at blind intersections, in alleys and near railroad crossings with poor visibility.
  • School bus flashing red: stop both ways (not across a divided or multilane highway).
  • Points: 4 in 12 months, 6 in 24, 8 in 36. Teen with three collisions or violations: six months suspended.
