How to Get a CDL: Federal Requirements Step by Step

Updated November 19, 2026

Not a government website. CDL Test Prep is an independent study site, not affiliated with any DMV, state agency or the FMCSA. Official information: FMCSA CDL program, 49 CFR Part 383, FMCSA ELDT rules and your state’s DMV (USAGov). All official sources

Your state issues your commercial driver’s license (CDL), but the main steps come from federal rules that every state applies (49 CFR Part 383). This guide puts those steps in order: who can apply, the DOT medical card, the commercial learner’s permit (CLP), entry-level driver training, the knowledge and skills tests, and what each endorsement takes. Every requirement links to the federal rule or agency page it comes from, and we checked each one on November 19, 2026. Federal rules are the minimum: your state may add steps, fees and waiting times, so confirm the details with your state’s licensing agency before you apply.

The steps to a CDL at a glance

  1. 1

    Check that you can apply

    At least 18, a valid driver’s license from your state and no other license. Driving in interstate commerce takes age 21.

  2. 2

    Get your DOT medical card

    From a certified medical examiner, if you will drive in non-excepted interstate commerce.

  3. 3

    Pass the knowledge tests and get your CLP

    General Knowledge plus the tests for your vehicle and endorsements, at least 80% correct on each.

  4. 4

    Complete entry-level driver training

    For a first Class A or B CDL, an upgrade from B to A and a first P, S or H endorsement.

  5. 5

    Pass the skills test

    No sooner than 14 days after your CLP was first issued: vehicle inspection, basic control and on-road driving.

  6. 6

    Keep your CDL valid

    Renew your medical card, keep your Clearinghouse status clear and avoid disqualifying offenses.

Do you need a CDL?

Under the federal standard described in the CDL manual, you need a CDL to drive a single vehicle with a gross vehicle weight rating (GVWR) of 26,001 pounds or more, a combination rated at 26,001 pounds or more when the towed vehicle is rated at more than 10,000 pounds, a vehicle designed to carry 16 or more people counting the driver, or a vehicle of any size that needs hazardous materials placards or carries a listed select agent or toxin. Your vehicle decides the class you need (A, B or C), and special vehicles and loads need endorsements. Our guide CDL Classes, Endorsement Codes and Restriction Codes explains each class, endorsement and restriction code.

Who can get a CDL: the basic requirements

Age

You must be at least 18, and prove your age, to get a commercial learner’s permit, which comes before the CDL (49 CFR 383.71). To drive a commercial vehicle in interstate commerce, for example across state lines, federal rules require you to be at least 21 (49 CFR 391.11). Under 21, your state’s rules decide what you may drive within the state.

One license, from the state that issues your permit

You need a valid driver’s license issued by the same state that issues your CLP (49 CFR 383.25). A commercial driver may hold only one driver’s license at a time (49 CFR 383.21), and when you apply you certify that you are not disqualified and do not hold a license from more than one state (49 CFR 383.71).

English

Interstate commercial drivers must read and speak English well enough to talk with the public, understand traffic signs and signals in English, answer questions from officials and fill in reports and records (49 CFR 391.11). FMCSA’s roadside enforcement policy, revised on April 16, 2026, tells inspectors how to check this in English and covers placing drivers who fail out of service. The CDL skills test is also given in English, as explained in step 4.

If your home is outside the United States

A non-domiciled CLP or CDL is for drivers whose home (domicile) is in a foreign country. Under an FMCSA final rule in effect since March 16, 2026, a state may issue one only to applicants in lawful H-2A, H-2B or E-2 status, and it is valid for one year at most, or less if the applicant’s authorized stay ends sooner. See FMCSA’s Non-Domiciled CDL 2026 Final Rule FAQs for the details.

Step 1: Get your DOT medical card

When you apply for a CLP, you certify which of four types of commercial driving you will do: non-excepted interstate, excepted interstate, non-excepted intrastate or excepted intrastate (49 CFR 383.71). If you certify non-excepted interstate driving, you must be examined and certified as medically qualified, and since June 23, 2025 FMCSA sends the certificate information to your state electronically. Your state licensing agency can tell you which type fits the driving you plan to do.

  • The examiner must be listed on FMCSA’s National Registry of Certified Medical Examiners, where you can search by city, state or ZIP code. This has been required since May 21, 2014.
  • A new exam is due when your last medical certification is more than 24 months old (49 CFR 391.45), so plan on an exam about every two years.

Step 2: Pass the knowledge tests and get your CLP

To get a commercial learner’s permit, you must (49 CFR 383.71):

  • be at least 18 and prove your age;
  • pass the General Knowledge test for the type of vehicle you will drive;
  • pass the knowledge test of each P, S or N endorsement you want on the permit;
  • certify your type of driving, that you are not disqualified and that you hold no license from another state.

Each knowledge test takes at least 80% correct to pass (49 CFR 383.135). Depending on your vehicle you may also need other knowledge tests, such as Air Brakes or Combination Vehicles: the classes guide shows who takes which test. Start with our CDL General Knowledge practice test.

What a CLP allows, and its limits

The federal rules for the permit (49 CFR 383.25):

  • It lets you drive a commercial vehicle on public roads for behind-the-wheel training, and only with a CDL holder of the right class and endorsements supervising you from the front seat. In a passenger vehicle, the CDL holder may sit directly behind you or in the first row behind you instead.
  • Only the P, S and N endorsements can go on a CLP, each with its own knowledge test and a restriction: no passengers with P or S, and empty tanks only with N. A CLP holder may not haul hazardous materials.
  • It is valid for one year at most from the day it was first issued. After that, you retake the knowledge tests.
  • You need a CLP before your first CDL, and before an upgrade that needs a skills test, such as from Class B to Class A.
  • You cannot take the CDL skills test in the first 14 days after your CLP was first issued.

What language are the knowledge tests in?

Federal rules let states give the knowledge tests in another language, as long as no interpreter is used (49 CFR 383.133). Some states give them only in English, for example Texas since June 1, 2026, so check with your state before you book. Our practice tests are in English.

Step 3: Complete entry-level driver training (ELDT)

If your CLP was issued on or after February 7, 2022, federal rules require entry-level driver training before some tests (FMCSA, ELDT Applicability):

You are applying forFinish the training before
A first Class A or Class B CDLThe CDL skills test
An upgrade from Class B to Class AThe CDL skills test
A first P or S endorsementThe P or S skills test
A first H endorsementThe H knowledge test

What the training involves (FMCSA, ELDT training requirements FAQ):

  • It must come from a training provider listed on FMCSA’s Training Provider Registry.
  • There is no minimum number of hours, but you must score at least 80% overall on the theory assessment.
  • The theory part can be taken online.
  • Behind-the-wheel training for Class A or B includes both range and public-road driving. For P and S, range or public road is accepted.
  • The H endorsement needs theory training only, with no behind-the-wheel part.

The N (tank) and T (doubles and triples) endorsements do not need ELDT on their own.

Step 4: Pass the CDL skills test

The skills test has three parts: a pre-trip vehicle inspection, basic vehicle control and on-road driving (49 CFR 383.113). They are taken in that order, and each part must be passed before the next (49 CFR 383.133).

  • Take it in a vehicle of the class you are applying for. The vehicle you use can add restrictions to your license, for example E (no manual transmission) or L (no air brakes), as our classes guide explains.
  • It is given in English: interpreters are not allowed, and neither you nor the examiner may use another language during the test (49 CFR 383.133).
  • A driving simulator cannot replace the on-street driving (49 CFR 383.113).
  • Wait at least 14 days after your CLP was first issued, and finish ELDT first if it applies to you.

CDL endorsement requirements at a glance

Endorsements let you drive special vehicles or haul special loads. Each one needs a knowledge test, and some need more (49 CFR 383.93). Open an endorsement for its full federal requirements and a free practice test.

Endorsement Tests ELDT (first time) On a CLP
H, Hazardous Materials Knowledge test, plus a TSA security threat assessment Theory only, before the knowledge test No
N, Tank Vehicle (tanker) Knowledge test Not needed Yes, empty tanks only
P, Passenger Knowledge and skills tests Before the skills test Yes, no passengers
S, School Bus Knowledge and skills tests Before the skills test Yes, no passengers
T, Double and Triple Trailers Knowledge test Not needed No

X on a license stands for the tank and HazMat endorsements together, so it takes both knowledge tests, the HazMat theory training (the first time) and the TSA threat assessment. Sources: 49 CFR 383.93 (tests), FMCSA ELDT rules, 49 CFR 383.25 (CLP) and 49 CFR 383.141 with TSA’s HAZMAT endorsement page (threat assessment).

Keeping your CDL

  • Medical card. Get a new exam before your certification is more than 24 months old (49 CFR 391.45).
  • Drug and Alcohol Clearinghouse. Since November 18, 2024, a driver whose Clearinghouse status is “prohibited” loses, or cannot get, a CDL or CLP: the state removes the commercial driving privileges. They come back only after the return-to-duty process is complete and the status is no longer prohibited (FMCSA Clearinghouse).
  • Disqualifying offenses. Some convictions take away your right to drive a commercial vehicle for a time. Our lessons explain the CDL manual’s rules on alcohol and drugs and on texting and hand-held phones.
  • HazMat endorsement. TSA says the endorsement generally has to be renewed every five years, with new fingerprints (TSA).
  • English and one license. Both rules above apply for as long as you drive.

Your state’s rules

Your state issues the CLP and CDL, sets the fees and test appointments, tells you which documents to bring and may add requirements of its own. Find your state’s licensing agency on USAGov’s list of state motor vehicle services.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to get a CDL?

There is no fixed federal time. The only federal waiting period is 14 days between first getting your CLP and taking the skills test, and ELDT has no minimum number of hours, so the total depends on your training, your state and test appointments.

Can I get a CDL at 18?

You can get a CLP at 18. To drive a commercial vehicle in interstate commerce you must be at least 21; under 21, your state’s rules decide what you may drive within the state.

Do I need ELDT for the tanker endorsement?

No. ELDT is required for a first Class A or B CDL, an upgrade from B to A and a first P, S or H endorsement, not for N. If you want the X endorsement (tank and HazMat), the HazMat part needs theory training.

Which endorsements need a skills test?

Only P (passenger) and S (school bus) need a skills test as well as a knowledge test. H, N and T need a knowledge test only (49 CFR 383.93).

Can I take the CDL test in Spanish?

It depends on your state. Federal rules allow knowledge tests in other languages without an interpreter, but some states, such as Texas since June 1, 2026, give them in English only. The skills test is always given in English.

How long is a CDL learner’s permit valid?

One year at most from the day it was first issued. A state may issue it for a shorter time and renew it, but not past one year in total without retaking the knowledge tests (49 CFR 383.25).

What score do I need on the CDL knowledge tests?

At least 80% correct on each test (49 CFR 383.135).
