CDL Skills Test: Basic Control Exercises and the Road Test Explained

After the pre-trip inspection come the basic control exercises and the on-road test. Here is how each exercise works, how points are counted and what the examiner checks on every turn, merge and crossing.

  • Manual sections 12 and 13

The CDL skills test has three parts taken in order: the vehicle inspection, the basic control skills test and the road test. You must pass each one before moving on, in the type of vehicle you want to be licensed for. The previous lesson covers the inspection; this one covers the other two, following Sections 12 and 13 of the CDL manual.

The basic control skills test

These exercises show that you can control the vehicle and judge where it is in relation to lines and cones. They may happen on a closed course or somewhere along the road test route. The examiner explains each exercise first, and you may ask questions (and, if allowed, ask to walk through the exercise area).

How it is scored

What is counted What it means
Pull-ups Pulling forward during a backing exercise to fix your position. Stopping without changing direction is not a pull-up. A few are free; too many count as errors.
Encroachments Touching or crossing a boundary line or cone with any part of the vehicle. Mirrors, and unusual parts mounted at or above door-mirror height (a bucket truck’s bucket, for example), do not count. Each one is an error, and the examiner stops you so you can get back inside the boundaries.
Looks Stopping to get out and check your position, opening the door, leaving your seat, or on a bus walking to the back to see better. Each counts as a look.
Final position Where the vehicle ends up in the two backing exercises. Ending outside the final boundaries as described fails the basic control test.

Before you get out for a look, set the parking brake and put the vehicle in neutral or park, then climb down facing the vehicle with three points of contact (on a bus, face forward and hold the handrail). Opening the door while the vehicle is moving, or forgetting the parking brake, is an unsafe act and can fail you immediately. If the examiner raises a hand, stop at once and bring the vehicle back within the exercise lines.

At the end of each exercise, set the parking brake, shift to neutral or park and sound the horn to tell the examiner you are done.

The four exercises

  1. Forward stop. Drive forward through the alley and stop once, with the front of the vehicle inside the stop box at the end and not past it. The examiner shows you which part counts as the front. You lose points if it is not in the box.
  2. Straight-line backing. Pull forward when told and stop at the examiner’s raised hand. Now reverse the whole length of the alley in a straight line, until your front end is past the last pair of cones. The examiner counts pull-ups and encroachments and checks your final position. You get one free pull-up and may get out to look one time.
  3. Forward offset tracking. Drive ahead and swing left through the gap into the other lane, keeping the right-hand rear tire inside the space marked by the cone and the line. You may not stop, back up or get out: stopping ends the exercise. Stop when you are parallel to the outer boundary, at or before the far cones.
  4. Reverse offset backing. From that position, back into the alley and park with the rear of the vehicle inside the 3-foot box at the end, straight and completely inside the boundaries. Backing past the rear of the alley means pulling straight forward past the alley entrance before trying again. You get two free pull-ups and up to two looks.

Tips: back slowly, use both mirrors, take your free look when you are unsure, and correct early rather than late. A small correction costs nothing; an encroachment or a bad final position costs points.

The road test

You drive a route with a mix of traffic situations, following the examiner’s directions, which come early enough for you to act. Throughout the test you must wear your seat belt, obey every sign, signal and law, and finish without a crash or a moving violation. You are scored on specific maneuvers and on your driving overall. If the route lacks a situation, you may be asked to describe what you would do.

Turns

  • Before the turn: check traffic in every direction, signal, and get into the right lane early.
  • Approaching: signal, slow down smoothly and change gears to keep power. Do not coast: rolling with the clutch pressed or the gearshift in neutral for a distance longer than your own vehicle counts as unsafe coasting.
  • If you must stop: stop smoothly behind the stop line, crosswalk or sign; behind another vehicle, stop where you can still see its rear tires. Do not roll, and keep the front wheels straight.
  • During the turn: check traffic, keep both hands on the wheel and watch the mirror so the inside of the turn hits nothing. Do not swing into oncoming traffic, and finish in the correct lane.
  • After: cancel the signal, get up to speed and move to the right-most lane when it is safe.

Intersections

Check all directions, slow down gently and brake smoothly. If you must stop, stop fully behind the line or sign, with a safe gap to the vehicle ahead, and do not roll. While crossing, give way to people and vehicles already in the intersection, stay in your lane and keep your hands on the wheel. Afterwards keep checking your mirrors and accelerate smoothly.

City driving, lane changes and expressways

  • City streets: check traffic regularly, keep a safe following distance, stay centered in the right-most lane and keep up with traffic without going over the speed limit.
  • Lane changes: you will be asked to move left and then back right. Check traffic first, signal, and move over smoothly only when it is safe.
  • Expressways: check traffic and signal before merging smoothly into the right lane; keep your lane position, spacing and speed while on it; when exiting, check traffic, signal, slow down in the exit lane and keep slowing on the ramp with safe spacing.

Stopping at the roadside and starting again

You will be asked to pull over as if to check something on the vehicle.

  • Stopping: check traffic, signal right, slow down and brake evenly to a full stop without coasting, parallel to the curb or shoulder and out of the traffic flow, not blocking driveways, hydrants, intersections or signs. Then cancel the turn signal, turn on the four-way flashers, set the parking brake, shift to neutral or park and take your feet off the pedals.
  • Starting: check traffic and mirrors, turn off the flashers, signal left, release the parking brake and move off straight. Keep the wheel still until the vehicle is rolling. Check traffic again, especially on the left, merge smoothly and cancel the signal.

Curves

Slow down before the curve so you do not need to brake or shift in it, stay in your lane and keep checking traffic.

Railroad crossings

Every driver should slow down and shift as needed before the crossing, look and listen for trains, and check traffic. Never stop, shift, pass or change lanes while any part of the vehicle is on the crossing.

Drivers of buses, school buses and placarded vehicles should be ready to do more at every crossing that is not exempt:

  1. Turn on the four-way flashers as you approach.
  2. Stop no closer than 15 feet and no farther than 50 feet from the nearest rail.
  3. Look and listen both ways for trains and signals. A bus driver may also have to open the window and door.
  4. Cross with both hands on the wheel, without stopping, shifting or changing lanes.
  5. Turn off the flashers once across and keep checking your mirrors and traffic.

Not every route has a crossing, so you may have to explain and show the procedure at a pretend one.

Bridges, overpasses and signs

After an overpass you may be asked what clearance or height was posted; after a bridge, what weight limit was posted. Read every sign on the route and be ready to explain any of them.

School bus: student discharge

School bus applicants also show a student drop-off:

  • Approaching: slow down while checking traffic, turn on the amber warning lights and the right signal, move as far right as you can, and check traffic again.
  • Stopping: stop at least 10 feet away from the students, shift to neutral or park, set the parking brake, then turn on the stop arm and red lights.
  • Discharging and crossing: talk to the students, check traffic, open the door, and keep checking for students while they cross.
  • Leaving: check all mirrors, turn off the warning lights and stop arm, close the door and check traffic before you move.

The school bus lesson covers loading and unloading in detail.

What can fail you outright

  • Leaving the seat or opening the door without setting the parking brake, or opening the door while moving.
  • Not following the examiner’s instructions, or not finishing an exercise as directed.
  • A crash or moving violation during the road test.
  • Ending a backing exercise outside the final boundaries.
