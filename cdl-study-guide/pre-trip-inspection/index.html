CDL Pre-Trip Inspection: The Complete Vehicle Inspection Test Checklist

What the examiner expects at the vehicle inspection test, every item to check from the cab to the coupling, how to explain each one out loud, and the mistakes that end the test.

The vehicle inspection test, often called the pre-trip inspection, is the first part of the CDL skills test. You show the examiner that you can tell whether your vehicle is safe to drive. It is not enough to look: for every safety item you must name it, point to or touch it, and explain what you are checking it for. An item you skip or do not explain earns no credit. This lesson follows Section 11 of the CDL manual.

The rules of the test

  • You take it in the vehicle you will be licensed in. A vehicle with parts marked or labeled by anyone other than the manufacturer cannot be used.
  • You inspect one axle during the test, but you must be ready to inspect any of them the same way.
  • You can use the manual’s checklist and tick items off, but you may not write anything else on it beforehand. You do not have to crawl under the vehicle.
  • Chock the wheels for safety. Whenever you get out, set the parking brake and put the vehicle in neutral.
  • Climb in and out facing the vehicle with three points of contact (on a bus, face forward and hold the handrail).
  • Not following the examiner’s instructions, or an unsafe act such as forgetting the parking brake, can fail you on the spot. If an instruction is unclear, ask.

1. In the cab and at engine start

Item What you check and say
Dashboard indicators Left and right turn signal, four-way flasher and high beam indicators light up; the ABS light comes on and goes off (on a combination, the trailer ABS lamp on the rear driver’s side too); the DEF light works, if fitted.
Emergency equipment Spare fuses, or you point out that the vehicle uses circuit breakers; a charged, secured fire extinguisher; three red reflective triangles, or six fusees, or three liquid burning flares.
Windshield and mirrors Glass clean, no cracks, nothing blocking your view except an inspection sticker; mirrors (or cameras and their screen) clean and adjusted.
Wipers and washers Arms and blades secure and undamaged; turn the wipers on and off; washer fluid present and the washers spray.
Heater and defroster Show that they work using the controls.
Horn Air horn and/or electric horn sound.
Parking brake With the parking brake set (trailer brakes released on a combination), take your foot off the pedal and pull forward gently: the vehicle must not move.
Trailer brake (combination) Parking brake released, trailer parking brake set, pull forward gently: the trailer brakes must hold. This also checks the coupling.
Service brake Pull forward at about 5 mph and brake to a full stop. The vehicle must stop well and not pull to either side.

2. The brake system check

Air brakes: the four-part check

If you do not explain and perform all four parts correctly, you fail the vehicle inspection test.

  1. Air gauge and governor cut-out. Chock the wheels if needed, start the engine and check that the gauge works. Build the pressure until the governor cuts out, usually between 120 and 140 psi or the maker’s setting, and say where that is.
  2. Leakage rate. Engine off, key on, parking brake released (and on a combination the tractor protection valve). Press the brake pedal fully, let the needle settle after the first drop, then hold for one minute. The loss may not exceed 3 psi for a single vehicle or 4 psi for a combination.
  3. Low air warning. Without restarting, fan the pedal to lower the pressure and say when the buzzer, light or flag comes on. It must come on before the pressure falls below 55 psi.
  4. Emergency brakes. Keep fanning. The parking brake valve should pop out somewhere between 20 and 45 psi, or at the maker’s setting. On air-braked trailers, the tractor protection valve and trailer valve should pop out at the same moment or earlier. If the valves are not made to pop out, listen for the air that tells you the brakes applied. With primary and secondary systems, explain that losing pressure in only one of them will not make the valves pop out.

Know the manufacturer’s numbers for the vehicle you test in: the examiner expects you to state them.

Hydraulic brakes

Also an automatic fail if you get it wrong. Check that the warning lights work. With the transmission in “park”, note the normal pedal height, then pump the pedal three times and hold it down for five seconds (or follow the maker’s method): the pedal should stay firm with reserve travel and not sink. If there is a hydraulic power assist, show that it works.

3. Bus and school bus items

All buses:

  • Entry door and lift: the door is undamaged, opens smoothly and closes tightly from inside; handrails are secure; step lights work; steps are clear with tread that is not loose or worn. A wheelchair lift must not leak or have damaged or missing parts, and must be fully stowed and latched.
  • Emergency exits: every exit is labeled, undamaged, works smoothly and closes tightly. Open at least one to show it works, describe how the others open, check the release handles from inside and outside, and check the exit warning devices with the key on.
  • Seats: frames not broken and firmly attached to the floor; cushions secured to the frames.
  • Passenger mirrors and cameras: clean, adjusted, brackets straight and secure; on a school bus, the crossover mirrors too.

School buses only:

  • Student lights, front and rear: strobe light (if fitted), alternately flashing amber lights (if fitted) and alternately flashing red lights all work, with their dashboard indicators.
  • Stop arm and safety arm: securely mounted, nothing loose or broken, extending fully, stop arm lights and indicator working. The safety arm keeps students crossing well in front of the bus, for example 10 feet away, where you can see them.
  • First aid kit and body fluid cleanup kit: present, with the seal unbroken.

4. Lights check

Walk around the outside and check that every light works when switched on: turn signals, four-way flashers, low and high beams, clearance and marker lights, taillights, and brake lights that come on with the brakes and go off when released. On a combination you only check the rear of the trailer. Lead the examiner around the vehicle, name each light clearly, and ask for help if you need someone to work the switches. A light you do not name earns no credit.

5. Outside the vehicle

Front and engine area (engine off)

  • Lenses: right color, clean, not broken or missing.
  • Fluid levels: show where you would check engine oil, coolant, power steering fluid and (hydraulic brakes only) brake fluid, and say the level must be between the add and full marks. Never open the radiator cap on a hot engine.
  • Leaks: no puddles under the vehicle, no drips around the engine and transmission, hoses and air lines in good shape.
  • Steering: the steering box is secure and not leaking; hoses are not cracked or leaking; the links, arms and rods running out to the wheel show no wear or cracks; joints are not loose; no nuts, bolts or cotter pins are missing.

Wheels, suspension and brakes (the axle you inspect)

Item What you check
Tires Inflation with a tire gauge (kicking a tire or tapping it with a mallet earns no credit); no cuts that expose the plies, no bumps or bulges, valve caps and stems in place, dual tires of matching size. Minimum tread depth: 4/32 inch on steering axle tires and 2/32 inch on all others. With an automatic inflation or pressure monitoring system connected to the valve stems, say so and check that it works.
Rims Not cracked, bent or damaged, no welding repairs, no rust trails showing a loose rim, bolt holes not stretched out of round.
Lug nuts All present, none loose (look for rust trails or shiny threads), no bent, broken or missing studs.
Suspension Leaf springs not missing, shifted, cracked or broken; coil springs not broken or distorted; mounts not cracked; U-bolts and mounting bolts not loose or missing; air bags and shocks secure and not leaking; the vehicle sits level. On buses where these parts are hidden, tell the examiner and check that the bus is not leaning.
Brake lines and hoses Not leaking, cracked, worn or frayed; couplings and fittings secure. Electric brake wiring secure, with no worn or cracked casing.
Brake contamination Where you can see them, no grease or oil on the linings, pads, drums or discs: it reduces braking and can start a fire.

Sides and rear

  • Lenses and reflectors on the sides and rear: right color, clean, not broken or missing.
  • Mirrors and cameras: clean, not cracked, brackets straight and secure.
  • Battery: secure, connections tight without heavy corrosion, cables not cracked or worn, box or cover closed. If you cannot see the batteries, say so and describe what you would check.
  • Fuel and DEF tanks: mounted securely, caps tight, no leaks from the tanks or lines.
  • Frame and cargo floor: no cracks, broken welds or holes; on a trailer, the tandem release lever and pins secure.

6. Combination vehicles

  • Air and electric lines: connectors seated, sealed and locked on both the tractor and the trailer (you must check both ends for credit); hoses and cables not cut, chafed, spliced, taped or worn through; nothing tangled, pinched or dragging. Listen for air leaks.
  • Fifth wheel: skid plate securely mounted, no cracks or heavy wear, lubricated if required. Kingpin in place and not bent or worn, apron not cracked, and the trailer lying flat on the skid plate with no gap between apron and fifth wheel. Look into the gap: the locking jaws must be closed around the kingpin with no play, the release arm engaged and the safety latch in place, and sliding fifth wheel pins fully engaged.
  • Pintle hook and drawbar: hook secure on the frame with no loose or missing parts; drawbar ring or eye not bent, twisted or worn and firmly attached to the tongue; tongue free of dents, twists, cracks and broken welds. Latch locked, safety pins in place, safety chains secure and crossed, breakaway cable connected.
  • Other hitches: describe how your system works and check the mounting, the ball, the coupler and the tongue the same way.
  • Landing gear: fully raised, crank handle secured, frame and pads not damaged, no air or hydraulic leaks if powered, and the fifth wheel placed so the tractor frame clears the landing gear in turns.

How to explain an item: an example

Examiners score what you say. A good explanation names the part, says what it does and lists the faults you are looking for. For example, at the steering linkage:

“This is the steering linkage, from the steering box to the wheel. I’m checking the links, arms and rods for wear and cracks, making sure the joints and sockets are tight, and that no nuts, bolts or cotter pins are missing.”

Do the same for every item on the checklist, out loud, in the same order each time you practice.

Mistakes that cost points or fail the test

  • Leaving the cab without setting the parking brake and putting the vehicle in neutral.
  • Getting one of the four air brake check steps wrong, or not knowing the manufacturer’s pressures.
  • Checking tire pressure by kicking the tire instead of using a gauge.
  • Looking at an item without naming it and saying what you are checking.
  • Forgetting to check the connections on both the tractor and the trailer.
  • Not naming each light during the lights check.
