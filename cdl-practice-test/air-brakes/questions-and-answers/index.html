Air brake system parts (20 questions)

  1. What might it mean if the ABS malfunction lamp stays on after the bulb check?

    • AThe service brakes have failed completely
    • BThe air tanks need to be drained
    • CThe spring brakes have been applied
    • DYou may have lost ABS control at one or more wheels

    Answer: D. You may have lost ABS control at one or more wheels

    A lamp that does not go out after start-up, or that lights while driving, warns that ABS may not be working at one or more wheels.

    Manual section 5.1.16 Antilock Braking Systems (ABS).

  2. Truck tractors with air brakes built on or after which date are required to have antilock brakes?

    • AMarch 1, 1975
    • BMarch 1, 1994
    • CMarch 1, 1997
    • DMarch 1, 1998

    Answer: C. March 1, 1997

    The ABS requirement began for air-braked truck tractors built from March 1, 1997, one year earlier than for other air brake vehicles such as trucks, buses and trailers.

    Manual section 5.1.16 Antilock Braking Systems (ABS).

  3. Where is the yellow ABS malfunction lamp located on a trailer?

    • AOn the right side, next to the rear bumper
    • BOn the left side, at either the front or rear corner
    • COn the tractor's instrument panel only
    • DOn the roof, near the front of the trailer

    Answer: B. On the left side, at either the front or rear corner

    Tractors, trucks and buses show the ABS lamp on the dash, while trailers carry it on the left side at a front or rear corner.

    Manual section 5.1.16 Antilock Braking Systems (ABS).

  4. During cold weather, how often should you check the alcohol evaporator container and fill it as needed?

    • AOnce a week
    • BEvery day
    • COnce a month
    • DOnly when the low air warning comes on

    Answer: B. Every day

    In cold weather the alcohol supply should be checked and topped up daily so it keeps working against ice in the air system.

    Manual section 5.1.5 Alcohol Evaporator.

  5. Your application pressure gauge shows you must use more and more pressure to hold the same speed. What should you do?

    • ASpeed up so airflow cools the brakes
    • BSlow down and use a lower gear
    • CPump the brake pedal quickly
    • DPull out the parking brake knob

    Answer: B. Slow down and use a lower gear

    Needing more application pressure for the same speed is a sign of brake fade, so you should reduce speed and shift to a lower gear.

    Manual section 5.1.10 Application Pressure Gauge.

  6. If the air compressor has its own oil supply, what should you do before driving?

    • ADrain the compressor oil into the air tanks
    • BAdd alcohol to the compressor oil
    • CCheck the compressor's oil level
    • DRun the engine at a fast idle for ten minutes

    Answer: C. Check the compressor's oil level

    A compressor with a separate oil supply is not lubricated by engine oil, so its own oil level has to be checked before each trip.

    Manual section 5.1.1 Air Compressor.

  7. On a vehicle with dual parking control valves, why must you plan carefully when moving it with air from the separate tank?

    • AIt has enough air to release the spring brakes only a few times
    • BIt refills only while the engine is turned off
    • CUsing it turns off the service brakes
    • DIt must also supply air to the trailer brakes

    Answer: A. It has enough air to release the spring brakes only a few times

    The extra tank lets you release the spring brakes in an emergency, but its air runs out after a few uses, which could leave you stopped in a dangerous spot.

    Manual section 5.1.15 Parking Brake Controls.

  8. At about what air tank pressure does the governor stop the compressor from pumping air?

    • AAround 125 psi
    • BAround 55 psi
    • CAround 100 psi
    • DAround 150 psi

    Answer: A. Around 125 psi

    The cut-out level is about 125 psi; at that point the governor stops the compressor, and it lets pumping start again only when pressure falls to the cut-in level.

    Manual section 5.1.2 Air Compressor Governor.

  9. On an older vehicle with a front brake limiting valve, what happens when the cab control is set to slippery?

    • AAir pressure to the front brakes is doubled
    • BNormal air pressure to the front brakes is cut by half
    • CAir pressure to the rear brakes is cut by half
    • DThe front brakes are turned off completely

    Answer: B. Normal air pressure to the front brakes is cut by half

    The slippery setting halves the air going to the front brakes, which lowers the vehicle's stopping power, so the control should be kept at normal.

    Manual section 5.1.13 Front Brake Limiting Valve.

  10. How is a manually operated air tank drain valve opened?

    • ABy pressing the brake pedal several times
    • BBy turning it a quarter turn or by pulling a cable
    • CBy pulling out the yellow parking brake knob
    • DBy running the engine at a fast idle

    Answer: B. By turning it a quarter turn or by pulling a cable

    Manual drain valves at the bottom of each tank are worked by hand, either with a quarter turn of the valve or with a pull cable.

    Manual section 5.1.4 Air Tank Drains.

  11. On newer vehicles with air brakes, what does the parking brake control look like?

    • AA red, eight-sided push-pull knob
    • BA blue, round push-and-hold button
    • CA yellow, diamond-shaped push-pull knob
    • DA green, square toggle switch

    Answer: C. A yellow, diamond-shaped push-pull knob

    Newer air brake vehicles use a yellow diamond knob that is pulled out to set the spring brakes and pushed in to release them.

    Manual section 5.1.15 Parking Brake Controls.

  12. What can happen if you press and release the brake pedal when there is no need to?

    • AAir can be let out faster than the compressor can replace it
    • BThe governor raises the cut-out pressure
    • CThe spring brakes release by themselves
    • DThe brake linings cool down faster

    Answer: A. Air can be let out faster than the compressor can replace it

    Every release of the pedal lets some compressed air escape, so needless pumping can drain the tanks faster than the compressor refills them, and the brakes stop working if pressure gets too low.

    Manual section 5.1.7 The Brake Pedal.

  13. Why should you never push the brake pedal down while the spring brakes are on?

    • AIt would release the spring brakes and let the vehicle roll
    • BIt would empty all the air tanks at once
    • CIt would switch off the antilock brakes
    • DThe brakes could be damaged by the springs and air pressure acting together

    Answer: D. The brakes could be damaged by the springs and air pressure acting together

    With the springs already applying the brakes, adding air pressure from the pedal can overload and damage them, and not every system is built to prevent this.

    Manual section 5.1.15 Parking Brake Controls.

  14. At what pressure is the air system safety relief valve usually set to open?

    • A55 psi
    • B100 psi
    • C125 psi
    • D150 psi

    Answer: D. 150 psi

    The safety valve guards the first tank and the rest of the system against too much pressure, and it normally opens at 150 psi.

    Manual section 5.1.6 Safety Valve.

  15. What is the most common type of foundation brake?

    • AThe wedge brake
    • BThe air disc brake
    • CThe s-cam drum brake
    • DThe power screw brake

    Answer: C. The s-cam drum brake

    Foundation brakes sit at each wheel, and the s-cam drum brake is the type used most; wedge and disc brakes are less common.

    Manual section 5.1.8 Foundation Brakes.

  16. Tractor and straight truck spring brakes come fully on when air pressure drops into which range?

    • A20 to 45 psi
    • B55 to 60 psi
    • C80 to 85 psi
    • D100 to 125 psi

    Answer: A. 20 to 45 psi

    Spring brakes apply fully somewhere between 20 and 45 psi, most often between 20 and 30 psi, but you should stop safely as soon as the low air warning comes on rather than wait for this.

    Manual section 5.1.14 Spring Brakes.

  17. How does an air brake system turn on the brake lights?

    • AWith a mechanical switch on the parking brake knob
    • BWith a sensor on each slack adjuster
    • CWith an electric switch that works by air pressure
    • DWith a switch inside the compressor governor

    Answer: C. With an electric switch that works by air pressure

    Air pressure from a brake application operates an electric switch, which lights the brake lights to warn drivers behind you.

    Manual section 5.1.12 Stop Light Switch.

  18. How are the supply pressure gauges set up on a vehicle with a dual air brake system?

    • AA gauge for each half of the system, or one gauge with two needles
    • BOne gauge with a single needle for both halves
    • CA separate gauge at each wheel's brake chamber
    • DNo gauge at all, only a warning light

    Answer: A. A gauge for each half of the system, or one gauge with two needles

    With a dual system the driver needs to see the pressure in each half, so there are two gauges or a single gauge showing two needles.

    Manual section 5.1.9 Supply Pressure Gauges.

  19. If the air compressor stops working, how much braking do the air storage tanks still allow?

    • AEnough air for only one light brake application
    • BNo braking at all, because the tanks empty at once
    • CUnlimited braking until the engine is shut off
    • DEnough air to use the brakes several times

    Answer: D. Enough air to use the brakes several times

    The storage tanks keep a reserve of compressed air, so the brakes can still be applied a number of times after a compressor failure.

    Manual section 5.1.3 Air Storage Tanks.

  20. How is a manual reset wig wag different from an automatic wig wag?

    • AIt rises out of view by itself when pressure goes above 55 psi
    • BIt sounds a buzzer instead of dropping an arm
    • CIt drops into view only when pressure goes above 85 psi
    • DIt has to be put in the out-of-view position by hand

    Answer: D. It has to be put in the out-of-view position by hand

    An automatic wig wag lifts out of view on its own once pressure is above 55 psi, but the manual reset type must be moved back by the driver, and it will not stay up until pressure is above 55 psi.

    Manual section 5.1.11 Low Air Pressure Warning.

Dual air brake systems (3 questions)

  1. Before driving a vehicle with a dual air system, the compressor should build up at least how much pressure in both the primary and secondary systems?

    • A55 psi
    • B85 psi
    • C100 psi
    • D150 psi

    Answer: C. 100 psi

    You should wait until both halves of a dual system have at least 100 psi before you start driving.

    Manual section 5.2 Dual Air Brake.

  2. In a dual air brake system, what happens if one air system is very low on pressure?

    • AThe other system automatically doubles its pressure
    • BEither the front or the rear brakes will not be operating fully
    • CThe spring brakes release so the vehicle can roll freely
    • DOnly the trailer brakes stop working

    Answer: B. Either the front or the rear brakes will not be operating fully

    Each half of a dual system serves different axles, so losing pressure in one half weakens the front or rear brakes and makes stopping take longer.

    Manual section 5.2 Dual Air Brake.

  3. The low air warning light and buzzer should shut off when pressure in both systems rises to a value set by the manufacturer. That value must be:

    • AGreater than 55 psi
    • BLess than 20 psi
    • CBetween 20 and 45 psi
    • DExactly 45 psi

    Answer: A. Greater than 55 psi

    The manufacturer can choose the shut-off point for the warning, but it has to be above 55 psi.

    Manual section 5.2 Dual Air Brake.

Inspecting air brakes (9 questions)

  1. If brakes with automatic slack adjusters are found to be out of adjustment, what does the manual recommend?

    • AAdjust them by hand and keep driving as usual
    • BIgnore it, because they will adjust themselves
    • CDrain the air tanks and test them again
    • DTake the vehicle to a repair facility as soon as possible

    Answer: D. Take the vehicle to a repair facility as soon as possible

    An automatic adjuster that is out of limits usually points to a mechanical problem, and adjusting it by hand tends to hide that problem rather than fix it, so the vehicle should be repaired soon.

    Manual section 5.3.2 During Step 5 Walk-around Inspection.

  2. In a dual air system with the engine at operating rpm, air pressure should build from 85 to 100 psi within:

    • A45 seconds
    • B10 seconds
    • C90 seconds
    • D3 minutes

    Answer: A. 45 seconds

    A dual air system should go from 85 to 100 psi in no more than 45 seconds at operating rpm, unless larger-than-minimum tanks allow a longer time under the manufacturer's specifications.

    Manual section 5.3.3 Step 7 Final Air Brake Check.

  3. During the engine compartment checks, what should you check on a belt-driven air compressor?

    • AThe cut-out pressure shown on the dash gauges
    • BThe thickness of the brake linings
    • CThe color of the compressor's air filter
    • DThe condition and tightness of the compressor drive belt

    Answer: D. The condition and tightness of the compressor drive belt

    When the compressor is driven by a belt, the engine compartment check includes making sure that belt is in good shape and properly tight.

    Manual section 5.3.1 During Step 2 Engine Compartment Checks.

  4. During the walk-around inspection, brake drums or discs must not have cracks longer than:

    • AOne quarter of an inch
    • BOne inch
    • COne half the width of the friction area
    • DThe full width of the friction area

    Answer: C. One half the width of the friction area

    A crack in a drum or disc is acceptable only if it is shorter than half the width of the friction area.

    Manual section 5.3.2 During Step 5 Walk-around Inspection.

  5. During the final air brake check, how do you lower tank pressure to see whether the compressor cuts in?

    • ARun the engine at a fast idle
    • BWith the engine idling, step on and off the brake
    • CRev the engine several times in neutral
    • DWait with the engine idling and the brakes off

    Answer: B. With the engine idling, step on and off the brake

    Pressing and releasing the brake pedal uses up air, so the gauge falls until the governor should let the compressor start pumping again.

    Manual section 5.3.3 Step 7 Final Air Brake Check.

  6. How do you test that the parking brake will hold?

    • AStop, put the parking brake on, and pull hard against it in a high gear
    • BStop, put the parking brake on, and gently pull against it in a low gear
    • CRelease the parking brake and let the vehicle roll down a slope
    • DPut the parking brake on and press the brake pedal hard

    Answer: B. Stop, put the parking brake on, and gently pull against it in a low gear

    The test is a gentle pull in a low gear with the parking brake set, which shows whether the parking brake can keep the vehicle from moving.

    Manual section 5.3.3 Step 7 Final Air Brake Check.

  7. What is the most common problem found in roadside inspections?

    • AOut-of-adjustment brakes
    • BLoose air compressor belts
    • CBurned-out ABS malfunction lamps
    • DLeaking alcohol evaporators

    Answer: A. Out-of-adjustment brakes

    Brakes that are out of adjustment show up more than any other problem at roadside inspections, which is why checking the slack adjusters matters.

    Manual section 5.3.2 During Step 5 Walk-around Inspection.

  8. When you test the service brakes, about how fast should the vehicle be moving when you apply the brakes firmly?

    • AAbout 15 mph
    • BAbout 25 mph
    • CAbout 35 mph
    • DAbout five mph

    Answer: D. About five mph

    The service brake test is done at a slow roll of about five mph, while you watch for pulling to one side, an odd feel or a slow stop.

    Manual section 5.3.3 Step 7 Final Air Brake Check.

  9. If the low pressure warning signal does not work and you lose air pressure in a single-circuit air system, what could happen?

    • AThe compressor would pump faster to make up the loss
    • BThe service brakes would work normally until you stop
    • CSudden emergency braking
    • DThe brake lights would stay on until you stop

    Answer: C. Sudden emergency braking

    Without a working warning you would not notice falling air pressure, and in a single-circuit system that can lead to the brakes coming on suddenly.

    Manual section 5.3.3 Step 7 Final Air Brake Check.

Using air brakes (13 questions)

  1. If you always drive a straight truck or combination with working ABS on all axles, what may you do in an emergency stop?

    • AUse only the parking brake
    • BFully apply the brakes
    • CPump the brake pedal as fast as you can
    • DShift into neutral before braking

    Answer: B. Fully apply the brakes

    The one exception to braking as you always have is a vehicle with working ABS on every axle, where full brake application is allowed in an emergency stop.

    Manual section 5.4.2 Braking with Antilock Brakes.

  2. At 55 mph on dry pavement, about how much distance does air brake lag add?

    • AAbout 6 feet
    • BAbout 142 feet
    • CAbout 450 feet
    • DAbout 32 feet

    Answer: D. About 32 feet

    Because air takes time to reach the brakes, brake lag adds roughly 32 feet at 55 mph, on top of perception, reaction and braking distance.

    Manual section 5.4.4 Stopping Distance.

  3. With the controlled braking method, how hard do you apply the brakes?

    • AAs hard as you can without locking the wheels
    • BAll the way, releasing them only when the wheels lock
    • CJust hard enough to feel a definite slowdown
    • DLightly at first, then fully after three seconds

    Answer: A. As hard as you can without locking the wheels

    Controlled braking means braking at the edge of wheel lockup while keeping steering movements very small.

    Manual section 5.4.3 Emergency Stops.

  4. What happens as overheated brake drums expand?

    • AThe drums grip the linings harder and brake better
    • BThe shoes must move farther and press with less force
    • CThe air pressure in the storage tanks rises
    • DThe antilock brakes switch off to let them cool

    Answer: B. The shoes must move farther and press with less force

    Heat makes the drums grow larger, so the shoes travel farther and press with less force, which is one way brakes fade.

    Manual section 5.4.5 Brake Fading or Failure.

  5. Why should you not use the parking brakes when the brakes are very hot?

    • AThey will freeze so the vehicle cannot move
    • BThe heat can damage them
    • CThey will release by themselves after a few minutes
    • DThey will make the air tanks overfill

    Answer: B. The heat can damage them

    Setting the parking brakes on very hot brakes, such as right after a steep grade, can cause heat damage, so chock the wheels on level ground and let the brakes cool first.

    Manual section 5.4.8 Parking Brakes.

  6. Why does a heavily loaded vehicle take a long distance to stop when its spring brakes come on?

    • AThe spring brakes need at least 100 psi to work
    • BThe ABS turns the spring brakes off
    • CThe air tanks refill and release the spring brakes
    • DThe spring brakes do not work on all axles

    Answer: D. The spring brakes do not work on all axles

    Spring brakes act on only some of the axles, so a heavy load needs a long distance to stop, and it is safer to stop while there is still air for the foot brake.

    Manual section 5.4.7 Low Air Pressure.

  7. During a normal stop in a vehicle with a manual transmission, when should you push the clutch in?

    • AWhen the engine rpm is down close to idle
    • BAs soon as you begin pressing the brake pedal
    • CBefore you begin pressing the brake pedal
    • DWhen the speed drops to about 30 mph

    Answer: A. When the engine rpm is down close to idle

    Keep the clutch engaged while braking until engine speed has fallen almost to idle, then select a starting gear once stopped.

    Manual section 5.4.1 Normal Stops.

  8. In the proper braking technique for a downgrade, about how long should each brake application last?

    • AAbout three seconds
    • BAbout one second
    • CAbout ten seconds
    • DAbout thirty seconds

    Answer: A. About three seconds

    Each firm application to bring speed down by about five mph should take roughly three seconds.

    Manual section 5.4.6 Proper Braking Technique.

  9. With the proper braking technique on a downgrade, when do you release the brakes?

    • AWhen the vehicle has come to a complete stop
    • BWhen your speed is about 15 mph below your safe speed
    • CWhen your speed is about five mph below your safe speed
    • DWhen the low air pressure warning comes on

    Answer: C. When your speed is about five mph below your safe speed

    You brake until speed falls roughly five mph under your safe speed, let off, and brake again once speed climbs back up to the safe speed.

    Manual section 5.4.6 Proper Braking Technique.

  10. In stab braking, after you release the brakes, how long can it take for the wheels to start rolling?

    • AUp to five seconds
    • BUp to ten seconds
    • CUp to one second
    • DUp to thirty seconds

    Answer: C. Up to one second

    The wheels may need as long as a second to roll again, and reapplying the brakes before they do will keep the vehicle from straightening out.

    Manual section 5.4.3 Emergency Stops.

  11. In a vehicle without ABS, what happens when your steering wheels lock up during hard braking on a slippery surface?

    • AThe vehicle stops in a shorter distance
    • BThe ABS malfunction lamp comes on
    • CYou lose steering control
    • DThe spring brakes come on automatically

    Answer: C. You lose steering control

    Locked front wheels cannot steer the vehicle, so locking them up takes away your ability to steer.

    Manual section 5.4.2 Braking with Antilock Brakes.

  12. When only the tractor has ABS, what should you do if the trailer begins to swing out?

    • AApply the brakes fully and hold them
    • BPull out the parking brake knob
    • CSpeed up to pull the trailer straight
    • DLet up on the brakes, if you can safely do so

    Answer: D. Let up on the brakes, if you can safely do so

    Tractor-only ABS helps you keep steering and lowers the chance of a jackknife, but you still need to watch the trailer and ease off the brakes when it starts to swing.

    Manual section 5.4.2 Braking with Antilock Brakes.

  13. If your brakes are wet, how should you dry them?

    • ASet the parking brakes at every stop
    • BDrive in a high gear without using the brakes
    • CUse the brakes lightly while driving in a low gear
    • DDrain the air tanks more often

    Answer: C. Use the brakes lightly while driving in a low gear

    Light braking in a low gear heats the brakes and dries them out.

    Manual section 5.4.8 Parking Brakes.
