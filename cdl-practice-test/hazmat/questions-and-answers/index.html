Intent of the regulations (4 questions)

  1. What can be the result of not complying with the hazardous materials regulations?

    • AFines and jail time
    • BA verbal warning only
    • CA retraining class only
    • DA fuel tax penalty only

    Answer: A. Fines and jail time

    Breaking the hazardous materials rules is not only unsafe; it can lead to fines and even time in jail.

    Manual section 9.1.3 Assure Safe Drivers and Equipment.

  2. If a law enforcement officer stops your hazardous materials vehicle, what may the officer check?

    • AOnly your fuel receipts and toll road records
    • BYour papers, placards, and HM endorsement
    • COnly your vehicle registration and insurance card
    • DOnly the sales invoices for all of your cargo

    Answer: B. Your papers, placards, and HM endorsement

    Officers who stop a hazmat vehicle may look at the shipping papers, the placards and the endorsement on your license, and may also test what you know.

    Manual section 9.1.3 Assure Safe Drivers and Equipment.

  3. Whom are the hazardous materials regulations intended to protect?

    • AOnly the shipper and the carrier company
    • BYou, others nearby, and the environment
    • COnly police and firefighters at a crash
    • DOnly the owner of the cargo being moved

    Answer: B. You, others nearby, and the environment

    The rules exist to keep the driver, nearby people and the environment safe, not just one business or group.

    Manual section 9.1.1 Contain the Material.

  4. To communicate the risk of a hazardous material, who must put hazard warning labels on its packages?

    • AThe driver
    • BThe consignee
    • CThe shipper
    • DThe inspecting officer

    Answer: C. The shipper

    Labeling packages is the shipper's job, along with providing shipping papers, emergency response information and placards.

    Manual section 9.1.2 Communicate the Risk.

Shipper, carrier and driver duties (5 questions)

  1. Before transporting a hazardous materials shipment, what does the carrier check?

    • AThat the consignee has already paid for it
    • BThat the cargo is insured for its full value
    • CThat the shipper prepared it correctly
    • DThat the driver has repacked every package

    Answer: C. That the shipper prepared it correctly

    Before the trip, the carrier confirms that the shipper prepared the shipment properly, and it refuses shipments that are not.

    Manual section 9.2.2 The Carrier.

  2. According to the driver's duties, when does the driver placard the vehicle, if placards are required?

    • AAfter the first rest stop
    • BWhen crossing a state line
    • CAfter an officer asks
    • DAt the time of loading

    Answer: D. At the time of loading

    Any required placards are put on the vehicle by the driver at the time it is loaded.

    Manual section 9.2.3 The Driver.

  3. When does the driver report a hazardous materials accident or incident to the proper government agency?

    • AOnly when the shipper asks the driver to make the report
    • BWhen it occurs while the driver has physical control
    • COnly when the vehicle has to be towed from the scene
    • DOnly when it happens on an interstate highway

    Answer: B. When it occurs while the driver has physical control

    The driver's reporting duty covers accidents and incidents that happen while the driver has physical control of the hazardous materials.

    Manual section 9.2.3 The Driver.

  4. Which of these is an exception to the shipper certifying on the shipping paper that the shipment was prepared according to the rules?

    • AHauling shipments that weigh under 1,000 pounds
    • BMaking trips that stay entirely inside one state
    • CPulling cargo tanks you or your employer supplied
    • DCarrying shipments only during nighttime hours

    Answer: C. Pulling cargo tanks you or your employer supplied

    The shipper does not certify the shipment when the cargo tank you are pulling was supplied by you or by your employer.

    Manual section 9.2.1 The Shipper.

  5. Who uses the hazardous materials regulations to determine a product's identification number, proper shipping name, and hazard class?

    • AThe shipper
    • BThe driver
    • CThe consignee
    • DThe fire department

    Answer: A. The shipper

    Working out the identification number, shipping name, hazard class and related details from the regulations is a shipper duty.

    Manual section 9.2.1 The Shipper.

Communication rules (22 questions)

  1. When may you accept the shipper's certification that a package is properly packaged?

    • AUnless a package is clearly unsafe or not HMR compliant
    • BOnly after you open and inspect each package yourself
    • COnly when the shipper is also your own employer
    • DNever, because the driver must re-certify every package

    Answer: A. Unless a package is clearly unsafe or not HMR compliant

    You can rely on the shipper's signed certification about packaging unless a package is obviously unsafe or out of line with the regulations.

    Manual section 9.3.6 Shipper's Certification.

  2. In what order are the parts of a hazardous material's basic description listed?

    • AShipping name, hazard class, ID number, packing group
    • BHazard class, ID number, packing group, shipping name
    • CID number, shipping name, hazard class, packing group
    • DPacking group, shipping name, ID number, hazard class

    Answer: C. ID number, shipping name, hazard class, packing group

    The basic description goes from identification number to proper shipping name to hazard class or division, then the packing group when there is one.

    Manual section 9.3.5 The Item Description.

  3. A bulk package holds only the residue of a hazardous material. What is the placard rule?

    • AIt must still be placarded
    • BIts placards must be removed
    • CIt needs placards only over 1,001 pounds
    • DIt is marked EMPTY instead of placarded

    Answer: A. It must still be placarded

    Bulk packages, and the vehicles carrying them, keep their placards even when only residue is left inside.

    Manual section 9.3.11 Placard Tables.

  4. Which types of packages does the manual say are often used for hazardous materials shipments?

    • AEnvelopes and mailing tubes
    • BPaper sacks and gift boxes
    • CMesh bags and wooden crates
    • DCylinders and drums

    Answer: D. Cylinders and drums

    The kind of package is a clue: cylinders and drums frequently hold hazardous materials.

    Manual section 9.3.8 Recognizing Hazardous Materials.

  5. You are using DANGEROUS placards for a mixed Table 2 load. You must use the specific placard for a Table 2 material if you loaded how much of it at one place?

    • A1,001 pounds or more
    • B2,205 pounds or more
    • C3,000 pounds or more
    • D5,000 pounds or more

    Answer: B. 2,205 pounds or more

    The DANGEROUS placard cannot stand in for the specific placard of any Table 2 material when 2,205 pounds or more of it was loaded at a single place.

    Manual section 9.3.11 Placard Tables.

  6. Besides describing the hazardous materials correctly, what must shippers include on shipping papers?

    • AAn emergency response telephone number
    • BThe driver's home telephone number
    • CThe carrier's insurance policy number
    • DA list of the planned fuel stops

    Answer: A. An emergency response telephone number

    Shippers must put an emergency response phone number on the shipping papers along with a correct description of the materials.

    Manual section 9.3.1 Definitions.

  7. Column 3 of the Hazardous Materials Table shows the entry Forbidden for a material. What does this mean for you?

    • ATransport it only with a police escort
    • BTransport it only in a closed van
    • CTransport it only with Table 1 placards
    • DNever transport that material

    Answer: D. Never transport that material

    A material listed as Forbidden may not be transported at all.

    Manual section 9.3.3 Lists of Regulated Products.

  8. When are you allowed to move an improperly placarded vehicle?

    • AOnly for short trips of less than 25 miles
    • BOnly when the load weighs under 1,001 pounds
    • COnly while on the shipper's or carrier's own property
    • DOnly in an emergency, to protect life or property

    Answer: D. Only in an emergency, to protect life or property

    Placards go on before you drive; a vehicle without proper placards may be moved only in an emergency to protect life or property.

    Manual section 9.3.10 Placarding.

  9. The words INHALATION HAZARD appear on a shipping paper. When must you display both the hazard class placard and the POISON INHALATION HAZARD placard?

    • AAlways, even for small amounts
    • BOnly for 1,001 pounds or more
    • COnly when it is in bulk packaging
    • DOnly on trips that cross state lines

    Answer: A. Always, even for small amounts

    For materials marked as an inhalation hazard, both placards are required no matter how little of the material is carried.

    Manual section 9.3.3 Lists of Regulated Products.

  10. If a diamond-shaped hazard label will not fit on a package, what may the shipper do?

    • AShip the package without any hazard label at all
    • BPrint the label only on the shipping paper
    • CPut the label on the trailer door instead
    • DPut the label on a tag securely attached to it

    Answer: D. Put the label on a tag securely attached to it

    When a label cannot fit, it can go on a tag firmly attached to the package, as is done with some compressed gas cylinders.

    Manual section 9.3.2 Package Labels.

  11. To whom may you hand over a hazardous waste shipment?

    • AA registered carrier or a disposal/treatment facility
    • BAny carrier that happens to have an empty trailer
    • CAny business that is willing to sign the manifest
    • DAny person the shipper names to you by telephone

    Answer: A. A registered carrier or a disposal/treatment facility

    Hazardous waste may be passed only to another registered carrier or to a disposal or treatment facility.

    Manual section 9.3.9 Hazardous Waste Manifest.

  12. What does the Marine Pollutant marking on bulk packages look like?

    • AAn orange diamond with a wave and the letters MP
    • BA white triangle with an X through a fish
    • CA blue square with a fish and a drop of oil
    • DA black circle with a boat and an X through it

    Answer: B. A white triangle with an X through a fish

    The Marine Pollutant mark is a white triangle showing a fish crossed out with an X; it is a marking, not a placard.

    Manual section 9.3.3 Lists of Regulated Products.

  13. If a package needs more than one hazard label, where must the labels be placed?

    • AOn opposite sides of the package
    • BOne on the package and one on the shipping paper
    • COn the top and bottom of the package
    • DClose together, near the proper shipping name

    Answer: D. Close together, near the proper shipping name

    Several labels on one package are grouped close together next to the proper shipping name.

    Manual section 9.3.7 Package Markings and Labels.

  14. What do the letters NA in front of an identification number indicate?

    • AA shipping name used only for transportation by air
    • BA material that is not regulated on the highway
    • CA name used only in the U.S. and to and from Canada
    • DA material that needs no placards in any amount

    Answer: C. A name used only in the U.S. and to and from Canada

    NA marks a proper shipping name meant for use inside the United States and for shipments to and from Canada; ID is the prefix tied to air transport.

    Manual section 9.3.3 Lists of Regulated Products.

  15. How many different hazard classes are there?

    • AFive
    • BSix
    • CSeven
    • DNine

    Answer: D. Nine

    Hazardous materials are sorted into nine hazard classes, and each class reflects the kind of risk the material poses.

    Manual section 9.3.1 Definitions.

  16. A hazardous materials shipping paper has more than one page. What must the first page show?

    • AThe driver's signature and license number
    • BThe total number of pages
    • CThe planned route for the trip
    • DThe weight of every package listed

    Answer: B. The total number of pages

    Multi-page shipping papers carry page numbers, and page one states how many pages there are in all, such as Page 1 of 4.

    Manual section 9.3.4 The Shipping Paper.

  17. How far must each placard be from any other markings on the vehicle?

    • AAt least one inch
    • BAt least three inches
    • CAt least six inches
    • DAt least ten inches

    Answer: B. At least three inches

    Each placard needs a gap of three inches or more from other markings so it can be seen clearly.

    Manual section 9.3.10 Placarding.

  18. How long must shippers keep a copy of shipping papers for hazardous materials that are not hazardous waste?

    • A1 year
    • B2 years
    • C3 years
    • D5 years

    Answer: B. 2 years

    Shippers keep shipping papers, or an electronic image of them, for two years after the first carrier accepts the material; hazardous waste papers are kept for three years.

    Manual section 9.3.5 The Item Description.

  19. How much of a Placard Table 1 material must be on the vehicle before placards are required?

    • AAny amount at all
    • B1,001 pounds or more
    • C2,205 pounds or more
    • DA full bulk package

    Answer: A. Any amount at all

    Placard Table 1 materials need placards no matter how small the quantity.

    Manual section 9.3.11 Placard Tables.

  20. Except for bulk packaging, Placard Table 2 hazard classes need placards only when the total amount transported is:

    • A101 pounds or more
    • B501 pounds or more
    • C1,001 pounds or more
    • D2,205 pounds or more

    Answer: C. 1,001 pounds or more

    For non-bulk Table 2 materials, placards are needed once the combined total from all shipping papers, packaging included, reaches 1,001 pounds.

    Manual section 9.3.11 Placard Tables.

  21. Where does the shipper of a hazardous waste put the word WASTE on the shipping paper?

    • AAfter the hazard class
    • BBefore the proper shipping name
    • CIn the HM column in place of an X
    • DAt the bottom of the last page

    Answer: B. Before the proper shipping name

    For hazardous waste, the word WASTE goes in front of the proper shipping name, as in Waste Acetone.

    Manual section 9.3.5 The Item Description.

  22. Why must hazardous materials shipping papers be easy to locate after an accident?

    • ATow operators need them to set their towing fees
    • BYou may be hurt and unable to describe the hazards
    • CThe shipper has to sign them again at the scene
    • DInsurance adjusters need them before help arrives

    Answer: B. You may be hurt and unable to describe the hazards

    If the driver is hurt, the papers tell firefighters and police what hazardous materials are on board so they can limit damage and injury.

    Manual section 9.3.1 Definitions.

Loading and unloading (14 questions)

  1. What should you do before loading or unloading hazardous materials?

    • AStart the engine and let it idle in neutral
    • BSet the parking brake so the vehicle won't move
    • CRemove all of the placards from the vehicle
    • DOpen each package to check what is inside

    Answer: B. Set the parking brake so the vehicle won't move

    The vehicle must be secured with the parking brake so it cannot move before any loading or unloading starts.

    Manual section 9.4.1 General Loading Requirements.

  2. When must you close all manholes and valves on a tank of hazardous materials before moving it?

    • AOnly when the tank is more than half full
    • BOnly when the trip is longer than one mile
    • CWhenever it holds any amount, however small
    • DOnly when the tank holds a flammable liquid

    Answer: C. Whenever it holds any amount, however small

    Manholes and valves are closed before any move to prevent leaks, even for a tiny amount of product or a short distance.

    Manual section 9.5.2 Tank Loading.

  3. When may the liquid discharge valves on a compressed gas tank be open?

    • AWhenever the tank is less than half full
    • BWhile driving on the highway
    • COnly when loading and unloading
    • DWhile parked overnight to vent pressure

    Answer: C. Only when loading and unloading

    The liquid discharge valves stay closed at all times other than during loading and unloading.

    Manual section 9.5.4 Compressed Gas.

  4. Which hazardous materials package may you empty while it is on the vehicle?

    • AA steel drum
    • BA gas cylinder
    • CA cargo tank
    • DA fiberboard box

    Answer: C. A cargo tank

    A cargo tank may be emptied on the vehicle, but no other package may be emptied while it is on board.

    Manual section 9.4.1 General Loading Requirements.

  5. What must you do before loading or unloading any explosives?

    • ATurn your engine off
    • BTurn the cargo heater on
    • CUncouple the trailer
    • DRemove the placards

    Answer: A. Turn your engine off

    The engine has to be shut off before any explosives are loaded or unloaded; after that, the cargo space is checked.

    Manual section 9.4.1 General Loading Requirements.

  6. If safety requires an emergency transfer of Division 1.1, 1.2, or 1.3 explosives on a public roadway, what must you do?

    • ALight road flares all around both of the vehicles
    • BRemove the placards from both of the vehicles
    • CKeep both engines running during the whole transfer
    • DSet out red reflectors, flags, or electric lanterns

    Answer: D. Set out red reflectors, flags, or electric lanterns

    An emergency roadside transfer of these explosives calls for red reflectors, flags or electric lanterns to warn other road users.

    Manual section 9.4.1 General Loading Requirements.

  7. When filling a cargo tank of flammable liquid through an open filling hole, when should the tank be grounded?

    • ABefore opening the filling hole, until after it is closed
    • BAfter the filling hole is opened, until the tank is half full
    • COnly after the filling hole is closed again
    • DOnly when the weather is hot and dry

    Answer: A. Before opening the filling hole, until after it is closed

    The ground connection goes on before the filling hole is opened and stays on until the hole has been closed again.

    Manual section 9.5.3 Flammable Liquids.

  8. On cargo tanks and other bulk packaging, how tall must the black identification numbers be?

    • A25 mm (1 inch)
    • B50 mm (2 inches)
    • C75 mm (3 inches)
    • D100 mm (3.9 inches)

    Answer: D. 100 mm (3.9 inches)

    Identification numbers on bulk packaging are shown in black figures 100 mm, about 3.9 inches, high.

    Manual section 9.5.1 Markings.

  9. Which is the most common type of cargo tank used for liquids?

    • AMC306
    • BMC331
    • CMC338
    • DDOT 110A

    Answer: A. MC306

    The MC306 is the usual cargo tank for liquids, while the MC331 is the usual one for gases.

    Manual section 9.5 Bulk Packaging Marking, Loading and Unloading.

  10. When loading hazardous materials, what does the manual say about using hooks?

    • ADo not use hooks at all
    • BUse them only on drums
    • CUse them only on cartons
    • DUse them only with a helper

    Answer: A. Do not use hooks at all

    Hooks, like any other tool that could damage containers or packaging, are not to be used when loading hazardous materials.

    Manual section 9.4 Loading and Unloading.

  11. You cannot have an overhang or tailgate load of which of these?

    • AClass 8 (Corrosives)
    • BDivision 2.2 (Non-Flammable Gases)
    • CClass 4 (Flammable Solids)
    • DClass 9 (Miscellaneous)

    Answer: C. Class 4 (Flammable Solids)

    Flammable solids, like explosives and oxidizers, must ride in a closed cargo space rather than hang over the tailgate, unless the fire and water resistant exception applies.

    Manual section 9.4.1 General Loading Requirements.

  12. Where must you never load a package labeled POISON or POISON INHALATION HAZARD?

    • AIn a closed van-type trailer
    • BBehind a secured cargo bulkhead
    • CIn the driver's cab or sleeper
    • DOn a flat, even cargo floor

    Answer: C. In the driver's cab or sleeper

    Poison-labeled packages must stay out of the cab and sleeper and away from food meant for people or animals.

    Manual section 9.4.1 General Loading Requirements.

  13. The qualified person watching a cargo tank being loaded or unloaded must be within how many feet of the tank?

    • A10 feet
    • B15 feet
    • C25 feet
    • D50 feet

    Answer: C. 25 feet

    The person attending cargo tank loading or unloading must stay within 25 feet and have a clear view of the tank.

    Manual section 9.5.2 Tank Loading.

  14. The total transport index of all radioactive packages in a single vehicle must not exceed:

    • A10
    • B25
    • C50
    • D100

    Answer: C. 50

    Radiation from Class 7 packages adds up, so the combined transport index in one vehicle is capped at 50.

    Manual section 9.4.1 General Loading Requirements.

Driving and parking rules (12 questions)

  1. A person attending a parked placarded vehicle from outside it must stay within how many feet and keep it in clear view?

    • A50 feet
    • B100 feet
    • C200 feet
    • D300 feet

    Answer: B. 100 feet

    An attendant outside the vehicle must stay within 100 feet with a clear view of it; one inside must be awake and out of the sleeper berth.

    Manual section 9.6.3 Attending Parked Vehicles.

  2. Whenever your vehicle is placarded, which of these should you avoid if another route exists?

    • ACrowds, tunnels, narrow streets, and alleys
    • BInterstate highways and rural two-lane roads
    • CWeigh stations and roadside inspection sites
    • DToll roads, bypasses, and designated truck routes

    Answer: A. Crowds, tunnels, narrow streets, and alleys

    Placarded vehicles should route around crowded and built-up places, tunnels, narrow streets and alleys unless there is no other way.

    Manual section 9.6.5 Route Restrictions.

  3. A driver transporting chlorine in cargo tanks must have which item in the vehicle?

    • AA radiation survey meter
    • BAn approved gas mask
    • CA second fire extinguisher
    • DA set of road flares

    Answer: B. An approved gas mask

    Chlorine cargo tank drivers must carry an approved gas mask, along with a kit for controlling leaks in dome cover plate fittings.

    Manual section 9.6.11 Equipment for Chlorine.

  4. With Division 1.1, 1.2, or 1.3 explosives, you may let someone else watch your parked vehicle only if it is:

    • AOn the shipper's, carrier's, or consignee's property
    • BIn a well-lit and busy truck stop parking lot
    • COn the road shoulder with the hazard flashers on
    • DWithin clear sight of a police or fire station

    Answer: A. On the shipper's, carrier's, or consignee's property

    Someone other than the driver may watch an explosives-laden vehicle only while it sits on the shipper's, carrier's or consignee's property.

    Manual section 9.6.1 Parking with Division 1.1, 1.2, or 1.3 Explosives.

  5. While a vehicle containing hazardous materials is being fueled, where must someone always be?

    • AIn the cab, keeping the engine running
    • BAt the rear of the trailer, watching traffic
    • CInside the station, paying for the fuel
    • DAt the nozzle, controlling the fuel flow

    Answer: D. At the nozzle, controlling the fuel flow

    With the engine shut off, a person has to stay at the fuel nozzle the whole time and control the flow.

    Manual section 9.6.7 Refuel with Engine Off.

  6. Your vehicle with an empty tank used for Class 3 flammable liquids breaks down. Which stopped vehicle signals should you use?

    • AReflective triangles or red electric lights
    • BFlares, because the tank is now empty
    • CFuses placed on the road behind the vehicle
    • DAny burning signal set at least 100 feet back

    Answer: A. Reflective triangles or red electric lights

    Burning signals are not allowed around flammable liquid tanks even when they are empty, so reflective triangles or red electric lights are used.

    Manual section 9.6.4 No Flares!.

  7. You must not smoke within how many feet of a placarded cargo tank used for Class 3 flammable liquids?

    • A5 feet
    • B10 feet
    • C15 feet
    • D25 feet

    Answer: D. 25 feet

    Smoking is banned within 25 feet of a placarded cargo tank used for flammable liquids or Division 2.1 gases.

    Manual section 9.6.6 No Smoking.

  8. How should you make hazardous materials shipping papers stand out from your other papers?

    • AKeep them at the bottom of the stack
    • BSeal them in an envelope in the glove box
    • CStaple them to the back of the logbook
    • DTab them or keep them on top of the stack

    Answer: D. Tab them or keep them on top of the stack

    Hazardous materials papers are tabbed or placed on top of the pile so others can find them quickly after a crash.

    Manual section 9.6.10 Where to Keep Shipping Papers and Emergency Response Information.

  9. When a placarded vehicle stops before a railroad crossing, how far from the nearest rail must it stop?

    • A15 to 50 feet
    • B50 to 100 feet
    • C100 to 150 feet
    • D200 to 300 feet

    Answer: A. 15 to 50 feet

    The required stop is made between 15 and 50 feet before the closest rail.

    Manual section 9.6.12 Stop before Railroad Crossings.

  10. What is the only acceptable way to check tire pressure?

    • AKick the tire and listen
    • BStrike the tire with a mallet
    • CUse a tire pressure gauge
    • DLook at how much the tire bulges

    Answer: C. Use a tire pressure gauge

    A tire pressure gauge is the only accepted method for checking tire pressure.

    Manual section 9.6.9 Check Tires.

  11. What does the manual say about uncoupling a trailer loaded with hazardous materials on a public street?

    • AYou may leave it there for up to one hour
    • BDo not uncouple it and leave it there
    • CYou may leave it there if it is placarded
    • DYou may leave it there if its brakes are set

    Answer: B. Do not uncouple it and leave it there

    A trailer carrying hazardous materials must not be dropped and left behind on a public street.

    Manual section 9.6.2 Parking a Placarded Vehicle Not Transporting Division 1.1, 1.2, or 1.3) Explosives.

  12. What must you have and follow when transporting Division 1.1, 1.2, or 1.3 explosives?

    • AA police escort vehicle
    • BA second licensed driver
    • CA daylight-only travel permit
    • DA written route plan

    Answer: D. A written route plan

    These explosives require a written route plan, usually prepared in advance by the carrier, which the driver keeps and follows.

    Manual section 9.6.5 Route Restrictions.

Emergencies (12 questions)

  1. Who pays for cleaning up parking lots, roadways, and drainage ditches contaminated by hazardous materials from a vehicle?

    • AThe carrier
    • BThe state
    • CThe shipper
    • DThe fire department

    Answer: A. The carrier

    Cleanup costs fall on the carrier and can be huge, which is one reason not to leave a long trail of contamination.

    Manual section 9.7.3 Fires.

  2. What was CHEMTREC created to provide?

    • ATowing and repair services for disabled trucks
    • BTechnical information about hazardous materials
    • CHazardous materials endorsement tests for drivers
    • DRoute permits for drivers hauling explosives

    Answer: B. Technical information about hazardous materials

    CHEMTREC runs a 24-hour toll-free line that gives emergency personnel technical facts about hazardous materials.

    Manual section 9.7.5 Required Notification.

  3. At a crash involving hazardous materials, where should you keep people?

    • AClose by, to help with the cleanup
    • BFar away and upwind
    • CDownwind, out of the traffic lanes
    • DInside their vehicles with windows open

    Answer: B. Far away and upwind

    Bystanders should be kept at a distance and on the upwind side of the scene.

    Manual section 9.7.2 Crashes/Incidents.

  4. How is the Emergency Response Guidebook (ERG) indexed?

    • ABy shipper name and city of origin
    • BBy carrier name and truck number
    • CBy shipping date and destination
    • DBy shipping name and ID number

    Answer: D. By shipping name and ID number

    Responders look up materials in the guidebook by shipping name and ID number, which is why those entries must be correct on the shipping paper.

    Manual section 9.7.1 Emergency Response Guidebook (ERG).

  5. Before separating vehicles in a collision involving explosives, how far from the vehicles and occupied buildings should you place the explosives?

    • AAt least 100 feet
    • BAt least 200 feet
    • CAt least 300 feet
    • DAt least 500 feet

    Answer: B. At least 200 feet

    The explosives come out before the vehicles are pulled apart and are set down 200 feet or more from the vehicles and occupied buildings.

    Manual section 9.7.4 Responses to Specific Hazards.

  6. When should you fight a hazardous materials fire?

    • AWhenever you have a fire extinguisher on board
    • BAlways, even before you call anyone for help
    • COnly if the fire started inside the cargo
    • DOnly if trained and equipped to do it safely

    Answer: D. Only if trained and equipped to do it safely

    Fighting a hazmat fire takes special training and protective gear, so a driver without both should leave it to firefighters.

    Manual section 9.7.3 Fires.

  7. If you discover a cargo leak, how should you identify the hazardous materials that are leaking?

    • ACarefully sniff the air near the leaking material
    • BTouch a little of the material with a gloved hand
    • CUse shipping papers, labels, or package location
    • DOpen the leaking package and look at what is inside

    Answer: C. Use shipping papers, labels, or package location

    Identify a leak from the paperwork, the labels and where the package sits, never by touching or smelling the material.

    Manual section 9.7.3 Fires.

  8. You or your employer must phone the National Response Center when estimated property damage from a hazardous materials incident exceeds:

    • A$50,000
    • B$75,000
    • C$100,000
    • D$250,000

    Answer: A. $50,000

    Estimated property damage of more than 50,000 dollars is one of the events that requires a call to the National Response Center.

    Manual section 9.7.5 Required Notification.

  9. A vehicle was involved in a leak of Division 2.3 or Division 6.1 poisons. What must happen before it is used again?

    • AIt must sit parked for 24 hours
    • BIt must get new placards
    • CIt must be re-registered by the carrier
    • DIt must be checked for stray poison

    Answer: D. It must be checked for stray poison

    After a poison leak, the vehicle has to be inspected for leftover poison before it goes back into service.

    Manual section 9.7.4 Responses to Specific Hazards.

  10. After a spill of radioactive material, when may the vehicle be used again?

    • AAfter it has been parked outdoors for 24 hours
    • BAfter all of its placards have been replaced
    • CAfter cleaning and a check with a survey meter
    • DAfter the driver signs a written release form

    Answer: C. After cleaning and a check with a survey meter

    A vehicle involved in a radioactive spill stays out of use until it has been cleaned and tested with a survey meter.

    Manual section 9.7.4 Responses to Specific Hazards.

  11. When you send someone for help during a hazardous materials spill, which information should you give that person?

    • AThe total dollar value of the cargo on board
    • BYour CDL number and the date it expires
    • CYour exact location and direction of travel
    • DThe time and place of your last rest break

    Answer: C. Your exact location and direction of travel

    The person going for help must be able to tell responders exactly where you are and which way you were heading, along with the other listed details.

    Manual section 9.7.3 Fires.

  12. Within how many days of an incident must carriers make detailed written reports?

    • A5 days
    • B10 days
    • C15 days
    • D30 days

    Answer: D. 30 days

    Besides the phone call, the carrier must file a detailed written report no later than 30 days after the incident.

    Manual section 9.7.5 Required Notification.
