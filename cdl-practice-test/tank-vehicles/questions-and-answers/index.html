Tank vehicle endorsement (4 questions)

  1. What total (aggregate) rated capacity of the tanks is part of the rule for needing a tank endorsement on a Class A or B vehicle?

    • A1,000 gallons or more
    • B1,500 gallons or more
    • C2,500 gallons or more
    • D5,000 gallons or more

    Answer: A. 1,000 gallons or more

    Besides the size of each tank, the tanks together must be rated to hold at least 1,000 gallons.

    Manual section 8 Tank Vehicles.

  2. When does a Class C vehicle require a tank endorsement?

    • AWhenever it hauls any liquid in a tank
    • BNever; the endorsement is only for Class A and B
    • COnly when its tank holds over 5,000 gallons
    • DWhen it hauls hazardous liquids or gases in tanks of the rated sizes the rule covers

    Answer: D. When it hauls hazardous liquids or gases in tanks of the rated sizes the rule covers

    For Class C vehicles, the endorsement applies when they carry hazardous materials as a liquid or gas in tanks of the sizes the rule describes.

    Manual section 8 Tank Vehicles.

  3. For a Class A or B vehicle, the tank endorsement rule covers tanks with an individual rated capacity of more than how many gallons?

    • A50 gallons
    • B100 gallons
    • C119 gallons
    • D500 gallons

    Answer: C. 119 gallons

    Each tank counts toward the endorsement rule when its own rated capacity is over 119 gallons.

    Manual section 8 Tank Vehicles.

  4. If your vehicle needs a Class A or B CDL, does the liquid or gas you haul in a tank have to be a hazardous material for a tank endorsement to be required?

    • AYes, only hazardous liquids or gases count
    • BNo, it does not have to be a hazardous material
    • COnly if the cargo is a flammable liquid
    • DOnly if the cargo is a gas under pressure

    Answer: B. No, it does not have to be a hazardous material

    A tank endorsement can be needed even when the liquid or gas in the tank is not hazardous at all.

    Manual section 8 Tank Vehicles.

Inspecting tank vehicles (8 questions)

  1. What should you do about the emergency equipment required for your tank vehicle?

    • ALeave it at the terminal unless the cargo is hazardous
    • BFind out what is required, and make sure you have it and it works
    • CRely on the shipper to supply it at each loading site
    • DCheck it only once a year at the annual inspection

    Answer: B. Find out what is required, and make sure you have it and it works

    Learn which emergency items your vehicle must carry, then confirm each one is on board and in working order.

    Manual section 8.1.3 Special Equipment.

  2. What happens if you carry liquids or gases in a leaking tank?

    • ANothing, as long as the leak is small and slow
    • BYou get a warning and may finish the trip
    • CIt is a crime; you will be cited and stopped from driving
    • DIt is allowed if the cargo is not hazardous

    Answer: C. It is a crime; you will be cited and stopped from driving

    Hauling cargo in a leaking tank is against the law, so you get a citation and are not allowed to keep driving.

    Manual section 8.1.1 Leaks.

  3. On all tank vehicles, what is the most important item to check for during an inspection?

    • ATire tread depth
    • BMirror adjustment
    • CPlacard condition
    • DLeaks

    Answer: D. Leaks

    Checking for leaks comes first on every kind of tank vehicle, looking under and around the whole vehicle.

    Manual section 8.1.1 Leaks.

  4. When checking the manhole covers on a tank vehicle, what should you make sure of?

    • AThey are left slightly open for air
    • BThey are painted to match the tank
    • CThey have gaskets and close correctly
    • DThey are removed before loading

    Answer: C. They have gaskets and close correctly

    Each manhole cover needs its gasket in place and must shut and seal properly.

    Manual section 8.1.1 Leaks.

  5. Which of these should you never do with a tank vehicle?

    • ADrive it with open valves or manhole covers
    • BDrive it with its headlights on in daytime
    • CDrive it with the tank less than half full
    • DDrive it in the right lane on a highway

    Answer: A. Drive it with open valves or manhole covers

    Valves and manhole covers must all be closed before a tank vehicle moves.

    Manual section 8.1.2 Check Special Purpose Equipment.

  6. Because tank vehicles come in many types and sizes, where should you check to learn how to inspect your own tank vehicle?

    • AThe cargo's shipping papers
    • BThe vehicle's operator manual
    • CThe tank's capacity plate
    • DThe last driver's trip log

    Answer: B. The vehicle's operator manual

    Inspection items differ between tank designs, so the operator manual for your vehicle tells you how to inspect it.

    Manual section 8.1 Inspecting Tank Vehicles.

  7. Which of these is listed as special purpose equipment you must make sure works if your tank vehicle has it?

    • AA backup camera
    • BA sleeper berth heater
    • CA CB radio
    • DGrounding and bonding cables

    Answer: D. Grounding and bonding cables

    If a tank vehicle is equipped with grounding and bonding cables, checking that they work is part of the inspection.

    Manual section 8.1.2 Check Special Purpose Equipment.

  8. Which items must be in the correct position before you load, unload, or move a tank vehicle?

    • AThe intake, discharge, and cut-off valves
    • BThe mirrors and windshield wipers
    • CThe fifth wheel locking jaws
    • DThe trailer landing gear handles

    Answer: A. The intake, discharge, and cut-off valves

    As part of the leak check, you confirm that each tank valve is set the right way before any loading, unloading, or driving.

    Manual section 8.1.1 Leaks.

Driving tank vehicles (9 questions)

  1. What kind of liquid surge can still happen in a baffled tank?

    • ASide-to-side surge, which can cause a rollover
    • BNone, because baffles stop all surge
    • COnly surge when the tank is completely full
    • DOnly forward surge when the truck speeds up

    Answer: A. Side-to-side surge, which can cause a rollover

    Baffles help with front-to-back movement, but the liquid can still slosh sideways and tip the vehicle over.

    Manual section 8.2.4 Baffled Tanks.

  2. When loading and unloading a tank that bulkheads divide into smaller tanks, what should you avoid?

    • ALoading more than one of the smaller tanks
    • BLeaving any of the smaller tanks empty
    • CPutting too much weight on the front or rear
    • DUnloading the front tank before the rear tank

    Answer: C. Putting too much weight on the front or rear

    Each compartment you fill or empty shifts the vehicle's weight, so keep the load balanced and do not overload either end.

    Manual section 8.2.3 Bulkheads.

  3. Why may you often only partially fill a tank with a heavy liquid such as some acids?

    • AHeavy liquids do not expand when they warm
    • BAcids must have air above them to stay stable
    • CA full tank may exceed legal weight limits
    • DHeavy liquids surge more in a full tank

    Answer: C. A full tank may exceed legal weight limits

    Dense liquids weigh so much that filling the tank could put the vehicle over the legal weight.

    Manual section 8.2.7 How Much to Load?.

  4. What does a high center of gravity do to a tank vehicle?

    • AMakes it steadier in curves
    • BMakes it easier to stop on wet roads
    • CMakes it top-heavy and easy to roll over
    • DMakes it less likely to skid on ice

    Answer: C. Makes it top-heavy and easy to roll over

    With much of the load's weight carried high above the road, the tanker is top-heavy and can tip over easily.

    Manual section 8.2.1 High Center of Gravity.

  5. Why do different liquids need different amounts of outage?

    • AThey have different colors
    • BThey expand by different amounts
    • CSome are hazardous and some are not
    • DThey need different unloading hoses

    Answer: B. They expand by different amounts

    Outage is room left for a liquid to expand, and since each liquid expands a different amount, each needs its own amount of space.

    Manual section 8.2.6 Outage.

  6. What causes liquid surge in a tank vehicle?

    • AMovement of the liquid in partially filled tanks
    • BPressure building up in completely full tanks
    • CHeat from the engine warming the cargo
    • DWorn springs in the trailer suspension

    Answer: A. Movement of the liquid in partially filled tanks

    When a tank is only partly full, the liquid can move around inside it, and that movement is what surge is.

    Manual section 8.2.2 Danger of Surge.

  7. On a slippery surface such as ice, what can liquid surge do to a stopped tanker?

    • ALift its front wheels off the road
    • BShove it out into an intersection
    • CLock up its trailer brakes
    • DNothing, since the truck has stopped

    Answer: B. Shove it out into an intersection

    Even after the truck has stopped, the moving liquid can push it forward on ice, possibly into an intersection.

    Manual section 8.2.2 Danger of Surge.

  8. When you stop a partially filled tanker and the surge wave hits the end of the tank, which way does it tend to push the truck?

    • AOpposite to the direction the wave is moving
    • BStraight down onto the rear axles
    • CToward the side of the road
    • DIn the direction the wave is moving

    Answer: D. In the direction the wave is moving

    The moving liquid carries force, so when it reaches the tank wall it shoves the truck the same way the wave is traveling.

    Manual section 8.2.2 Danger of Surge.

  9. Un-baffled (smooth bore) tanks are usually used to haul what kind of cargo?

    • AGasoline and diesel fuel
    • BHeavy acids
    • CCompressed gases
    • DFood products such as milk

    Answer: D. Food products such as milk

    Tanks that carry food such as milk are usually built without baffles, because sanitation rules ban baffles that make the inside hard to clean.

    Manual section 8.2.5 Un-baffled Tanks.

Safe driving rules (7 questions)

  1. What is the right way to take a curve in a tank vehicle?

    • AKeep a steady speed and brake in the middle of the curve
    • BCoast through the curve with the clutch pushed in
    • CBrake firmly all the way through the curve
    • DSlow down before the curve, then accelerate slightly through it

    Answer: D. Slow down before the curve, then accelerate slightly through it

    Do your slowing before you enter the curve, then add a little power while you go through it.

    Manual section 8.3.3 Curves.

  2. Compared with a full tank vehicle, how might an empty tank vehicle stop?

    • AIt may take longer to stop
    • BIt always stops in half the distance
    • CIt stops in exactly the same distance
    • DIt cannot skid when braking

    Answer: A. It may take longer to stop

    A lighter load does not always mean a shorter stop; an empty tanker can need more distance than a loaded one.

    Manual section 8.3.4 Stopping Distance.

  3. If you must make a quick stop in a tank vehicle to avoid a crash, which braking method should you use?

    • AControlled or stab braking
    • BLocking all wheels and holding them
    • CPulling the parking brake only
    • DPumping the trailer hand valve

    Answer: A. Controlled or stab braking

    For an emergency stop in a tanker, use controlled braking or stab braking; subsection 2.17.2 explains both methods.

    Manual section 8.3.2 Controlling Surge.

  4. How should you make turns and lane changes in a tank vehicle?

    • AQuickly, to spend less time in the turn
    • BWith light braking through the whole move
    • CWith a firm, sharp turn of the wheel
    • DSmoothly, without sudden moves

    Answer: D. Smoothly, without sudden moves

    A top-heavy tanker with moving liquid must be handled gently, and that includes turning and changing lanes.

    Manual section 8.3.1 Drive Smoothly.

  5. What may happen if you steer quickly while braking in a tank vehicle?

    • AThe brakes may fade completely
    • BThe vehicle may roll over
    • CThe engine may stall
    • DThe tank valves may open

    Answer: B. The vehicle may roll over

    Sharp steering during braking can tip a tanker over, so keep steering inputs gentle while you brake.

    Manual section 8.3.2 Controlling Surge.

  6. On a tank trailer, what may happen if the drive wheels or trailer wheels begin to skid?

    • AThe tank valves may open
    • BThe liquid will stop surging
    • CThe vehicle may jackknife
    • DThe engine will shut down

    Answer: C. The vehicle may jackknife

    When drive or trailer wheels lose grip on a tank trailer, the rig can jackknife, so avoid oversteering, overaccelerating and overbraking.

    Manual section 8.3.5 Skids.

  7. What do wet roads do to the normal stopping distance of a tank vehicle?

    • ALeave it unchanged
    • BDouble it
    • CTriple it
    • DMake it about 10 percent longer

    Answer: B. Double it

    On wet pavement, plan on needing twice your usual distance to stop.

    Manual section 8.3.4 Stopping Distance.
