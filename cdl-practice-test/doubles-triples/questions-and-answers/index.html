Pulling double and triple trailers (7 questions)

  1. Why are doubles and triples more likely to turn over than other combination vehicles?

    • ABecause of the crack-the-whip effect
    • BBecause of the brake fade effect
    • CBecause of the air lag effect
    • DBecause of the bobtail effect

    Answer: A. Because of the crack-the-whip effect

    The manual names the crack-the-whip effect as the reason these rigs tip over more readily, which is why gentle steering matters so much.

    Manual section 7.1.2 Beware of the Crack-the-whip Effect.

  2. A curve speed that is safe for a single trailer combination vehicle may be:

    • AAlways safe for a set of doubles or triples
    • BToo slow for a set of doubles or triples
    • CToo fast for a set of doubles or triples
    • DSafe for doubles or triples only when empty

    Answer: C. Too fast for a set of doubles or triples

    Doubles and triples need a lower speed on curves than a straight truck or single trailer rig, so a speed that works for those can still be too fast.

    Manual section 7.1.1 Prevent Trailer from Rolling Over.

  3. In bad weather, why is there a greater chance of skids and loss of traction with double and triple bottoms?

    • AThe rear trailers have no brakes on their axles
    • BTheir tires are narrower than other truck tires
    • CThey have more length and more dead axles to pull
    • DThey have fewer axles than other combination vehicles

    Answer: C. They have more length and more dead axles to pull

    The drive axles have to pull a longer rig with more unpowered axles behind them, so slippery roads and mountain driving call for extra care.

    Manual section 7.1.6 Adverse Conditions.

  4. Converter dollies built on or after which date are required to have antilock brakes?

    • AJanuary 1, 1990
    • BJuly 1, 1995
    • CMarch 1, 2008
    • DMarch 1, 1998

    Answer: D. March 1, 1998

    The manual sets March 1, 1998 as the build date from which converter dollies must come with antilock brakes.

    Manual section 7.1.8 Antilock Braking Systems on Converter Dollies.

  5. Besides being longer, why do doubles and triples need more space than other commercial vehicles?

    • AThey must always travel in the right lane
    • BThey cannot be turned or stopped suddenly
    • CThey need room for the dolly to swing out
    • DThey have to stop at every weigh station

    Answer: B. They cannot be turned or stopped suddenly

    Because these rigs cannot make sudden turns or stops, you leave more following distance and wait for bigger gaps in traffic.

    Manual section 7.1.5 Manage Space.

  6. Why should you look far ahead when driving doubles or triples?

    • ASo you can brake hard at the last moment
    • BSo you can pass slower traffic more quickly
    • CSo you can spot weigh stations before others
    • DSo you can slow down or change lanes gradually

    Answer: D. So you can slow down or change lanes gradually

    These rigs must be driven very smoothly to avoid a rollover or jackknife, and seeing trouble early gives you time to make gradual changes.

    Manual section 7.1.4 Look Far Ahead.

  7. When parking doubles or triples, what kind of spot should you avoid getting into?

    • AA spot you cannot pull straight through
    • BA spot that is wider than your rig
    • CA spot on firm and level pavement
    • DA spot close to the exit of the lot

    Answer: A. A spot you cannot pull straight through

    Getting out of a tight spot with more than one trailer can be long and hard, so pick a place you can drive straight out of.

    Manual section 7.1.7 Parking the Vehicle.

Coupling and uncoupling (11 questions)

  1. When uncoupling the rear trailer, what is the last step before slowly pulling the dolly out from under the rear semitrailer?

    • ARelease the pintle hook on the first trailer
    • BDisconnect the safety chains on the dolly
    • CRaise the landing gear of the rear trailer
    • DRelease the converter dolly fifth wheel latch

    Answer: D. Release the converter dolly fifth wheel latch

    Once the dolly's fifth wheel latch is released, the tractor, first trailer and dolly are eased forward together so the dolly comes out from under the rear trailer.

    Manual section 7.2.2 Uncoupling Twin Trailers.

  2. When you wheel the converter dolly into place in front of the second trailer, what should it be lined up with?

    • AThe landing gear of the second trailer
    • BThe glad hands of the second trailer
    • CThe kingpin of the second trailer
    • DThe rear axle of the first trailer

    Answer: C. The kingpin of the second trailer

    The dolly has to sit in line with the second trailer's kingpin so it can then be backed under that trailer.

    Manual section 7.2.1 Coupling Twin Trailers.

  3. Before positioning a converter dolly that does not have spring brakes, how do you release the dolly brakes?

    • AOpen the dolly air tank petcock
    • BPull out the trailer air supply knob
    • CClose the shut-offs on the first trailer
    • DRelease the pintle hook on the trailer

    Answer: A. Open the dolly air tank petcock

    Letting the air out through the dolly tank petcock frees its brakes so it can be moved; a dolly with spring brakes uses its parking brake control instead.

    Manual section 7.2.1 Coupling Twin Trailers.

  4. For the safest handling on the road, where should the more heavily loaded semitrailer go in a set of doubles?

    • AIn the rear position, behind the dolly
    • BIn first position, right behind the tractor
    • CIn either position if the dolly has ABS
    • DIn the rear position on slippery roads

    Answer: B. In first position, right behind the tractor

    Putting the heavier trailer up front and the lighter one at the back gives the safest handling.

    Manual section 7.2.1 Coupling Twin Trailers.

  5. After backing the converter dolly under the rear trailer, why do you raise the landing gear slightly off the ground?

    • ATo level the trailer for the brake test
    • BTo let air reach the trailer brakes
    • CTo unlock the dolly fifth wheel latch
    • DTo prevent damage if the trailer moves

    Answer: D. To prevent damage if the trailer moves

    Lifting the landing gear a little keeps it from being damaged should the trailer shift before the coupling is finished.

    Manual section 7.2.1 Coupling Twin Trailers.

  6. For a combination whose coupling method is not covered in the manual, how should you learn to couple and uncouple it?

    • ABy using the triples method for every rig
    • BBy using the tractor-semitrailer method
    • CBy asking any driver who has pulled one
    • DFrom the manufacturer and/or owner specifications

    Answer: D. From the manufacturer and/or owner specifications

    There are too many truck-trailer and tractor-trailer types for one manual, so you follow what the maker or owner of your vehicle specifies.

    Manual section 7.2.4 Coupling and Uncoupling Other Combinations.

  7. How do you test the coupling after backing the converter dolly under the second semitrailer?

    • APush the trailer air supply knob in and out
    • BPull against the pin of the second semitrailer
    • CBack firmly against the first semitrailer
    • DLift the dolly tongue by hand to see if it moves

    Answer: B. Pull against the pin of the second semitrailer

    Pulling forward against the second trailer's kingpin shows whether the dolly's fifth wheel has really locked onto it.

    Manual section 7.2.1 Coupling Twin Trailers.

  8. When uncoupling a triple-trailer rig, which trailer do you uncouple first?

    • AThe first trailer
    • BThe second trailer
    • CThe third trailer
    • DThe heaviest trailer

    Answer: C. The third trailer

    You work from the back: the third trailer comes off first, and the rest of the rig is then uncoupled like a set of doubles.

    Manual section 7.2.3 Coupling and Uncoupling Triple Trailers.

  9. Once the dolly is out from under the rear trailer, what is the first step in uncoupling the converter dolly?

    • ARelease the pintle hook
    • BLower the dolly landing gear
    • CSlowly pull clear of the dolly
    • DDisconnect the safety chains

    Answer: B. Lower the dolly landing gear

    The dolly's own landing gear goes down first, before the safety chains are undone and the pintle hook is released.

    Manual section 7.2.2 Uncoupling Twin Trailers.

  10. When uncoupling the rear trailer, how far should you lower the second semitrailer's landing gear?

    • AEnough to remove some weight from the dolly
    • BJust until it touches, with no weight on it
    • CUntil the trailer lifts fully off the dolly
    • DNot at all until the dolly is pulled out

    Answer: A. Enough to remove some weight from the dolly

    The landing gear is lowered until it takes some of the load off the dolly, not just until it touches the ground.

    Manual section 7.2.2 Uncoupling Twin Trailers.

  11. Where should you park the rig before uncoupling the rear trailer of a set of doubles?

    • AOn a slight downgrade so the dolly rolls free
    • BAt an angle so the dolly can come out sideways
    • CIn a straight line on firm, level ground
    • DOn soft ground so the landing gear can sink in

    Answer: C. In a straight line on firm, level ground

    Having the rig straight and on solid, level ground keeps the trailers and dolly steady while you separate them.

    Manual section 7.2.2 Uncoupling Twin Trailers.

Inspecting doubles and triples (7 questions)

  1. When inspecting the air lines to the trailer, how should they be secured?

    • AWith enough slack for turns
    • BPulled tight so they cannot move
    • CTied to the landing gear crank
    • DWrapped around the safety chains

    Answer: A. With enough slack for turns

    The air lines must be fastened in place but still have enough extra length to allow for turns.

    Manual section 7.3.1 Additional Checks.

  2. The extra coupling and trailer checks for doubles and triples are added to which step of the Section 2 inspection?

    • AStep 2
    • BStep 5
    • CStep 6
    • DStep 7

    Answer: B. Step 5

    These extra checks are added to the walk-around part of the inspection, which is Step 5 in Section 2.

    Manual section 7.3.1 Additional Checks.

  3. Why should you check that the fifth wheel is not too far forward?

    • ASo the air lines can reach the glad hands without stretching
    • BSo the frame and cab do not hit the landing gear or trailer in turns
    • CSo the trailer does not sit too high on the fifth wheel
    • DSo the locking jaws can reach the head of the kingpin

    Answer: B. So the frame and cab do not hit the landing gear or trailer in turns

    If the fifth wheel sits too far forward, parts of the tractor can strike the trailer or its landing gear when the rig turns.

    Manual section 7.3.1 Additional Checks.

  4. When checking the lower fifth wheel, where should the locking jaws be closed?

    • AAround the head of the kingpin, not the shank
    • BAround the glide plate on the trailer frame
    • CAround the shank of the kingpin, not the head
    • DAround the pintle-eye of the converter dolly

    Answer: C. Around the shank of the kingpin, not the head

    The jaws must close on the kingpin's shank; jaws closed on the head of the kingpin do not pass the check.

    Manual section 7.3.1 Additional Checks.

  5. If the landing gear is power operated, what should you check for during the inspection?

    • AThe crank handle has been removed
    • BThe legs rest lightly on the ground
    • CNo air or hydraulic leaks
    • DThe motor stays on while driving

    Answer: C. No air or hydraulic leaks

    For landing gear that is power operated, the inspection includes making sure nothing is leaking air or hydraulic fluid.

    Manual section 7.3.1 Additional Checks.

  6. Which inspection procedure should you use to inspect a double or triple combination vehicle?

    • AThe seven-step procedure described in Section 2
    • BA five-step procedure used only for trailers
    • CA ten-step procedure done only at a shop
    • DA two-step check of the tractor and dolly

    Answer: A. The seven-step procedure described in Section 2

    Doubles and triples are inspected with the same seven-step method as other vehicles, plus extra items for the added trailers and couplings.

    Manual section 7.3 Inspecting Doubles and Triples.

  7. When inspecting a sliding fifth wheel, what should you confirm about the locking pins?

    • AAt least half of them are engaged
    • BThey are removed before driving
    • CThey are greased and left unlocked
    • DAll are present and locked in place

    Answer: D. All are present and locked in place

    Every locking pin on a sliding fifth wheel has to be there and engaged, not just some of them.

    Manual section 7.3.1 Additional Checks.

Doubles and triples air brake check (5 questions)

  1. Why must air reach all the way to the back of a double or triple rig?

    • ASo the trailer lights get power
    • BSo the landing gear can be raised
    • CSo the dolly ABS lamp will light
    • DSo all the brakes will work

    Answer: D. So all the brakes will work

    Air has to flow through every trailer and dolly to the last trailer, or some of the brakes will not work.

    Manual section 7.4.1 Additional Air Brake Checks.

  2. The trailer air supply control is also called the:

    • ATrailer hand valve
    • BTractor protection valve control
    • CService line shut-off
    • DConverter dolly petcock

    Answer: B. Tractor protection valve control

    The manual uses both names for the same control, and it may also be called the trailer emergency valve.

    Manual section 7.4.1 Additional Air Brake Checks.

  3. In normal operation, the foot brake pedal applies air to which brakes?

    • AOnly the trailer emergency brakes
    • BOnly the tractor steering axle brakes
    • CThe service brakes at all wheels
    • DOnly the converter dolly brakes

    Answer: C. The service brakes at all wheels

    The hand valve is used for testing the trailer brakes, but in everyday driving the foot pedal sends air to the service brakes on every wheel.

    Manual section 7.4.1 Additional Air Brake Checks.

  4. If the tractor protection valve does not work properly and a trailer brake leak drains all the air from the tractor, what could happen?

    • AThe emergency brakes could come on, with possible loss of control
    • BThe trailer lights could stop working until the leak is fixed
    • CThe engine could shut off to save the remaining air
    • DThe service brakes would work but the parking brake would not

    Answer: A. The emergency brakes could come on, with possible loss of control

    A faulty tractor protection valve lets a trailer leak empty the tractor's air too, and losing that air sets the emergency brakes, which can cost you control.

    Manual section 7.4.1 Additional Air Brake Checks.

  5. In the tractor protection valve test, the trailer air supply control should pop out when air pressure falls into the manufacturer's range, which is usually:

    • A5 to 15 psi
    • B60 to 75 psi
    • C90 to 100 psi
    • D20 to 45 psi

    Answer: D. 20 to 45 psi

    The control should move to the emergency position once pressure drops into the maker's range, normally somewhere from 20 to 45 psi.

    Manual section 7.4.1 Additional Air Brake Checks.
