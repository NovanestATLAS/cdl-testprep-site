Driving combinations safely (11 questions)

  1. While backing a trailer, you see it start to drift off the proper path. How should you correct it?

    • ATurn the top of the steering wheel away from the drift
    • BHold the wheel straight and let the trailer settle
    • CTurn the top of the steering wheel in the direction of the drift
    • DSpeed up slightly so the trailer straightens out

    Answer: C. Turn the top of the steering wheel in the direction of the drift

    Fix the drift right away by turning the top of the wheel toward the side the trailer is drifting to; backing slowly gives you time to make these corrections.

    Manual section 6.1.7 Backing with a Trailer.

  2. Compared with a tractor-semitrailer loaded to maximum gross weight, how does a bobtail tractor (a tractor with no semitrailer) stop?

    • AIt takes longer to stop
    • BIt stops in about half the distance
    • CIt stops in the same distance
    • DIt stops faster but pulls to one side

    Answer: A. It takes longer to stop

    Tests show a tractor running without a semitrailer is hard to stop smoothly and needs more distance than a fully loaded tractor-semitrailer.

    Manual section 6.1.3 Brake Early.

  3. How does the stopping distance of a large combination vehicle compare when it is empty versus fully loaded?

    • AIt is shorter when empty
    • BIt is the same whether empty or loaded
    • CIt is shorter when empty, except on wet roads
    • DIt is longer when empty

    Answer: D. It is longer when empty

    A lightly loaded rig has poor traction because of its stiff springs and strong brakes, so it needs more distance to stop than a full one.

    Manual section 6.1.3 Brake Early.

  4. When pulling trailers at more than 40 mph, how much following distance does the manual call for?

    • AAt least 1 second for each 10 feet of vehicle length, plus 1 more second
    • BAt least 1 second for each 20 feet of vehicle length, plus 1 more second
    • CAt least 2 seconds for each 10 feet of vehicle length, with no extra time
    • DAt least 1 second for each 10 feet of vehicle length, with no extra time

    Answer: A. At least 1 second for each 10 feet of vehicle length, plus 1 more second

    The base rule is one second per 10 feet of rig length, and above 40 mph you add one more second on top of that.

    Manual section 6.1.2 Steer Gently.

  5. What is more likely if the load sits to one side so that it makes the trailer lean?

    • AThe trailer brakes failing
    • BThe air-lines coming apart
    • CThe fifth wheel unlocking
    • DThe rig rolling over

    Answer: D. The rig rolling over

    An off-center load shifts weight to one side of the trailer and raises the chance of tipping over, so cargo should be centered and spread out.

    Manual section 6.1.1 Rollover Risks.

  6. In a rig with more than one trailer, which wheels off-track the most in a turn?

    • AThe rear wheels of the tractor
    • BThe rear wheels of the first trailer
    • CThe front wheels of the tractor
    • DThe rear wheels of the last trailer

    Answer: D. The rear wheels of the last trailer

    Off-tracking grows toward the back of the rig: the tractor's rear wheels off-track some, a trailer's more, and the last trailer's rear wheels the most.

    Manual section 6.1.6 Turn Wide.

  7. On the rearward amplification scale, what does a value of 2.0 mean?

    • AThe rear trailer weighs twice as much as the tractor
    • BThe rear trailer is twice as likely to turn over as the tractor
    • CThe rear trailer needs twice the stopping distance of the tractor
    • DThe rear trailer off-tracks twice as far as the tractor

    Answer: B. The rear trailer is twice as likely to turn over as the tractor

    Rearward amplification compares how easily the rear trailer rolls with the tractor, so a value of 2.0 means the rear trailer is two times as likely to roll.

    Manual section 6.1.2 Steer Gently.

  8. According to the manual, about what share of truck driver deaths in crashes result from truck rollovers?

    • AFewer than one in ten
    • BAbout one in five
    • CMore than half
    • DAbout one in three

    Answer: C. More than half

    Rollovers account for the majority of truck driver crash deaths, which is why keeping the rig upright matters so much.

    Manual section 6.1.1 Rollover Risks.

  9. If your rig gets stuck on railroad tracks, what should you do first?

    • AStay in the cab and keep trying to rock the rig free
    • BLower the landing gear to lift the trailer off the rails
    • CGet out of the vehicle and away from the tracks
    • DStay in the cab and call 911 for help

    Answer: C. Get out of the vehicle and away from the tracks

    Leave the rig and move clear of the rails before anything else; then look for the emergency information posted at the crossing and call for help.

    Manual section 6.1.4 Railroad-highway Crossings.

  10. Once you see in your mirrors that the trailer has started to skid, what should you do with the brakes?

    • AApply them harder to stop the rig quickly
    • BRelease them so the wheels get traction back
    • CPump them rapidly until the skid stops
    • DHold steady pressure on them until the rig stops

    Answer: B. Release them so the wheels get traction back

    Letting off the brakes allows the trailer wheels to roll and grip the road again, and then the trailer starts to follow the tractor and straighten out.

    Manual section 6.1.5 Prevent Trailer Skids.

  11. If you cannot complete a turn without entering another traffic lane, when should you turn wide?

    • ABefore starting the turn, by swinging wide to the left
    • BAs you complete the turn
    • CAt the very start, before the front wheels reach the corner
    • DAt no point; keep the whole rig in your own lane

    Answer: B. As you complete the turn

    Going wide at the end of the turn keeps the rear of the rig near the curb so other drivers cannot pass on the right, which swinging left first would allow.

    Manual section 6.1.6 Turn Wide.

Combination air brakes (11 questions)

  1. On newer vehicles, what does the trailer air supply control look like?

    • AA blue round knob
    • BA yellow diamond-shaped knob
    • CA red eight-sided knob
    • DA black lever on the steering column

    Answer: C. A red eight-sided knob

    Newer rigs use a red knob with eight sides in the cab to work the tractor protection valve.

    Manual section 6.2.3 Trailer Air Supply Control.

  2. If you cross the air-lines when coupling, where does the supply air go?

    • ATo the service line instead of charging the trailer air tanks
    • BBack into the tractor's main air tanks instead
    • COut through the drain valves on the trailer tanks
    • DStraight to the trailer spring brakes, releasing them

    Answer: A. To the service line instead of charging the trailer air tanks

    With the lines crossed, the air meant to fill the trailer tanks ends up in the service line, so no air is available to release the trailer spring brakes.

    Manual section 6.2.5 Hose Couplers (Glad Hands).

  3. How often should you drain each trailer air tank?

    • AOnce a week
    • BEvery day
    • COnce a month
    • DOnly when the brakes start to feel weak

    Answer: B. Every day

    Daily draining keeps water and oil from building up and spoiling brake performance; even tanks with automatic drains should be opened to make sure.

    Manual section 6.2.6 Trailer Air Tanks.

  4. What color is often used to code emergency air-lines so they are not mixed up with service lines?

    • ARed
    • BBlue
    • CYellow
    • DGreen

    Answer: A. Red

    Red hoses, couplers or other parts mark the emergency line, while blue marks the service line.

    Manual section 6.2.4 Trailer Air-lines.

  5. When you connect glad hands, how should the couplers be held as you press the two seals together?

    • AParallel to each other
    • BAt a 45 degree angle to each other
    • CEnd to end in a straight line
    • DAt a 90 degree angle to each other

    Answer: D. At a 90 degree angle to each other

    The seals go together with the couplers at right angles, and then a turn of the glad hand on the hose joins and locks them.

    Manual section 6.2.5 Hose Couplers (Glad Hands).

  6. Why should you never use the trailer hand valve to park?

    • AAll the air might leak out and unlock the brakes
    • BIt applies the tractor brakes much too hard
    • CIt closes the tractor protection valve right away
    • DIt drains the air from the tractor tanks

    Answer: A. All the air might leak out and unlock the brakes

    The hand valve holds the trailer with air, and on a trailer without spring brakes that air can leak away and let the brakes go, so use the parking brakes instead.

    Manual section 6.2.1 Trailer Hand Valve.

  7. Which units are not required to have spring brakes?

    • ATrailers built after March 1, 1998
    • BConverter dollies and trailers built before 1975
    • CAny trailer used to tow another trailer
    • DTrucks and truck tractors built before 1975

    Answer: B. Converter dollies and trailers built before 1975

    Converter dollies and trailers from before 1975 may lack spring brakes and rely on emergency brakes that run on air stored in the trailer tank.

    Manual section 6.2.8 Trailer Service, Parking and Emergency Brakes.

  8. When might you first notice a major leak in the service line?

    • AWhen you first start the engine
    • BWhen you push in the trailer air supply control
    • CDuring a sharp turn
    • DWhen you try to put the brakes on

    Answer: D. When you try to put the brakes on

    A service line leak can stay hidden until you brake, when the escaping air quickly drops tank pressure and may set off the trailer emergency brakes.

    Manual section 6.2.8 Trailer Service, Parking and Emergency Brakes.

  9. What do shut-off valves (cut-out cocks) in the trailer air-lines allow you to do?

    • ADrain water and oil from the trailer air tanks
    • BRelease the trailer spring brakes by hand
    • CClose off the air-lines when no other trailer is towed
    • DKeep air in the tractor if the trailer breaks away

    Answer: C. Close off the air-lines when no other trailer is towed

    These valves let you seal off the service and supply lines when no other trailer is hooked up behind.

    Manual section 6.2.7 Shut-off Valves.

  10. When the tractor protection valve closes, what happens to the trailer?

    • AThe trailer brakes are fully released
    • BThe trailer service line gets extra air pressure
    • CThe trailer air tanks are refilled from the tractor
    • DThe trailer emergency brakes come on

    Answer: D. The trailer emergency brakes come on

    A closed valve stops air leaving the tractor and vents the trailer's emergency line, so the trailer emergency brakes apply, possibly with loss of control.

    Manual section 6.2.2 Tractor Protection Valve.

  11. The tractor protection valve closes automatically when air pressure falls into what range?

    • A60 to 80 psi
    • B20 to 45 psi
    • C100 to 125 psi
    • D5 to 10 psi

    Answer: B. 20 to 45 psi

    Once pressure gets low, roughly 20 to 45 psi, the valve shuts on its own so the tractor keeps its air.

    Manual section 6.2.2 Tractor Protection Valve.

Trailer antilock brakes (4 questions)

  1. What does ABS do for you during hard braking?

    • AIt always shortens your stopping distance on any road
    • BIt helps you keep control but may not shorten stopping distance
    • CIt adds extra braking power to your normal brakes
    • DIt lets you safely follow other vehicles more closely

    Answer: B. It helps you keep control but may not shorten stopping distance

    ABS works to prevent wheel lockup so you stay in control; it does not promise a shorter stop and adds no extra braking power.

    Manual section 6.3.2 Braking with ABS.

  2. If the ABS on your combination vehicle malfunctions, what should you do?

    • AStop and do not move the vehicle until it is repaired
    • BDrive only in the right lane at reduced speed until it is repaired
    • CBrake only with the trailer hand valve until it is repaired
    • DDrive normally and get the system serviced soon

    Answer: D. Drive normally and get the system serviced soon

    A failed ABS still leaves your regular brakes working, so you can keep driving as usual but should have the system fixed soon.

    Manual section 6.3.2 Braking with ABS.

  3. Trailers and converter dollies built on or after what date are required to have ABS?

    • AJanuary 1, 1975
    • BMarch 1, 1988
    • CMarch 1, 1998
    • DJanuary 1, 2008

    Answer: C. March 1, 1998

    The ABS requirement covers trailers and converter dollies built from March 1, 1998 onward, though many older units were fitted with ABS voluntarily.

    Manual section 6.3.1 Trailers Required to Have ABS.

  4. Only your trailer has ABS, and you start a tractor jackknife. What should you do?

    • ALet up on the brakes, if safe, until you regain control
    • BBrake harder so the trailer ABS can take over
    • CApply the trailer hand valve to straighten the rig
    • DPull out the trailer air supply control right away

    Answer: A. Let up on the brakes, if safe, until you regain control

    Trailer ABS makes the trailer less likely to swing out but does not prevent a tractor jackknife, so ease off the brakes when it is safe until the tractor is back under control.

    Manual section 6.3.2 Braking with ABS.

Coupling and uncoupling (15 questions)

  1. Which gear should you use to back the tractor under the trailer?

    • AThe highest reverse gear
    • BNeutral, letting the tractor roll back
    • CAny reverse gear, as long as the engine is at idle
    • DThe lowest reverse gear

    Answer: D. The lowest reverse gear

    Backing slowly in the lowest reverse gear keeps you from striking the kingpin too hard, and you stop once it is locked into the fifth wheel.

    Manual section 6.4.1 Coupling Tractor-Semitrailers.

  2. After backing under the trailer, how do you check that the trailer is locked onto the tractor?

    • ARelease the trailer brakes and back up firmly against it
    • BPull gently forward while the trailer brakes are still locked
    • CDrive a short loop around the lot with the landing gear down
    • DRock the trailer by hand from the rear bumper

    Answer: B. Pull gently forward while the trailer brakes are still locked

    With the landing gear raised slightly off the ground, a gentle tug forward against the locked trailer brakes shows whether the trailer has latched onto the tractor.

    Manual section 6.4.1 Coupling Tractor-Semitrailers.

  3. When coupling a drawbar, what does the manual say if the safety cover bar will not lodge properly into its seating?

    • ATravel is strictly forbidden
    • BYou may drive slowly to a repair shop
    • CTighten the lock screws further and drive on
    • DUse the safety chains in its place

    Answer: A. Travel is strictly forbidden

    If the safety cover bar does not sit correctly in its seat, the vehicle must not be driven at all.

    Manual section 6.4.5 Coupling a Drawbar.

  4. What is the first step when uncoupling a drawbar?

    • ARemove the rubber dust proof bellows cover
    • BMake sure the trailer brake is on
    • CRotate the safety cover bar outwards
    • DLift the trailer drawbeam

    Answer: B. Make sure the trailer brake is on

    Drawbar uncoupling starts with confirming the trailer brake is applied; only after that do you remove the cover and loosen the screws.

    Manual section 6.4.6 Uncoupling a Drawbar.

  5. When uncoupling, why do you back up gently after shutting off the trailer air supply?

    • ATo ease pressure on the fifth wheel locking jaws
    • BTo set the trailer spring brakes before unhooking
    • CTo line the tractor up straight with the trailer
    • DTo lower the landing gear onto the ground

    Answer: A. To ease pressure on the fifth wheel locking jaws

    Backing gently takes the strain off the fifth wheel jaws, which makes the release lever easier to work; the parking brakes then hold the rig in that position.

    Manual section 6.4.2 Uncoupling Tractor-Semitrailers.

  6. Before coupling, how should the fifth wheel be set?

    • ALevel, with the jaws closed
    • BTilted down toward the rear of the tractor, with the jaws open
    • CTilted up toward the cab, with the jaws open
    • DTilted down toward the rear of the tractor, with the jaws closed

    Answer: B. Tilted down toward the rear of the tractor, with the jaws open

    For coupling, the fifth wheel should slope down toward the back of the tractor with its jaws open and the safety unlocking handle in the automatic lock position.

    Manual section 6.4.1 Coupling Tractor-Semitrailers.

  7. What problem can happen if the fifth wheel plate is not kept lubricated?

    • ASteering problems from tractor-trailer friction
    • BAir leaks at the glad hands when the rig turns
    • CThe landing gear will not raise all the way
    • DThe trailer lights will flicker or fail

    Answer: A. Steering problems from tractor-trailer friction

    Without enough grease, the tractor and trailer rub against each other at the fifth wheel, and that friction can make the rig hard to steer.

    Manual section 6.4.1 Coupling Tractor-Semitrailers.

  8. When you pull the tractor partly clear during uncoupling, why should you stop with the tractor frame still under the trailer?

    • ASo the air-lines can still be disconnected safely
    • BSo the trailer won't fall if the landing gear gives way
    • CSo the fifth wheel can be greased before you leave
    • DSo the trailer brakes can be tested one last time

    Answer: B. So the trailer won't fall if the landing gear gives way

    The frame acts as a backup support in case the landing gear gives way, and you then secure the tractor and inspect the trailer supports.

    Manual section 6.4.2 Uncoupling Tractor-Semitrailers.

  9. When uncoupling a gooseneck hitch, what does the coupler do after you rotate the handle and raise the trailer off the ball?

    • AIt returns to the load position automatically
    • BIt must be reset by hand with a wrench
    • CIt locks back onto the ball
    • DIt releases the trailer brakes

    Answer: A. It returns to the load position automatically

    Once the trailer is lifted off the ball, the coupler resets itself to the load position, and you then put the safety pin and clip back in.

    Manual section 6.4.8 Uncoupling a Gooseneck Hitch.

  10. What does the manual say about safety chains when hooking up a gooseneck trailer?

    • AThey are needed only on loaded trailers
    • BThey are optional once the clamp is latched
    • CAll trailers are required by law to have them
    • DThey are used only with pintle hooks

    Answer: C. All trailers are required by law to have them

    Attaching safety chains is a required step because the law calls for them on every trailer.

    Manual section 6.4.7 Coupling a Gooseneck Hitch.

  11. When uncoupling a loaded trailer, what should you do after the landing gear makes firm contact with the ground?

    • AStop cranking right away
    • BKeep cranking until the trailer lifts off the fifth wheel
    • CTurn the crank in low gear a few extra turns
    • DSwitch to high gear and crank until the gear is fully extended

    Answer: C. Turn the crank in low gear a few extra turns

    A few extra low-gear turns take some weight off the tractor without lifting the trailer off the fifth wheel, which makes unlatching and the next coupling easier.

    Manual section 6.4.2 Uncoupling Tractor-Semitrailers.

  12. Why should you never back under a trailer at an angle?

    • AThe air-lines could get crossed or pulled loose
    • BThe trailer brakes could release and let it roll
    • CYou might push the trailer sideways and break the landing gear
    • DThe kingpin could bend against the edge of the fifth wheel

    Answer: C. You might push the trailer sideways and break the landing gear

    Backing in crooked can shove the trailer to the side and damage its landing gear, so put the tractor directly in front of the trailer first.

    Manual section 6.4.1 Coupling Tractor-Semitrailers.

  13. When uncoupling a pintle hook, what must you check before extending the jack?

    • AThat the safety chains are still attached to the tow vehicle
    • BThat all the trailer lights and brake lights work
    • CThat the ground under the jack pad can hold the tongue load
    • DThat the drawbar eye has been freshly greased

    Answer: C. That the ground under the jack pad can hold the tongue load

    The jack is about to carry the weight of the trailer tongue, so the surface under its pad has to be firm enough to hold that load.

    Manual section 6.4.4 Uncoupling a Pintle Hook.

  14. When a pintle hook latch is pushed closed and correctly locked, what does the lock handle do?

    • AIt drops down below the bottom of the latch
    • BIt pops out of the hook body and hangs free
    • CIt stays loose so it can be turned by hand
    • DIt rotates and moves up flush with the latch top

    Answer: D. It rotates and moves up flush with the latch top

    A good lock shows itself by the handle turning and rising to sit even with the top of the latch; then the tethered lock pin goes in, if the hook has one.

    Manual section 6.4.3 Coupling a Pintle Hook.

  15. When you secure the vehicle before inspecting the coupling, why should you take the key with you?

    • ASo nobody else moves the truck while you are under it
    • BSo the trailer brakes stay applied while you check
    • CSo the air pressure does not drop while you work
    • DSo the electrical cord cannot be unplugged by mistake

    Answer: A. So nobody else moves the truck while you are under it

    You will be working underneath the rig, so keeping the key on you prevents someone from driving it while you are in harm's way.

    Manual section 6.4.1 Coupling Tractor-Semitrailers.

Inspecting a combination vehicle (5 questions)

  1. When inspecting the air-lines to the trailer, what should you check about how they are secured?

    • AThey are pulled tight so they cannot move
    • BThey are taped to the electrical cord
    • CThey hang loose enough to rest on the frame
    • DThey are secured with enough slack for turns

    Answer: D. They are secured with enough slack for turns

    The lines should be connected to the glad hands without leaks and fastened so they still have enough give for the rig to turn.

    Manual section 6.5.1 Additional Things to Check during a Walk-around Inspection.

  2. Why do you check that a sliding fifth wheel is not set too far forward?

    • AThe trailer would sit too low for the landing gear to clear the ground
    • BThe fifth wheel grease would wear off the plate much faster
    • CThe frame could hit the landing gear, or the cab the trailer, in turns
    • DThe air-lines would be too short to reach the trailer glad hands

    Answer: C. The frame could hit the landing gear, or the cab the trailer, in turns

    With the fifth wheel too far forward, the tractor frame can strike the landing gear and the cab can strike the trailer when the rig turns.

    Manual section 6.5.1 Additional Things to Check during a Walk-around Inspection.

  3. During a walk-around inspection, where should the fifth wheel locking jaws be?

    • AAround the head of the kingpin, not the shank
    • BAround the base of the kingpin, not the shank
    • CAround the shank of the kingpin, not the head
    • DAround both the head and the base of the kingpin

    Answer: C. Around the shank of the kingpin, not the head

    The locking jaws belong around the kingpin's shank; jaws closed on the head instead mean the coupling is not right.

    Manual section 6.5.1 Additional Things to Check during a Walk-around Inspection.

  4. On a sliding fifth wheel, what should you check about the locking pins?

    • AThey are all present and locked in place
    • BAt least half of them are engaged
    • CThey are removed before driving
    • DThey are pulled out so the slide moves freely

    Answer: A. They are all present and locked in place

    Every locking pin on the slide has to be there and locked; a missing or unlocked pin is a problem to fix before driving.

    Manual section 6.5.1 Additional Things to Check during a Walk-around Inspection.

  5. What can happen if the tractor protection valve does not work right?

    • AThe trailer spring brakes would not apply when parked
    • BThe trailer ABS warning lamp would stay on
    • CThe trailer service brakes would apply too slowly
    • DA hose or trailer brake leak could drain all the tractor's air

    Answer: D. A hose or trailer brake leak could drain all the tractor's air

    The valve is meant to save the tractor's air when the trailer side leaks; if it fails, all that air can be lost, the emergency brakes come on, and control may be lost.

    Manual section 6.5.2 Combination Vehicle Brake Check.
