Vehicle inspection (10 questions)

  1. When should you do an after-trip inspection?

    • AOnly when you noticed a defect during the trip
    • BOnce a week, on the same day
    • COnly before a state inspection
    • DAt the end of the trip, day, or tour of duty on each vehicle you drove

    Answer: D. At the end of the trip, day, or tour of duty on each vehicle you drove

    Each vehicle you operated gets an after-trip inspection when the trip, day or tour of duty ends. A written report may list any problems.

    Manual section 2.1.2 Types of Vehicle Inspection.

  2. While testing the service brakes at about 5 mph, the vehicle pulls to one side when you brake firmly. What can this mean?

    • AThe ABS is working normally
    • BThe tires are overinflated
    • CThe parking brake is still set
    • DBrake trouble

    Answer: D. Brake trouble

    Pulling to either side during the stopping test is a sign that something may be wrong with the brakes.

    Manual section 2.1.5 Seven-step Inspection Method.

  3. Which tires are prohibited on the front wheels of a bus?

    • ATires with more than 4/32 inch of tread
    • BTires of the same size on both sides
    • CTires with valve caps in place
    • DRe-grooved, recapped, or retreaded tires

    Answer: D. Re-grooved, recapped, or retreaded tires

    A bus may not run re-grooved, recapped or retreaded tires on its front wheels.

    Manual section 2.1.3 What to Look For.

  4. How much tread depth do you need in every major groove on a front tire?

    • A1/32 inch
    • B2/32 inch
    • C4/32 inch
    • D6/32 inch

    Answer: C. 4/32 inch

    Front tires need at least 4/32 inch of tread in each major groove. The lower 2/32 inch minimum is for the other tires.

    Manual section 2.1.3 What to Look For.

  5. You test hydraulic brakes for leaks by pumping the pedal three times, then holding firm pressure for five seconds. What result shows no problem?

    • AThe pedal slowly sinks toward the floor
    • BThe pedal pushes back up against your foot
    • CThe brake warning light comes on
    • DThe pedal does not move

    Answer: D. The pedal does not move

    A pedal that stays put under steady pressure passes the test. If it moves, there may be a leak, so get it fixed before driving.

    Manual section 2.1.5 Seven-step Inspection Method.

  6. What share of missing leaves in a leaf spring will put a vehicle out of service?

    • AOne-tenth or more
    • BOne-fourth or more
    • COne-half or more
    • DAll of them

    Answer: B. One-fourth or more

    Losing a quarter or more of the leaves puts the vehicle out of service, although any leaf spring defect can be dangerous.

    Manual section 2.1.3 What to Look For.

  7. After you start the engine, how soon should oil pressure come up to normal?

    • AWithin seconds
    • BWithin 3 minutes
    • CAfter 10 minutes of idling
    • DOnly after the vehicle starts moving

    Answer: A. Within seconds

    Oil pressure should reach its normal range a few seconds after start-up. If it does not, something is wrong.

    Manual section 2.1.5 Seven-step Inspection Method.

  8. How do you check that the parking brake holds?

    • ASet it, put the vehicle in a low gear and gently pull forward against it
    • BPump the brake pedal three times and hold it
    • CDrive at about 5 mph and push the brake pedal firmly
    • DRelease it on a downhill slope and see how far you roll

    Answer: A. Set it, put the vehicle in a low gear and gently pull forward against it

    With the parking brake set and a low gear selected, a gentle pull forward shows whether the brake keeps the vehicle from moving.

    Manual section 2.1.5 Seven-step Inspection Method.

  9. During an inspection you see rust around the wheel nuts. What might this mean?

    • AThe nuts may be loose
    • BThe tire is underinflated
    • CThe brakes are overheating
    • DThe wheel bearing needs oil

    Answer: A. The nuts may be loose

    Rust streaks around wheel nuts can be a sign of looseness, so check how tight they are.

    Manual section 2.1.3 What to Look For.

  10. Steering wheel play of more than how much can make a vehicle hard to steer?

    • A5 degrees
    • B10 degrees
    • C20 degrees
    • D45 degrees

    Answer: B. 10 degrees

    More than 10 degrees of free play, about 2 inches at the rim of a 20-inch wheel, is a steering defect to look for.

    Manual section 2.1.3 What to Look For.

Basic control and shifting (8 questions)

  1. Why would you select a low range on an automatic transmission?

    • ATo save fuel on the highway
    • BTo make the transmission shift up sooner
    • CTo make starting on ice easier
    • DTo get more engine braking on downgrades

    Answer: D. To get more engine braking on downgrades

    A low range keeps the transmission from shifting up, which gives you more engine braking on the way down a grade.

    Manual section 2.3.3 Automatic Transmissions.

  2. When you must back and turn, which way does the manual recommend turning?

    • AToward the driver's side
    • BToward the right side
    • CWhichever side has less traffic
    • DNever turn; only back in a straight line

    Answer: A. Toward the driver's side

    Backing toward the driver's side lets you watch the rear of the vehicle through the side window. Backing toward the right is much harder to see.

    Manual section 2.2.4 Backing Safely.

  3. Which gear should you use when backing?

    • AThe highest reverse gear
    • BNeutral, letting the vehicle roll
    • CWhichever reverse gear keeps the engine quiet
    • DThe lowest reverse gear

    Answer: D. The lowest reverse gear

    Backing slowly in the lowest reverse gear makes steering errors easier to correct and lets you stop quickly.

    Manual section 2.2.4 Backing Safely.

  4. When should you downshift for a curve?

    • ABefore entering the curve
    • BIn the middle of the curve
    • CAs you leave the curve
    • DOnly when the curve goes downhill

    Answer: A. Before entering the curve

    Slowing and choosing the right gear before the curve lets you use some power through it, which keeps the vehicle more stable.

    Manual section 2.3.1 Manual Transmissions.

  5. Before starting down a hill, which gear should you usually be in?

    • AThe highest gear the engine can pull
    • BNeutral, to let the vehicle coast
    • COne gear higher than you would need to climb the hill
    • DA gear lower than you would need to climb the same hill

    Answer: D. A gear lower than you would need to climb the same hill

    Shift down before the downgrade, usually below the climbing gear, so you can control speed without hard braking.

    Manual section 2.3.1 Manual Transmissions.

  6. When should you turn the retarder off?

    • AWhenever the road is wet, icy, or snow covered
    • BWhen going down a long grade
    • CWhen the vehicle is fully loaded
    • DWhenever you drive faster than 40 mph

    Answer: A. Whenever the road is wet, icy, or snow covered

    On a slippery road a retarder can make the drive wheels skid, so switch it off in rain, ice or snow.

    Manual section 2.3.4 Retarders.

  7. When a retarder is on, which wheels does it slow?

    • AAll wheels
    • BThe steering wheels only
    • CThe trailer wheels only
    • DThe drive wheels only

    Answer: D. The drive wheels only

    A retarder brakes only the drive wheels, and it works whenever you fully release the accelerator.

    Manual section 2.3.4 Retarders.

  8. While speeding up on a snowy road, your drive wheels begin to spin. What should you do?

    • ATake your foot off the accelerator
    • BPress the accelerator harder
    • CBrake firmly
    • DShift quickly to a higher gear

    Answer: A. Take your foot off the accelerator

    Spinning drive wheels mean too much power for the traction you have. Easing off the accelerator lets them grip again.

    Manual section 2.2.1 Accelerating.

Seeing and communicating (11 questions)

  1. When turning, when should you cancel your turn signal?

    • ANot until you have completed the turn
    • BAs soon as you start the turn
    • CHalfway through the turn
    • DBefore you enter the intersection

    Answer: A. Not until you have completed the turn

    Keep signaling through the whole turn so others know what you are doing, then turn it off once the turn is done.

    Manual section 2.5.1 Signal Your Intentions.

  2. How should you hold reflective triangles while setting them out?

    • ABetween yourself and oncoming traffic
    • BBehind your back
    • CAbove your head
    • DOn the side away from traffic

    Answer: A. Between yourself and oncoming traffic

    Carrying the triangles on the traffic side helps approaching drivers see you while you walk.

    Manual section 2.5.2 Communicating Your Presence.

  3. Compared with looking at them directly, how do things look in a convex (curved) mirror?

    • ALarger and closer than they really are
    • BExactly the same size and distance
    • CSmaller and farther away than they really are
    • DLarger and farther away than they really are

    Answer: C. Smaller and farther away than they really are

    Convex mirrors show a wider area, but objects in them look smaller and more distant, so allow for that.

    Manual section 2.4.2 Seeing to the Sides and Rear.

  4. A driver behind you wants to pass. What does the manual say about signaling to them that it is safe to pass?

    • ADo it only on straight roads
    • BYou should not do it
    • CDo it with your right turn signal
    • DDo it only in daylight

    Answer: B. You should not do it

    Waving other drivers on could cause a crash, and you could be blamed for it. Let them decide for themselves.

    Manual section 2.5.1 Signal Your Intentions.

  5. During a lane change, why check your mirrors again after you have signaled?

    • ATo check that your turn signal is flashing
    • BTo see whether the vehicle ahead is braking
    • CTo check that your cargo is secure
    • DTo check that no one has moved into your blind spot

    Answer: D. To check that no one has moved into your blind spot

    Someone may move into your blind spot after you signal, so check again before moving over.

    Manual section 2.4.2 Seeing to the Sides and Rear.

  6. At dawn, at dusk or in rain, which lights help other drivers see you?

    • AIdentification and clearance lights only
    • BHeadlights on low beam
    • CHeadlights on high beam
    • DFour-way flashers only

    Answer: B. Headlights on low beam

    When visibility is poor, turn on the headlights themselves and keep them on low beam, since high beams bother other drivers.

    Manual section 2.5.2 Communicating Your Presence.

  7. On the open highway, looking 12 to 15 seconds ahead means looking about how far ahead?

    • AAbout one block
    • BAbout a quarter of a mile
    • CAbout 100 feet
    • DAbout two miles

    Answer: B. About a quarter of a mile

    At highway speed, 12 to 15 seconds of travel covers roughly a quarter mile. At lower speeds it is about one block.

    Manual section 2.4.1 Seeing Ahead.

  8. When can mirror adjustment be checked accurately?

    • AWhen the trailer or trailers are straight
    • BWhile turning
    • CWhile backing
    • DOnly at night

    Answer: A. When the trailer or trailers are straight

    Check mirror adjustment before each trip with the trailer straight behind you, otherwise the view is misleading.

    Manual section 2.4.2 Seeing to the Sides and Rear.

  9. You are about to pass a bicyclist at night. When it is legal, how can you let them know you are there?

    • ASound the horn for several seconds
    • BTurn on your four-way flashers
    • CFlash your lights from low to high beam and back
    • DPull alongside and slow down

    Answer: C. Flash your lights from low to high beam and back

    Assume the person does not see you. A quick change from low to high beam and back can alert them at night.

    Manual section 2.5.2 Communicating Your Presence.

  10. If you stop on the shoulder of a road, how soon must you put out your emergency warning devices?

    • AWithin 30 minutes
    • BWithin one hour
    • CWithin ten minutes
    • DOnly if it is dark

    Answer: C. Within ten minutes

    Warning devices must be set out within 10 minutes of stopping on a road or shoulder, day or night.

    Manual section 2.5.2 Communicating Your Presence.

  11. You stop on a two-lane road with traffic in both directions. Where do the warning devices go?

    • A10 feet, 100 feet and 200 feet behind the vehicle
    • B50 feet behind and 50 feet ahead
    • COne device, 500 feet behind the vehicle
    • DWithin 10 feet of a front or rear corner, plus 100 feet behind and 100 feet ahead

    Answer: D. Within 10 feet of a front or rear corner, plus 100 feet behind and 100 feet ahead

    On an undivided road traffic comes from both sides, so one device marks the vehicle and the others go 100 feet away in each direction.

    Manual section 2.5.2 Communicating Your Presence.

Speed and space management (18 questions)

  1. The temperature is below freezing and the road ahead looks wet. What should you watch out for?

    • ABleeding tar
    • BBlack ice
    • CFresh oil from the first rain
    • DLoose gravel

    Answer: B. Black ice

    Black ice is thin and clear, so the road just looks wet. Below freezing, a wet-looking road is a warning sign.

    Manual section 2.6.2 Matching Speed to the Road Surface.

  2. As the temperature drops, which usually freezes first?

    • ABridges
    • BThe road surface next to them
    • CBoth at exactly the same time
    • DNeither, until it is well below zero

    Answer: A. Bridges

    Bridges ice over before the rest of the road, so take extra care near 32 degrees Fahrenheit.

    Manual section 2.6.2 Matching Speed to the Road Surface.

  3. What have tests shown about trucks with a high center of gravity on curves?

    • AThey can roll over at the posted speed limit for the curve
    • BThey are safe at any speed below the posted limit
    • CThey roll over only on wet roads
    • DThey are less likely to roll over than cars

    Answer: A. They can roll over at the posted speed limit for the curve

    A posted curve speed is not always safe for a top-heavy truck, so slow down before the curve.

    Manual section 2.6.3 Speed and Curves.

  4. You fit under a bridge with a loaded van. Later the van is empty. What should you know?

    • AThe empty van sits lower, so it will fit
    • BThe empty van sits higher, so it may not fit
    • CLoad does not change a van's height
    • DYou can always rely on the earlier trip

    Answer: B. The empty van sits higher, so it may not fit

    Removing the load lets the van ride higher, so clearing a bridge loaded does not prove you can clear it empty.

    Manual section 2.7.4 Space Overhead.

  5. You drive a 50-foot vehicle at 30 mph. How many seconds of following distance does the manual's rule call for?

    • A3 seconds
    • B5 seconds
    • C6 seconds
    • D10 seconds

    Answer: B. 5 seconds

    Below 40 mph the rule is one second for every 10 feet of vehicle length, so a 50-foot vehicle needs 5 seconds.

    Manual section 2.7.1 Space Ahead.

  6. Under the manual's following-distance rule, what changes when you drive faster than 40 mph?

    • ADouble the following distance
    • BSubtract 1 second
    • CNothing changes
    • DAdd 1 second

    Answer: D. Add 1 second

    Above 40 mph, take the one-second-per-10-feet figure and add one more second.

    Manual section 2.7.1 Space Ahead.

  7. In heavy traffic, what is the safest speed?

    • AAbout 10 mph below the speed of traffic
    • BThe speed of other vehicles
    • CAs fast as needed to pass slower vehicles
    • DFaster than traffic, to stay clear of other vehicles

    Answer: B. The speed of other vehicles

    Vehicles moving in the same direction at the same speed rarely hit each other, as long as that speed is legal and safe.

    Manual section 2.6.5 Speed and Traffic Flow.

  8. Your vehicle starts to hydroplane. How can you regain control?

    • ABrake hard
    • BSpeed up to get through the water
    • CRelease the accelerator and push in the clutch
    • DTurn the steering wheel sharply

    Answer: C. Release the accelerator and push in the clutch

    Easing off the accelerator and pushing in the clutch slows the vehicle and lets the wheels turn freely. Do not brake.

    Manual section 2.6.2 Matching Speed to the Road Surface.

  9. If there is a lot of water on the road, hydroplaning can happen at speeds as low as:

    • A10 mph
    • B30 mph
    • C50 mph
    • D70 mph

    Answer: B. 30 mph

    With enough standing water, tires can lose contact with the road even at about 30 mph, especially with low pressure or worn tread.

    Manual section 2.6.2 Matching Speed to the Road Surface.

  10. Why shouldn't you trust the height posted at a bridge or overpass?

    • APosted heights are always measured in meters
    • BPosted heights include a 2-foot safety margin
    • CRe-paving or packed snow may have reduced the clearance
    • DHeights are posted only for cars

    Answer: C. Re-paving or packed snow may have reduced the clearance

    The road surface can rise after a sign is posted, for example from new pavement or packed snow, so real clearance may be lower.

    Manual section 2.7.4 Space Overhead.

  11. When is a road especially slippery during rain?

    • AAfter several hours of steady rain
    • BOnly at night
    • COnly on bridges
    • DRight after the rain begins

    Answer: D. Right after the rain begins

    Fresh rain mixes with oil left on the pavement, which makes the surface very slippery until the rain washes it away.

    Manual section 2.6.2 Matching Speed to the Road Surface.

  12. Your vehicle cannot make a right turn without swinging into another lane. What should you do?

    • ASwing wide to the left before starting the turn
    • BBack up to make more room
    • CStart the turn from the left lane
    • DTurn wide as you complete the turn, keeping the rear close to the curb

    Answer: D. Turn wide as you complete the turn, keeping the rear close to the curb

    Keeping the rear near the curb and going wide at the end stops other drivers from passing on your right.

    Manual section 2.7.6 Space for Turns.

  13. In ideal conditions at 55 mph, what is the minimum total stopping distance, counting perception, reaction and braking distance?

    • A142 feet
    • B216 feet
    • C419 feet
    • D1,000 feet

    Answer: C. 419 feet

    Perception, reaction and braking distance together add up to at least 419 feet at 55 mph on dry pavement.

    Manual section 2.6.1 Stopping Distance.

  14. Someone is tailgating you. What does the manual advise about your speed?

    • ADon't speed up; being tailgated is safer at a low speed
    • BSpeed up to get away from them
    • CFlash your brake lights to warn them
    • DBrake suddenly so they back off

    Answer: A. Don't speed up; being tailgated is safer at a low speed

    Speeding up only makes a crash worse. Keep a low, steady speed and give yourself more room ahead.

    Manual section 2.7.2 Space Behind.

  15. Compared with 20 mph, how much greater are impact and braking distance at 60 mph?

    • A3 times
    • B6 times
    • C9 times
    • D12 times

    Answer: C. 9 times

    Tripling your speed makes both the striking power and the braking distance nine times greater.

    Manual section 2.6.1 Stopping Distance.

  16. There are two left-turn lanes. Which one should you use?

    • AThe right-hand turn lane
    • BThe left-hand (inside) turn lane
    • CWhichever has less traffic
    • DEither, as long as you signal early

    Answer: A. The right-hand turn lane

    Starting from the inside lane may force you to swing right during the turn. The right-hand lane avoids that.

    Manual section 2.7.6 Space for Turns.

  17. On a wet road, by about how much should you reduce your speed?

    • AAbout one-tenth
    • BAbout one-half or more
    • CTo a crawl
    • DAbout one-third

    Answer: D. About one-third

    Slow by roughly a third on wet pavement, for example from 55 to about 35 mph. Packed snow and ice call for even more.

    Manual section 2.6.2 Matching Speed to the Road Surface.

  18. What is the number one cause of injury and death in roadway work zones?

    • APoor lighting
    • BUneven road surfaces
    • CSpeeding traffic
    • DConstruction equipment

    Answer: C. Speeding traffic

    Speed is the leading danger in work zones. Obey posted limits and slow down more when workers are near the road.

    Manual section 2.6.7 Roadway Work Zones.

Hazards and distracted driving (9 questions)

  1. Compared with hand-held phones, how likely are hands-free devices to distract you?

    • AThey never distract you
    • BThey distract you only in heavy traffic
    • CThey are half as likely to distract you
    • DThey are no less likely to distract you

    Answer: D. They are no less likely to distract you

    Your attention leaves the road with either kind of phone, so hands-free does not remove the distraction.

    Manual section 2.9.4 Cell/Mobile Phones.

  2. Which of these is listed as a clue that a driver may be impaired?

    • ADriving exactly at the speed limit
    • BSignaling well before turning
    • CKeeping a long following distance
    • DDriving with an open window in cold weather

    Answer: D. Driving with an open window in cold weather

    Among the signs of a sleepy, drunk, drugged or ill driver are weaving, stopping at the wrong time, and a window left open in the cold.

    Manual section 2.8.3 Drivers Who Are Hazards.

  3. A CDL holder is convicted a second time within 3 years for using a hand-held phone while driving a CMV. How long is the disqualification?

    • A30 days
    • B60 days
    • C120 days
    • D1 year

    Answer: B. 60 days

    A second conviction within 3 years brings a 60-day disqualification. Three or more within 3 years bring 120 days.

    Manual section 2.9.4 Cell/Mobile Phones.

  4. What does the manual say about speed limits posted on off ramps and on ramps?

    • AThey are set for trucks, so they are always safe
    • BThey may be safe for cars but not for large or heavily loaded vehicles
    • CThey apply only to cars
    • DThey can be exceeded by 5 mph in dry weather

    Answer: B. They may be safe for cars but not for large or heavily loaded vehicles

    Ramp speed signs are often set with cars in mind. A big or heavy vehicle may need to go slower, especially on downhill curves.

    Manual section 2.8.2 Hazardous Roads.

  5. What does the slow moving vehicle symbol look like?

    • AA yellow diamond with a black arrow
    • BA white rectangle with black letters
    • CA red triangle with an orange center
    • DA round black-on-yellow sign

    Answer: C. A red triangle with an orange center

    Farm, construction and other slow machines may carry this red-bordered orange triangle. Seeing it early helps avoid a crash.

    Manual section 2.8.3 Drivers Who Are Hazards.

  6. Why should you be careful near a stopped bus?

    • ABuses always pull out without signaling
    • BPassengers may cross in front of or behind it and often can't see you
    • CStopped buses may roll backward
    • DBus drivers often open the doors into traffic

    Answer: B. Passengers may cross in front of or behind it and often can't see you

    People getting off a bus may walk around it into your path without seeing your vehicle.

    Manual section 2.8.3 Drivers Who Are Hazards.

  7. Under the federal rule for CMV drivers, which of these counts as texting?

    • ATalking to a passenger
    • BListening to the radio
    • CReading a text message on a phone
    • DChecking the mirrors

    Answer: C. Reading a text message on a phone

    Texting covers typing text into an electronic device and also reading text from one.

    Manual section 2.9.5 Texting.

  8. How much greater are the odds of a safety-critical event for CMV drivers who text while driving than for those who don't?

    • A2 times
    • B6 times
    • C23.2 times
    • D100 times

    Answer: C. 23.2 times

    Research cited in the manual puts the risk at 23.2 times greater for texting drivers. Dialing a phone raises it 6 times.

    Manual section 2.9.5 Texting.

  9. Which are clues that a driver may be a tourist who is unfamiliar with the area?

    • ACar-top luggage and out-of-state license plates
    • BA clean car and new tires
    • CA long following distance
    • DHeadlights on during the day

    Answer: A. Car-top luggage and out-of-state license plates

    Visitors who do not know the roads may turn or stop suddenly. Luggage on the roof and plates from another state can give them away.

    Manual section 2.8.3 Drivers Who Are Hazards.

Night, fog and weather (12 questions)

  1. In very hot weather, why watch for spots where tar rises to the road surface?

    • AThey are very slippery
    • BThey can puncture tires
    • CThey show the road is about to crack
    • DThey mark where to stop

    Answer: A. They are very slippery

    Tar that bleeds up through hot pavement makes a slick surface.

    Manual section 2.14.2 Driving.

  2. On long trips, how often does the manual suggest taking a break?

    • AAbout every 300 miles or 5 hours
    • BAbout every 100 miles or 2 hours
    • COnly when you start to feel sleepy
    • DOnce per day

    Answer: B. About every 100 miles or 2 hours

    Regular breaks on long trips, roughly every 2 hours or 100 miles, help you stay alert.

    Manual section 2.11.2 Driver Factors.

  3. Within what distance of an oncoming vehicle should you dim your headlights?

    • A100 feet
    • B250 feet
    • C500 feet
    • D1,000 feet

    Answer: C. 500 feet

    Switch to low beams when an oncoming vehicle is within 500 feet, and also when you follow another vehicle that closely.

    Manual section 2.11.5 Night Driving Procedures.

  4. You notice signs of fatigue while driving. What does the manual tell you to do?

    • AStop driving and sleep for the night or take a short nap
    • BOpen the window and keep going
    • CDrink coffee and push on
    • DDrive faster to finish sooner

    Answer: A. Stop driving and sleep for the night or take a short nap

    Pushing on when tired is a major cause of fatal crashes. Sleep, or at least a 15 to 20 minute nap, is the answer.

    Manual section 2.11.2 Driver Factors.

  5. What is the best advice for driving in fog?

    • AUse high beams to see farther
    • BSpeed up to get through it quickly
    • CFollow the taillights ahead closely
    • DDon't; wait at a rest area or truck stop until visibility improves

    Answer: D. Don't; wait at a rest area or truck stop until visibility improves

    If you can, pull off the road and wait. If you must drive, slow down before entering the fog.

    Manual section 2.12 Driving in Fog.

  6. If you must drive in fog, which lights give the best visibility?

    • ALow-beam headlights and fog lights
    • BHigh-beam headlights
    • CParking lights only
    • DNo lights during daytime

    Answer: A. Low-beam headlights and fog lights

    Low beams and fog lights work best in fog, even in daytime. Turning on the 4-way flashers also helps drivers behind you.

    Manual section 2.12 Driving in Fog.

  7. In very hot weather, how often should you inspect your tires?

    • AOnce a day
    • BEvery 30 minutes
    • CEvery two hours or every 100 miles
    • DOnly before the trip

    Answer: C. Every two hours or every 100 miles

    Heat raises tire pressure and temperature, so check the tires every 2 hours or 100 miles.

    Manual section 2.14.1 Vehicle Checks.

  8. About how far ahead can you see with low beams?

    • A100 feet
    • B250 feet
    • C500 feet
    • D1,000 feet

    Answer: B. 250 feet

    Low beams light up roughly 250 feet of road and high beams about 350 to 500 feet, so slow down enough to stop within that range.

    Manual section 2.11.4 Vehicle Factors.

  9. In winter, you notice there is no spray coming off other vehicles' tires. What does this tell you?

    • AThe road is dry and safe
    • BIce has formed on the road
    • COther drivers have chains on
    • DThe temperature is rising

    Answer: B. Ice has formed on the road

    When tires stop throwing up spray on a winter road, the water has likely frozen.

    Manual section 2.13.2 Driving.

  10. The engine has cooled and your hands are protected. How do you start removing the radiator cap to add coolant?

    • ARemove it quickly in one turn
    • BTap it with a tool until it loosens
    • CPush down hard and turn it all the way at once
    • DTurn it slowly to the first stop to release the pressure seal

    Answer: D. Turn it slowly to the first stop to release the pressure seal

    Turning to the first stop lets the pressure escape while you step back. Only then press down and remove the cap.

    Manual section 2.14.1 Vehicle Checks.

  11. What can happen if the radiator shutters freeze shut or the winterfront is closed too much?

    • AThe heater will blow cold air
    • BThe engine may overheat and stop
    • CThe brakes will lock
    • DThe fuel will gel

    Answer: B. The engine may overheat and stop

    Blocking too much airflow to the radiator can make the engine overheat, even in winter.

    Manual section 2.13.1 Vehicle Checks.

  12. Why should you avoid dark or tinted corrective lenses at night?

    • AThey make headlights look brighter
    • BThey are illegal in every state
    • CThey cut down the light you need to see clearly
    • DThey fog up in cold weather

    Answer: C. They cut down the light you need to see clearly

    Even if they seem to reduce glare, tinted lenses block light that you need for night driving.

    Manual section 2.11.2 Driver Factors.

Railroad crossings and mountain driving (9 questions)

  1. On a long downgrade, why do brakes that are out of adjustment matter?

    • AThey stop doing their share first, so the other brakes can overheat and fade
    • BThey do more than their share of the work
    • CThey only affect parking
    • DThey make the engine braking weaker

    Answer: A. They stop doing their share first, so the other brakes can overheat and fade

    Every brake must carry its part of the load. Poorly adjusted brakes leave the work to the others, which then overheat.

    Manual section 2.16.3 Brake Fading or Failure.

  2. What happens when brakes begin to fade from overheating?

    • AThey grab harder than normal
    • BThey work better once they are hot
    • CYou must press them harder and harder for the same stopping power
    • DThey stop the vehicle with less pedal pressure

    Answer: C. You must press them harder and harder for the same stopping power

    Hot brakes lose effectiveness. If you keep using them hard, they can fade until you cannot slow down at all.

    Manual section 2.16 Mountain Driving.

  3. When is the braking effect of the engine greatest?

    • AAt idle in a high gear
    • BIn neutral
    • CAt low rpm in the top gear
    • DNear the governed rpm with the transmission in a lower gear

    Answer: D. Near the governed rpm with the transmission in a lower gear

    Engine braking is your main speed control on downgrades, and it works best at high rpm in a low gear.

    Manual section 2.16.1 Select a "Safe" Speed.

  4. What does the advance warning sign before a public railroad crossing look like?

    • ADiamond-shaped, black on orange
    • BEight-sided, white on red
    • CTriangular, red and white
    • DRound, black on yellow

    Answer: D. Round, black on yellow

    The round yellow sign with black markings tells you to slow down, look and listen, and be ready to stop.

    Manual section 2.15.2 Warning Signs and Devices.

  5. At a crossing with two tracks, a train has just passed. What should you keep in mind?

    • ATrains never use both tracks at once
    • BA train on one track may hide a train on the other
    • CThe gates always stay down for a second train
    • DYou may cross as soon as the first train clears

    Answer: B. A train on one track may hide a train on the other

    One train can block your view of a second one, so make sure no other train is near before you start across.

    Manual section 2.15.3 Driving Procedures.

  6. What should you avoid doing while crossing railroad tracks?

    • ALooking both ways
    • BKeeping a steady speed
    • CShifting gears
    • DKeeping both hands on the wheel

    Answer: C. Shifting gears

    Stay in one gear on the tracks. Make sure before you start that you can get all the way across.

    Manual section 2.15.5 Crossing the Tracks.

  7. There is no white stop line at a railroad crossing. A vehicle that must stop there should stop how far from the nearest rail?

    • A5 to 10 feet
    • B15 to 50 feet
    • C50 to 100 feet
    • DAt least 100 feet

    Answer: B. 15 to 50 feet

    Without a painted stop line, stop at least 15 feet but no more than 50 feet from the nearest rail.

    Manual section 2.15.2 Warning Signs and Devices.

  8. Your vehicle gets stuck on railroad tracks. What should you do first?

    • AGet out of the vehicle and away from the tracks
    • BStay in the cab and keep trying to move
    • CSet out triangles on the tracks
    • DWait in the cab for the train crew

    Answer: A. Get out of the vehicle and away from the tracks

    Get yourself to safety first. Then use the emergency number posted at the crossing and give its location.

    Manual section 2.15.6 Special Situations.

  9. On a long downgrade your safe speed is 45 mph. Using the manual's braking technique, at about what speed do you release the brakes?

    • A30 mph
    • B35 mph
    • C40 mph
    • D45 mph

    Answer: C. 40 mph

    Brake firmly when you reach the safe speed, release at about 5 mph below it, then repeat when you are back at the safe speed.

    Manual section 2.16.4 Proper Braking Technique.

Emergencies, ABS and skids (12 questions)

  1. Your ABS stops working. What does that mean for braking?

    • AYou still have regular brakes; drive normally and get it serviced soon
    • BYou have no brakes and must stop at once
    • CYou must brake only with the parking brake
    • DYou must pump the brakes at all times

    Answer: A. You still have regular brakes; drive normally and get it serviced soon

    ABS is added to the normal brakes. If it fails, the normal brakes still work, but have the system repaired soon.

    Manual section 2.18.7 Braking If ABS Is Not Working.

  2. Truck tractors with air brakes must have ABS if they were built on or after:

    • AMarch 1, 1997
    • BMarch 1, 1998
    • CMarch 1, 1999
    • DMarch 1, 2001

    Answer: A. March 1, 1997

    The requirement started with air-braked truck tractors in 1997, other air-braked vehicles in 1998, and heavier hydraulic-braked trucks and buses in 1999.

    Manual section 2.18.2 Vehicles Required to Have Antilock Braking Systems.

  3. What is controlled braking?

    • ABraking fully and releasing when the wheels lock
    • BBraking as hard as you can without locking the wheels
    • CPumping the pedal as fast as possible
    • DUsing only the parking brake

    Answer: B. Braking as hard as you can without locking the wheels

    Controlled braking keeps the wheels rolling while you brake hard, with only small steering moves.

    Manual section 2.17.2 How to Stop Quickly and Safely.

  4. What is the first step to correct a drive-wheel braking skid?

    • ABrake harder
    • BAccelerate
    • CStop braking
    • DShift to a lower gear

    Answer: C. Stop braking

    Releasing the brakes lets the rear wheels roll again so they stop sliding. Then counter-steer as the vehicle straightens.

    Manual section 2.19.2 Correcting a Drive-wheel Braking Skid.

  5. In a front-wheel skid, what is the only way to stop the skid?

    • ATurn the steering wheel more sharply
    • BAccelerate
    • CPump the brakes hard
    • DLet the vehicle slow down

    Answer: D. Let the vehicle slow down

    In a front-wheel skid the vehicle keeps going straight however you steer. Ease off the turning and braking and let it slow.

    Manual section 2.19.3 Front-wheel Skids.

  6. If your steering suddenly feels heavy, what is it probably a sign of?

    • AA failed rear tire
    • BWet brakes
    • CA retarder that is switched on
    • DA failed front tire

    Answer: D. A failed front tire

    Heavy steering usually points to a front tire failure. A rear tire failure may cause fishtailing instead.

    Manual section 2.17.4 Tire Failure.

  7. In an emergency you drive off the road onto the shoulder. When should you start braking?

    • ARight away and as hard as possible
    • BOnly after you are back on the pavement
    • CWhen your speed has dropped to about 50 mph
    • DWhen your speed has dropped to about 20 mph, then gently

    Answer: D. When your speed has dropped to about 20 mph, then gently

    Braking hard on a loose surface can cause a skid. Wait until you have slowed to about 20 mph, then brake very gently.

    Manual section 2.17.1 Steering to Avoid a Crash.

  8. An oncoming driver has drifted into your lane. Which way is usually best to move?

    • ATo your left, into the other lane
    • BStraight ahead while braking hard
    • CTo your right
    • DStop in your lane and sound the horn

    Answer: C. To your right

    If the other driver notices the mistake, they will likely return to their own lane, so moving right avoids meeting them there.

    Manual section 2.17.1 Steering to Avoid a Crash.

  9. You must return to the road from the shoulder before stopping. When do you counter-steer?

    • AWhen both front tires are on the paved surface
    • BBefore the front tires leave the shoulder
    • CWhen only the rear tires are on the pavement
    • DAfter driving 100 feet on the road

    Answer: A. When both front tires are on the paved surface

    Turn back sharply onto the road, then counter-steer as soon as both front tires are on the pavement, as one motion.

    Manual section 2.17.1 Steering to Avoid a Crash.

  10. What causes most serious skids?

    • AWorn brake linings
    • BDriving too fast for road conditions
    • CStrong crosswinds
    • DUnderinflated tires

    Answer: B. Driving too fast for road conditions

    Most bad skids come from too much speed for the conditions. Adjusting speed avoids over-braking and over-steering.

    Manual section 2.19 Skid Control and Recovery.

  11. With stab braking, how long can it take for the wheels to start rolling after you release the brakes?

    • AUp to five seconds
    • BUp to ten seconds
    • CUp to one second
    • DThey start rolling instantly

    Answer: C. Up to one second

    Wait for the wheels to roll again before reapplying the brakes, or the vehicle will not straighten out.

    Manual section 2.17.2 How to Stop Quickly and Safely.

  12. Compared with stopping, how quickly can you usually turn to miss an obstacle?

    • AAlways more slowly
    • BAlmost always more quickly
    • CAbout the same
    • DOnly more quickly when empty

    Answer: B. Almost always more quickly

    When there is not enough room to stop, steering around the obstacle is often faster, though top-heavy vehicles may tip.

    Manual section 2.17.1 Steering to Avoid a Crash.

Accidents, fires and alcohol (14 questions)

  1. What is the first thing to do at an accident scene?

    • AKeep another accident from happening in the same spot
    • BMove the injured into your vehicle
    • CTake photos of the damage
    • DExchange insurance details

    Answer: A. Keep another accident from happening in the same spot

    Protecting the area comes first: move your vehicle aside if you can, turn on flashers and set out triangles.

    Manual section 2.20.1 Protect the Area.

  2. As blood alcohol builds up, which part of the brain is affected first?

    • AThe part that controls breathing
    • BThe part that controls judgment and self-control
    • CThe part that controls hearing
    • DThe part that controls long-term memory

    Answer: B. The part that controls judgment and self-control

    Judgment and self-control go first, which is why drinkers often cannot tell they are getting drunk.

    Manual section 2.22.1 Alcohol and Driving.

  3. There is a cargo fire in a van trailer. What should you do with the doors?

    • AKeep them shut
    • BOpen them to let the smoke out
    • COpen them to reach the fire
    • DOpen only the side door

    Answer: A. Keep them shut

    Opening the doors feeds the fire with oxygen and can make it burn very fast, especially with hazardous materials.

    Manual section 2.21.3 Fire Fighting.

  4. You have an engine fire. What does the manual say about the hood?

    • AOpen it fully to reach the fire
    • BOpen it a little to let smoke out
    • CRemove it before using the extinguisher
    • DDon't open it if you can avoid it

    Answer: D. Don't open it if you can avoid it

    Turn the engine off and keep the hood closed if possible. Spray through the louvers, radiator or from underneath.

    Manual section 2.21.3 Fire Fighting.

  5. Where should you aim a fire extinguisher?

    • AUp into the flames
    • BAt the source or base of the fire
    • CAt the smoke
    • DAbove the fire

    Answer: B. At the source or base of the fire

    Spray the base of the fire, staying as far back as you can and upwind.

    Manual section 2.21.3 Fire Fighting.

  6. Your vehicle is on fire. Where should you stop?

    • AAt the nearest service station
    • BNext to a building, so help is close
    • CIn the travel lane, so others can see you
    • DIn an open area away from buildings, trees, brush and other vehicles

    Answer: D. In an open area away from buildings, trees, brush and other vehicles

    Get off the road in an open spot where the fire cannot spread. Never pull into a service station.

    Manual section 2.21.3 Fire Fighting.

  7. When do you need the hazardous materials endorsement to drive a vehicle carrying hazardous materials?

    • AWhenever any hazardous material is on board
    • BOnly for loads over 10,000 pounds
    • COnly on interstate trips
    • DWhen the vehicle requires placards

    Answer: D. When the vehicle requires placards

    You may drive a vehicle with hazardous materials that does not need placards. If it needs placards, you need the endorsement.

    Manual section 2.23.3 Lists of Regulated Products.

  8. How does the manual say to stop heavy bleeding?

    • ARaise the person's feet
    • BApply direct pressure to the wound
    • CPour cold water on the wound
    • DMove the person to a warm place

    Answer: B. Apply direct pressure to the wound

    Press directly on the wound to control heavy bleeding, and keep the injured person warm.

    Manual section 2.20.3 Care for the Injured.

  9. When should you move a severely injured person?

    • AAlways, to get them off the road
    • BWhenever they ask to be moved
    • COnly if fire or passing traffic makes it necessary
    • DOnly after giving them water

    Answer: C. Only if fire or passing traffic makes it necessary

    Leave a badly hurt person where they are unless staying put is more dangerous because of fire or traffic.

    Manual section 2.20.3 Care for the Injured.

  10. How many identical placards must a placarded vehicle have?

    • AAt least one
    • BAt least two
    • CAt least four
    • DAt least six

    Answer: C. At least four

    Placards go on the front, the rear and both sides, so others can read them from every direction.

    Manual section 2.23.3 Lists of Regulated Products.

  11. When may a driver on duty use a drug a doctor has prescribed?

    • ANever
    • BOnly on trips under 100 miles
    • CWhen the doctor says it will not affect safe driving ability
    • DWhenever it was bought legally

    Answer: C. When the doctor says it will not affect safe driving ability

    A prescribed drug is allowed if the doctor tells the driver it will not affect safe driving.

    Manual section 2.22.2 Other Drugs.

  12. Which drink contains the same amount of alcohol as a 12-ounce glass of 5% beer?

    • AA 5-ounce glass of 12% wine
    • BA 12-ounce glass of 12% wine
    • CTwo shots of 80 proof liquor
    • DTwo 12-ounce glasses of 5% beer

    Answer: A. A 5-ounce glass of 12% wine

    A 12-ounce 5% beer, a 5-ounce 12% wine and a 1.5-ounce shot of 80 proof liquor all hold the same amount of alcohol.

    Manual section 2.22.1 Alcohol and Driving.

  13. While you are driving, where may hazardous materials shipping papers be kept?

    • AIn a pouch on the driver's door or in clear view within reach
    • BLocked in the cargo area
    • CIn a toolbox under the trailer
    • DAt the motor carrier's office

    Answer: A. In a pouch on the driver's door or in clear view within reach

    Emergency responders need to find these papers quickly. When you leave the vehicle, they go on the driver's seat.

    Manual section 2.23.2 Why Are There Rules?.

  14. Why should you not use water on a gasoline fire?

    • AIt can cause an electric shock
    • BIt works too slowly
    • CIt will spread the flames
    • DIt damages the engine

    Answer: C. It will spread the flames

    Water spreads burning gasoline. On electrical fires water is also wrong, because of the shock risk.

    Manual section 2.21.3 Fire Fighting.

Cargo safety (10 questions)

  1. Under a bridge formula, axles that are closer together are allowed:

    • ALess maximum axle weight
    • BMore maximum axle weight
    • CThe same weight as any other axles
    • DNo weight limit on state roads

    Answer: A. Less maximum axle weight

    Bridge formulas lower the weight allowed on closely spaced axles to protect bridges and roads.

    Manual section 3.2.2 Legal Weight Limits.

  2. Why check your cargo covers in the mirrors while driving?

    • AA flapping cover can tear loose and block your view or someone else's
    • BTo see whether the cover is the right color
    • CBecause covers must be replaced every trip
    • DTo check that the cover is wet

    Answer: A. A flapping cover can tear loose and block your view or someone else's

    A loose cover can uncover the cargo and blow into the view of you or other drivers.

    Manual section 3.3.4 Covering Cargo.

  3. After starting a trip, within how many miles should you inspect the cargo and its securing devices again?

    • A10 miles
    • B25 miles
    • C50 miles
    • D150 miles

    Answer: C. 50 miles

    Re-inspect cargo and tie-downs within the first 50 miles and make any adjustments needed.

    Manual section 3.1 Inspecting Cargo.

  4. What can too little weight on the driving axles cause?

    • AHard steering
    • BBrake fade
    • COverheated tires
    • DPoor traction

    Answer: D. Poor traction

    Light drive axles let the drive wheels spin easily, and in bad weather the truck may not be able to keep going.

    Manual section 3.2.4 Balance the Weight.

  5. What is the Gross Vehicle Weight Rating (GVWR)?

    • AThe weight shown on a scale at the moment of weighing
    • BThe weight carried by one axle
    • CThe most weight a tire can carry
    • DThe loaded weight of a single vehicle as specified by the manufacturer

    Answer: D. The loaded weight of a single vehicle as specified by the manufacturer

    GVWR is the manufacturer's figure for how much one vehicle may weigh when loaded.

    Manual section 3.2.1 Definitions You Should Know.

  6. Why does hanging meat in a refrigerated truck call for extra care on sharp curves and ramps?

    • AIt is heavier than other cargo
    • BIt can spoil if you drive slowly
    • CIt can be a very unstable load with a high center of gravity
    • DIt must be tied down every 5 feet

    Answer: C. It can be a very unstable load with a high center of gravity

    Suspended meat can swing and raise the center of gravity, so go slowly on off ramps, on ramps and sharp curves.

    Manual section 3.4.2 Hanging Meat.

  7. What does a front-end header board protect you from?

    • AWind and rain
    • BYour cargo moving forward in a crash or emergency stop
    • CLow bridges
    • DSun glare

    Answer: B. Your cargo moving forward in a crash or emergency stop

    The header board, or headache rack, should block cargo from sliding forward into the cab.

    Manual section 3.3.3 Header Boards.

  8. How should you load cargo to keep the center of gravity low?

    • APut the heaviest parts on top
    • BPut all the weight over the rear axle
    • CPut the heaviest parts under the lightest parts
    • DStack the cargo as high as possible

    Answer: C. Put the heaviest parts under the lightest parts

    A low center of gravity makes rollover less likely, so heavy items go at the bottom.

    Manual section 3.2.3 Don't Be Top-heavy.

  9. When hauling less than a full load of livestock, what should you use?

    • ATie-downs on each animal
    • BFalse bulkheads to keep the animals bunched together
    • CA tarp over the trailer
    • DExtra weight on the front axle

    Answer: B. False bulkheads to keep the animals bunched together

    Animals that can move around shift the load. Even when bunched, they lean on curves, so take extra care.

    Manual section 3.4.3 Livestock.

  10. Federal rules require the aggregate working load limit of a cargo securement system to be at least what share of the cargo's weight?

    • AOne-quarter
    • BOne-half
    • CEqual to the weight
    • DTwice the weight

    Answer: B. One-half

    All the tie-downs together must be rated for at least half the weight of the article or group of articles they secure.

    Manual section 3.3.2 Cargo Tie-down.
