Danger zones and mirrors (6 questions)

  1. When should you check each mirror to get the maximum viewing area?

    • ABefore you operate the school bus
    • BAfter you finish the first stop of the route
    • COnly when you return to the bus lot
    • DOnly when another driver has used the bus

    Answer: A. Before you operate the school bus

    Every mirror is checked before you drive so you start with the widest possible view of the danger zones; a mirror that is off should be adjusted.

    Manual section 10.1.2 Correct Mirror Adjustment.

  2. What should you keep in mind about the view in the outside convex mirrors?

    • AIt shows people and objects at their exact size and distance
    • BIt does not show the true size and distance of people and objects
    • CIt shows only the area directly in front of the front bumper
    • DIt shows only the passengers seated inside the bus

    Answer: B. It does not show the true size and distance of people and objects

    Convex mirrors give a wide-angle view of the sides, so what you see in them is not a true picture of how big or how far away something is.

    Manual section 10.1.4 Outside Left and Right Side Convex Mirrors.

  3. Which mirrors must you use to watch traffic approaching the large blind spot behind the bus?

    • AThe overhead inside rearview mirror
    • BA mirror mounted on the rear emergency door
    • CThe exterior side mirrors
    • DA small mirror above the service door

    Answer: C. The exterior side mirrors

    The inside rearview mirror is for watching passengers and cannot cover the long blind area behind the bus, so traffic coming into that area is watched with the outside side mirrors.

    Manual section 10.1.6 Overhead Inside Rearview Mirror.

  4. Why is the area to the left of a school bus always considered dangerous?

    • AStudents usually line up on that side
    • BThe service door opens on that side
    • CCars may be passing the bus on that side
    • DThe fuel tank is mounted on that side

    Answer: C. Cars may be passing the bus on that side

    Traffic goes by the bus on its left, so the manual treats that whole area as a constant danger to children near the bus.

    Manual section 10.1.1 Danger Zones.

  5. How should the crossover, convex and flat mirrors be viewed to make sure no child is in a danger zone?

    • AIn a logical sequence, one after another
    • BOnly the crossover mirrors, at each stop
    • COnly the flat mirrors, while the bus moves
    • DIn any order, whenever there is time

    Answer: A. In a logical sequence, one after another

    The three mirror types work together to cover the danger zones, so you look through them in a set order each time so that no child or object is missed.

    Manual section 10.1.5 Outside Left and Right Side Crossover Mirrors.

  6. Depending on the length and width of the bus, how far can the blind spot behind the bus extend?

    • AUp to 30 feet
    • BUp to 1,000 feet
    • CUp to 10 feet
    • DUp to 400 feet

    Answer: D. Up to 400 feet

    The area behind the bus that the outside flat mirrors cannot show is long, and on a long, wide bus it can reach about 400 feet.

    Manual section 10.1.3 Outside Left and Right Side Flat Mirrors.

Loading and unloading (14 questions)

  1. What do you need before you change the location of a school bus stop?

    • ASpoken agreement from the parents at that stop
    • BA vote of the students who use that stop
    • CApproval from another school bus driver
    • DWritten approval from the appropriate district official

    Answer: D. Written approval from the appropriate district official

    School districts set the official routes and stops, so a driver may move a stop only with written permission from the proper district official.

    Manual section 10.2.1 Approaching the Stop.

  2. When students who must cross the road reach the edge of the roadway, what should they check on the bus?

    • AWhether the service door has closed
    • BWhether the red flashing lights are still flashing
    • CWhether the engine is still running
    • DWhether the stop arm has folded back in

    Answer: B. Whether the red flashing lights are still flashing

    At the road edge, students look in all directions, confirm the bus's red flashing lights are still on, and then wait for the driver's signal before crossing.

    Manual section 10.2.3 Unloading Procedures on the Route.

  3. After crossing in front of the bus on your signal, where should students stop and look again for your signal?

    • AAt the center line, before the next lane
    • BAt the right front corner of the bumper
    • CBeside the service door of the bus
    • DAt the left edge of the school bus

    Answer: D. At the left edge of the school bus

    Students pause again when they reach the bus's left edge and wait for a second signal from the driver before crossing the rest of the road.

    Manual section 10.2.3 Unloading Procedures on the Route.

  4. Each year, in which situation are more students killed?

    • ARiding as passengers inside a school bus
    • BGetting on or off a school bus
    • CRiding in the front seats of a school bus
    • DRiding in the rear seats of a school bus

    Answer: B. Getting on or off a school bus

    Loading and unloading is the deadliest part of school bus travel, with more student deaths than among passengers riding inside the bus.

    Manual section 10.2 Loading and Unloading.

  5. What should students be told to do if they drop an object near the bus?

    • ALeave it, move out of the danger zones and get the driver's attention
    • BPick it up quickly, before the bus begins to move away
    • CReach under the bus for it while the door is still open
    • DGo back for it as soon as the bus door has closed

    Answer: A. Leave it, move out of the danger zones and get the driver's attention

    Stopping or turning back for a dropped item can take a student out of the driver's sight at a dangerous moment, so the student should get to a safe spot and signal the driver instead.

    Manual section 10.2.5 Special Dangers of Loading and Unloading.

  6. Before you completely open the door and signal students to approach, what should your final check confirm?

    • AThat the dome light is turned on
    • BThat every student has a seat partner
    • CThat the engine has been turned off
    • DThat all traffic has stopped

    Answer: D. That all traffic has stopped

    Students are called to the bus only after you have made sure that every vehicle around the bus has come to a stop.

    Manual section 10.2.1 Approaching the Stop.

  7. What is the danger known as a handrail hang-up?

    • AA student slipping on a wet step while boarding
    • BA handrail that comes loose from the stepwell
    • CA student blocking the door while others wait
    • DClothing, accessories or body parts caught in the handrail or door

    Answer: D. Clothing, accessories or body parts caught in the handrail or door

    Students getting off the bus can be snagged by the handrail or door, which has caused injuries and deaths, so watch each one reach a safe spot before you move the bus.

    Manual section 10.2.5 Special Dangers of Loading and Unloading.

  8. You realize you have driven past a student's unloading stop. What should you do?

    • ADo not back up; follow local procedures
    • BBack up slowly while using the mirrors
    • CLet the student off at the next corner
    • DBack up only if no traffic is behind you

    Answer: A. Do not back up; follow local procedures

    Backing up to reach a missed stop is not allowed; you handle the missed stop the way your local procedures direct.

    Manual section 10.2.3 Unloading Procedures on the Route.

  9. While loading at a stop, you notice that a student who should be there is missing. What should you do?

    • ALeave right away so the route stays on time
    • BAsk the other students where the student is
    • CHonk the horn and wait at least ten minutes
    • DDrive around the block and come back later

    Answer: B. Ask the other students where the student is

    Counting riders and knowing who belongs at each stop helps you notice a missing student, and the students who are there may know where that student is.

    Manual section 10.2.2 Loading Procedures.

  10. During a post-trip inspection, which items unique to school buses deserve special attention?

    • ASeat covers, floor mats and window shades
    • BMirror systems, flashing warning lamps and stop signal arms
    • CRadio, heater and interior dome lights
    • DFuel cap, battery box and mud flaps

    Answer: B. Mirror systems, flashing warning lamps and stop signal arms

    When looking for mechanical problems after a route or trip, focus on the equipment that sets a school bus apart: its mirror system, warning lamps and stop arms.

    Manual section 10.2.6 Post-trip Inspection.

  11. When approaching a stop, about how far before pulling over should you turn on the right turn signal?

    • AAbout 100-300 feet, or about 3-5 seconds
    • BAbout 500-600 feet, or about 10-15 seconds
    • CAbout 20-50 feet, or about 1 second
    • DOnly after the bus has come to a full stop

    Answer: A. About 100-300 feet, or about 3-5 seconds

    The right signal goes on roughly 100 to 300 feet, or 3 to 5 seconds, ahead of pulling over, which tells others you are about to stop.

    Manual section 10.2.1 Approaching the Stop.

  12. When unloading at school, what should you watch for as students step off the bus?

    • AThat each one shows a bus pass at the door
    • BThat they line up beside the front bumper
    • CThat all of them move promptly away from the unloading area
    • DThat they wait by the door until the bus leaves

    Answer: C. That all of them move promptly away from the unloading area

    As students leave the bus at school, you watch each one get clear of the unloading area without delay.

    Manual section 10.2.4 Unloading Procedures at School.

  13. When you stop at a designated stop, how far from the waiting students should the front bumper be?

    • ARight beside the students, so they take few steps
    • BAt least 3 feet away from the students
    • CAt least 10 feet away from the students
    • DAt least 50 feet away from the students

    Answer: C. At least 10 feet away from the students

    Stopping 10 feet or more short of the students makes them walk up to the bus, which gives you a better view of where they are going.

    Manual section 10.2.1 Approaching the Stop.

  14. During loading, you cannot account for a student outside the bus. What should you do?

    • APull ahead slowly while watching the mirrors
    • BSound the horn and then continue the route
    • CSecure the bus, take the key, and check around and under it
    • DAsk a student to walk around the bus and look

    Answer: C. Secure the bus, take the key, and check around and under it

    A missing student could be beside or beneath the bus, so you make the bus safe, keep the key with you and look around and under it yourself.

    Manual section 10.2.2 Loading Procedures.

Emergency exit and evacuation (7 questions)

  1. If time permits, what should you do before deciding whether to evacuate the bus?

    • AAsk the students to vote on leaving the bus
    • BContact your dispatcher to explain the situation
    • CDrive the bus to the nearest school
    • DWait for a police officer to arrive

    Answer: B. Contact your dispatcher to explain the situation

    When there is time, a call to dispatch describing what is happening should come before you make the evacuation decision.

    Manual section 10.3.1 Planning for Emergencies.

  2. During an evacuation, what should you do with a radio microphone or telephone that still works?

    • ADangle it out of the driver's window for later use
    • BSwitch it off and leave it on the driver's seat
    • CLock it inside the driver's compartment
    • DGive it to the student assistant at the rear exit

    Answer: A. Dangle it out of the driver's window for later use

    Hanging a working radio microphone or phone outside the driver's window keeps it available to use later on.

    Manual section 10.3.2 Evacuation Procedures.

  3. As a general rule, how are student safety and control best kept during an emergency?

    • ABy sending students to the nearest house at once
    • BBy having students wait outside beside the bus
    • CBy keeping students on the bus unless that exposes them to needless risk
    • DBy letting older students decide where everyone goes

    Answer: C. By keeping students on the bus unless that exposes them to needless risk

    Students are usually safest and easiest to manage on the bus, as long as staying there does not put them at unnecessary risk; any decision to leave must be made in time.

    Manual section 10.3.1 Planning for Emergencies.

  4. If there is a risk from spilled hazardous materials, where should you lead the students?

    • ADownwind of the bus, at least 100 feet
    • BBack onto the bus with the windows open
    • CUpwind of the bus, at least 300 feet
    • DUpwind of the bus, about 25 feet

    Answer: C. Upwind of the bus, at least 300 feet

    With a hazardous materials spill, students are taken upwind and kept a long way off, no less than 300 feet from the bus.

    Manual section 10.3.2 Evacuation Procedures.

  5. When should you move a student you believe may have a neck or spinal injury?

    • AAs soon as all other students are off the bus
    • BOnly if the student's life is in immediate danger
    • COnly after the student says the pain is gone
    • DWhenever the bus must be evacuated

    Answer: B. Only if the student's life is in immediate danger

    A student who may have a neck or spine injury stays where they are unless staying would put their life at immediate risk, and special procedures are needed to move such a victim.

    Manual section 10.3.2 Evacuation Procedures.

  6. According to the manual's tips, where is a safe place for students after an evacuation?

    • ARight next to the bus on the shoulder
    • BAt least 25 feet behind the bus
    • CAt least 50 feet ahead of the bus on the shoulder
    • DAt least 100 feet off the road toward oncoming traffic

    Answer: D. At least 100 feet off the road toward oncoming traffic

    Moving students well off the road on the side that traffic comes from keeps them out of reach of debris if another vehicle hits the bus.

    Manual section 10.3.2 Evacuation Procedures.

  7. When possible, how many student assistants should be assigned to each emergency exit?

    • ATwo responsible, older students
    • BOne student from each row of seats
    • CFour students of any age
    • DOnly the student seated nearest the exit

    Answer: A. Two responsible, older students

    Planning ahead means choosing two mature, reliable students for every emergency exit and teaching them how to help the others off the bus.

    Manual section 10.3.2 Evacuation Procedures.

Railroad-highway crossings (7 questions)

  1. As a general rule, how do you work out an acceptable containment or storage area on the far side of a crossing?

    • AAdd 5 feet to the length of the bus
    • BAdd 15 feet to the length of the bus
    • CDouble the length of the bus
    • DUse the length of the bus with nothing added

    Answer: B. Add 15 feet to the length of the bus

    To be sure the whole bus can clear the tracks if it has to stop on the other side, you need room equal to the bus length plus 15 feet.

    Manual section 10.4.4 Special Situations.

  2. The crossing gate comes down after you have started across the tracks. What should you do?

    • AStop and back up off the tracks
    • BStop where you are and wait for the gate
    • CStop and have the students get off
    • DKeep going and drive through the gate

    Answer: D. Keep going and drive through the gate

    Once the bus is crossing, keep moving until the tracks are cleared, even if that means breaking the gate arm.

    Manual section 10.4.3 Recommended Procedures.

  3. The train has passed, but the crossing gate stays down. What should you do?

    • ADrive carefully around the end of the gate
    • BBack up and use the lane on the other side
    • CDo not drive around it; call your dispatcher
    • DRaise the gate by hand and drive through

    Answer: C. Do not drive around it; call your dispatcher

    A gate that does not rise after the train goes by is never a reason to go around it; contact your dispatcher instead.

    Manual section 10.4.2 Warning Signs and Devices.

  4. When approaching a railroad crossing, about how far before it should you activate the hazard lights?

    • AAbout 50 feet before the crossing
    • BAbout 200 feet before the crossing
    • CAbout 1,000 feet before the crossing
    • DOnly once the bus has stopped

    Answer: B. About 200 feet before the crossing

    Turning on the hazard lights roughly 200 feet out lets other drivers know that you plan to stop at the crossing.

    Manual section 10.4.3 Recommended Procedures.

  5. At a multiple-track crossing, where should the school bus stop?

    • AOnly before the first set of tracks
    • BBefore each set of tracks
    • CBetween the last two sets of tracks
    • DOnly after crossing the first set of tracks

    Answer: A. Only before the first set of tracks

    You stop once, ahead of the first track, and when you are sure no train is coming on any track you cross all of them until the bus is fully clear.

    Manual section 10.4.3 Recommended Procedures.

  6. Your bus stalls on the tracks and everyone gets out. Which way should they move?

    • AStraight along the tracks, away from the train
    • BJust off the tracks, next to the bus
    • CFar from the bus at an angle away from the tracks and toward the train
    • DBack to the nearest crossing signal to wait

    Answer: C. Far from the bus at an angle away from the tracks and toward the train

    Everyone gets off the tracks at once and goes well away from the bus, angling both off the tracks and in the direction the train is coming from.

    Manual section 10.4.4 Special Situations.

  7. At a railroad crossing with no white stop line painted on the pavement, where must you stop the bus?

    • AAfter the first set of tracks
    • BJust past the cross-buck sign
    • CWith the front bumper over the near rail
    • DBefore the cross-buck sign

    Answer: D. Before the cross-buck sign

    When there is no painted stop line to guide you, the cross-buck sign marks the point that the bus must stop in front of.

    Manual section 10.4.2 Warning Signs and Devices.

Student management (3 questions)

  1. A behavior problem starts on the bus while students are unloading. When should you deal with it?

    • ARight away, while the door is still open
    • BBefore any more students are let off
    • CAt the end of the route, without stopping
    • DAfter the unloading students are safely off and have moved away

    Answer: D. After the unloading students are safely off and have moved away

    Loading and unloading need all of your attention outside the bus, so the problem waits until the departing students are clear; pull over to handle it if needed.

    Manual section 10.5.1 Don't Deal with On-bus Problems When Loading and Unloading.

  2. Where are you allowed to put a student off the bus?

    • AAt the nearest safe intersection
    • BAt any business with a telephone
    • COnly at school or the student's designated bus stop
    • DAt any parking lot off the road

    Answer: C. Only at school or the student's designated bus stop

    Even when a student misbehaves, the student leaves the bus only at school or at that student's own stop; if you cannot drive safely, call an administrator or the police to remove the student.

    Manual section 10.5.2 Handling Serious Problems.

  3. If a misbehaving student needs a change of seating, where should you ask the student to sit?

    • AIn a seat near you
    • BIn the very last row
    • CIn any empty seat they choose
    • DNext to a student assistant

    Answer: A. In a seat near you

    The manual's tip for a needed seat change is to have the student move up to a seat close to the driver.

    Manual section 10.5.2 Handling Serious Problems.

Antilock brakes (4 questions)

  1. Hydraulically braked trucks and buses with a GVWR of 10,000 lbs or more must have ABS if built on or after which date?

    • AMarch 1, 1998
    • BMarch 1, 1999
    • CJanuary 1, 1995
    • DJanuary 1, 2005

    Answer: B. March 1, 1999

    The ABS rule for hydraulic-brake trucks and buses of 10,000 pounds GVWR or more covers vehicles built from March 1, 1999; the date for air brake vehicles is a year earlier.

    Manual section 10.6.1 Vehicles Required to Have Antilock Braking Systems.

  2. Your bus's ABS malfunctions. What should you do?

    • ADrive normally and get the system serviced soon
    • BStop at once and call for a tow truck
    • CPump the brakes at every stop until repaired
    • DKeep your speed under 5 mph until repaired

    Answer: A. Drive normally and get the system serviced soon

    A failed ABS still leaves the normal brakes working, so you can keep driving as usual, but the system should be repaired soon.

    Manual section 10.6.4 Braking if ABS is Not Working.

  3. Which kind of skid should ABS help prevent?

    • AA skid from spinning the drive wheels
    • BA skid from going too fast in a turn
    • CA skid caused by braking too hard
    • DAny skid, no matter what causes it

    Answer: C. A skid caused by braking too hard

    ABS works against skids that come from over-braking, but it does nothing about power skids or skids from taking a turn too fast.

    Manual section 10.6.5 Safety Reminders.

  4. Braking hard on a slippery surface without ABS, what happens if your steering wheels lock up?

    • AThe bus stops in a much shorter distance
    • BThe rear of the bus always swings left
    • CThe engine automatically shuts off
    • DYou lose steering control

    Answer: D. You lose steering control

    Once the steering wheels lock, they can no longer steer the bus, and ABS helps by keeping the wheels from locking so you stay in control.

    Manual section 10.6.2 How ABS Helps You.

Special safety considerations (4 questions)

  1. If you must back a school bus, what is the purpose of the lookout?

    • ATo give you steering directions while you back
    • BTo keep the students on the bus quiet
    • CTo stop traffic in both directions
    • DTo warn you about obstacles, approaching people and other vehicles

    Answer: D. To warn you about obstacles, approaching people and other vehicles

    The lookout only alerts you to hazards around the bus; the lookout is not supposed to tell you how to back the bus.

    Manual section 10.7.3 Backing.

  2. You are caught in strong winds while driving the school bus. What should you do?

    • ASlow down, or pull off the roadway and wait
    • BSpeed up to get through the windy area sooner
    • CLoosen your grip so the bus can drift with gusts
    • DMove to the left lane to stay off the shoulder

    Answer: A. Slow down, or pull off the roadway and wait

    Lower speed reduces how much the wind pushes on the side of the bus; if needed, get off the road and wait, and keep a firm grip on the wheel.

    Manual section 10.7.2 Driving in High Winds.

  3. If your bus has a roof-mounted white strobe light, when should you use it?

    • AOnly while students are loading at school
    • BWhen you have limited visibility
    • COnly when crossing railroad tracks
    • DWhenever the bus is parked at night

    Answer: B. When you have limited visibility

    The overhead strobe is meant for times when you cannot easily see what is around the bus, always following your state or local rules for its use.

    Manual section 10.7.1 Strobe Lights.

  4. How much tail swing can a school bus have?

    • AUp to one foot
    • BUp to ten feet
    • CUp to three feet
    • DUp to six inches

    Answer: C. Up to three feet

    The rear of a school bus can swing out as much as three feet, so check your mirrors before and during every turn.

    Manual section 10.7.4 Tail Swing.
