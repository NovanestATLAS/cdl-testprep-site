Vehicle inspection (7 questions)

  1. During your check of the outside of the bus, what should you do with an open baggage or engine access panel?

    • AClose it before driving
    • BLeave it open until the first stop
    • CLock it in a partly open position
    • DNote it on the report and drive on

    Answer: A. Close it before driving

    Access panels such as those for baggage, restroom service and the engine must be shut before you drive away, just like any open emergency exits.

    Manual section 4.1.2 Access Doors and Panels.

  2. If your bus has a red emergency door light, when should it be turned on?

    • AOnly when a rider opens the emergency door
    • BAt night or whenever you use your outside lights
    • COnly while the bus is stopped to load riders
    • DOnly when the bus has broken down on the road

    Answer: B. At night or whenever you use your outside lights

    The red emergency door light must work, and it should be on in the dark and at any other time your outside lights are on.

    Manual section 4.1.3 Bus Interior.

  3. What type of tires are not allowed on the front wheels of a bus?

    • ATires with a deep mud and snow tread
    • BTubeless tires with steel belts
    • CTires with a higher load rating
    • DRecapped or re-grooved tires

    Answer: D. Recapped or re-grooved tires

    Part of the tire check before driving is making sure neither front wheel has a tire that was recapped or re-grooved.

    Manual section 4.1.1 Vehicle Systems.

  4. What should you keep in mind while driving with emergency roof hatches partly open?

    • AThe bus's longer stopping distance
    • BThe bus's wider turning path
    • CThe bus's higher clearance
    • DThe bus's lower fuel mileage

    Answer: C. The bus's higher clearance

    With roof hatches open the bus needs more overhead room than usual, so watch for low clearances until they are closed.

    Manual section 4.1.4 Roof Hatches.

  5. When should you sign the previous driver's inspection report for your bus?

    • AAs soon as you have read it, before checking any defects
    • BOnly if earlier defects were certified repaired or not needing repair
    • CAfter you have driven the bus for its first trip of the day
    • DWhenever the previous driver listed no more than two defects

    Answer: B. Only if earlier defects were certified repaired or not needing repair

    Your signature certifies that the problems found earlier were dealt with, so sign only after they are certified as repaired or as not needing repair.

    Manual section 4.1 Vehicle Inspection.

  6. Why should you always check the interior of your bus before driving?

    • ARiders must watch the driver complete the check
    • BIt takes the place of the outside inspection
    • CThe seats must be rearranged for every trip
    • DPeople sometimes damage buses left unattended

    Answer: D. People sometimes damage buses left unattended

    A bus that sat unattended may have been damaged by someone, so an inside check before each trip helps keep your riders safe.

    Manual section 4.1.3 Bus Interior.

  7. If a bus uses fuses rather than reflective triangles as its emergency reflectors, at least how many fuses must it carry?

    • A3 fuses
    • B4 fuses
    • C6 fuses
    • D8 fuses

    Answer: C. 6 fuses

    The law calls for three reflective triangles, three liquid burning flares, or a minimum of six fuses as the bus's emergency reflectors.

    Manual section 4.1.1 Vehicle Systems.

Loading and trip start (8 questions)

  1. Where must riders not be allowed to leave their carry-on baggage?

    • AIn a doorway or aisle
    • BUnder their own seats
    • COn the overhead racks
    • DIn the baggage bay below

    Answer: A. In a doorway or aisle

    Bags left in a doorway or aisle can trip other riders, so those spaces must stay clear of carry-ons.

    Manual section 4.2 Loading and Trip Start.

  2. Why should charter bus drivers keep riders off the bus until departure time?

    • ATo keep the fuel tank from running low
    • BTo give riders time to buy their tickets
    • CTo help prevent theft or vandalism of the bus
    • DTo keep the standee line clear for loading

    Answer: C. To help prevent theft or vandalism of the bus

    Keeping a charter bus closed to riders until it is time to leave helps protect the bus from theft and damage.

    Manual section 4.2.4 At Your Destination.

  3. How many different diamond-shaped hazard labels are there?

    • AFour
    • BSix
    • CEight
    • DNine

    Answer: D. Nine

    There are nine hazard labels in all, each a four-inch diamond, which is why you should watch for that shape on cargo and baggage.

    Manual section 4.2.1 Hazardous Materials.

  4. Shippers must mark containers of hazardous material with the material's name, its identification number and:

    • AThe shipper's phone number
    • BThe weight of the contents
    • CThe hazard label
    • DThe date it was packed

    Answer: C. The hazard label

    Each container of hazardous material must carry three markings from the shipper: the material's name, its ID number and its hazard label.

    Manual section 4.2.1 Hazardous Materials.

  5. What is the most total weight of allowed hazardous materials a bus may carry?

    • A250 pounds
    • B500 pounds
    • C750 pounds
    • D1,000 pounds

    Answer: B. 500 pounds

    A bus must never carry over 500 pounds of allowed hazardous materials in all, and no more than 100 pounds of any one class.

    Manual section 4.2.2 Forbidden Hazardous Materials.

  6. When may a bus carry small amounts of some other hazardous materials?

    • AWhen riders sign a written waiver
    • BWhen the shipper cannot send them any other way
    • CWhen the bus is less than half full
    • DWhen the trip is shorter than 50 miles

    Answer: B. When the shipper cannot send them any other way

    Beyond the items buses may always carry, small amounts of some other hazardous materials are allowed only when the shipper has no other way to ship them.

    Manual section 4.2.2 Forbidden Hazardous Materials.

  7. No rider may stand forward of:

    • AThe rear of the driver's seat
    • BThe front edge of the stairwell
    • CThe first row of rider seats
    • DThe center of the front door

    Answer: A. The rear of the driver's seat

    Standing riders must stay behind the back of the driver's seat, and buses that allow standing mark this limit on the floor.

    Manual section 4.2.3 Standee Line.

  8. If the aisle is lower than the seats, when is it best to remind riders about the step-down?

    • AAfter the front door has been opened
    • BOnly when a rider asks for help
    • CAt the start of the trip only
    • DBefore the bus comes to a complete stop

    Answer: D. Before the bus comes to a complete stop

    Give the step-down reminder while the bus is still moving toward the stop, not after it has already stopped.

    Manual section 4.2.4 At Your Destination.

On the road (9 questions)

  1. If your bus leans toward the outside on a banked curve, what does that mean?

    • AThe tires need more air pressure
    • BThe load has shifted to one side
    • CYou are driving too fast
    • DThe curve is banked the wrong way

    Answer: C. You are driving too fast

    A bus leaning outward on a banked curve is a sign of too much speed, so slow down for the curve.

    Manual section 4.3.4 Speed on Curves.

  2. When you must stop at a drawbridge, at least how far before the draw should you stop?

    • A15 feet
    • B25 feet
    • C50 feet
    • D100 feet

    Answer: C. 50 feet

    Where a stop is required at a drawbridge, keep a gap of no less than 50 feet between your bus and the draw.

    Manual section 4.3.6 Drawbridges.

  3. Where do bus accidents often happen?

    • AOn long, straight highways
    • BIn bus parking garages
    • COn divided four-lane roads
    • DAt intersections

    Answer: D. At intersections

    Intersections are a common place for bus crashes, so stay careful there even when signals or stop signs control the other traffic.

    Manual section 4.3.3 Common Accidents.

  4. When pulling out from a bus stop into traffic, what should you do?

    • AWait until a gap big enough for the bus opens
    • BSignal and expect other drivers to brake for you
    • CEase out slowly so traffic will make room
    • DSound the horn to warn drivers you are coming

    Answer: A. Wait until a gap big enough for the bus opens

    Know how much space the bus needs to speed up and merge, and do not leave the stop until that space is there.

    Manual section 4.3.3 Common Accidents.

  5. At which railroad crossing do you not have to stop, though you must still slow down and check for other vehicles?

    • AOne on a rural road without gates
    • BOne where the tracks look clear
    • COne where the crossing lights are off
    • DOne where a flagman is directing traffic

    Answer: D. One where a flagman is directing traffic

    A stop is not required where a police officer or flagman is directing traffic, but you must still slow down and look carefully for other vehicles.

    Manual section 4.3.5 Railroad-highway Crossing/ Stops.

  6. When stopped at a railroad crossing, when should you open your forward door?

    • AOnly when the crossing has no gates or lights
    • BIf it helps you see or hear an approaching train
    • COnly after the front wheels reach the first rail
    • DOnly when a flagman tells you to proceed

    Answer: B. If it helps you see or hear an approaching train

    Opening the front door can make it easier to hear or spot a train that is coming, so do it whenever it improves your check.

    Manual section 4.3.5 Railroad-highway Crossing/ Stops.

  7. If your bus has a manual transmission, what must you never do while crossing railroad tracks?

    • AChange gears
    • BSound the horn
    • CCheck your mirrors
    • DUse the turn signal

    Answer: A. Change gears

    Shifting while on the tracks is not allowed with a manual transmission; stay in the same gear until you are across.

    Manual section 4.3.5 Railroad-highway Crossing/ Stops.

  8. When should you mention the carrier's rules about smoking, drinking or playing radios?

    • AAt the start of the trip
    • BOnly after a rider breaks a rule
    • CAt the first rest stop
    • DWhen riders leave at the destination

    Answer: A. At the start of the trip

    Explaining the comfort and safety rules as the trip begins helps you avoid trouble with riders later on.

    Manual section 4.3.1 Passenger Supervision.

  9. After riders get on at a stop, what should you wait for before starting the bus?

    • AFor riders to put every carry-on in the overhead rack
    • BFor riders to sit down or brace themselves
    • CFor riders to show their tickets a second time
    • DFor the interior aisle lights to switch off

    Answer: B. For riders to sit down or brace themselves

    Riders can stumble when the bus moves off, so give them time to be seated or holding on before you start.

    Manual section 4.3.2 At Stops.

After-trip vehicle inspection (2 questions)

  1. If you drive for an interstate carrier, what must you complete for each bus you drove?

    • AA written inspection report
    • BA rider head count form
    • CA fuel purchase record
    • DA route timing sheet

    Answer: A. A written inspection report

    Drivers for an interstate carrier must write up an inspection report for every bus they drove, listing any defect that affects safety or could cause a breakdown.

    Manual section 4.4 After-trip Vehicle Inspection.

  2. Why should you report rider damage to parts such as seats and handholds at the end of your shift?

    • ASo the riders can be charged for the damage
    • BSo mechanics can repair it before the bus goes out again
    • CSo the bus can be kept off its route for a week
    • DSo the next driver can decide whether to fix it

    Answer: B. So mechanics can repair it before the bus goes out again

    Reporting damage when your shift ends gives mechanics time to fix safety-related parts before the bus is used for another trip.

    Manual section 4.4 After-trip Vehicle Inspection.

Prohibited practices (2 questions)

  1. Where must you never refuel a bus with riders on board?

    • AAt an outdoor truck stop
    • BAt a self-service fuel island
    • CAt the carrier's own fuel pump
    • DIn a closed building

    Answer: D. In a closed building

    Fueling with riders aboard should be avoided unless truly needed, and it is never allowed inside a closed building.

    Manual section 4.5 Prohibited Practices.

  2. When may you tow or push a disabled bus while riders are still aboard?

    • AWhenever the tow is less than one mile
    • BWhen all riders agree to stay on board
    • COnly when getting off would be unsafe for them
    • DWhen your employer cannot send another bus

    Answer: C. Only when getting off would be unsafe for them

    Riders should leave a disabled bus before it is towed or pushed, unless leaving the bus would put them in danger.

    Manual section 4.5 Prohibited Practices.

Brake-door interlocks (2 questions)

  1. On an urban mass transit coach, what does the brake and accelerator interlock do when the rear door is open?

    • AApplies the brakes and holds the throttle at idle
    • BSounds a buzzer and flashes the brake lights
    • CShuts off the engine and turns on hazard lights
    • DLocks the front door and releases the brakes

    Answer: A. Applies the brakes and holds the throttle at idle

    While the rear door is open, the interlock keeps the brakes on and the throttle at idle so the coach cannot move off.

    Manual section 4.6 Use of Brake-door Interlocks.

  2. What releases the brake and accelerator interlock on a transit coach?

    • APressing the accelerator pedal down hard
    • BClosing the rear door
    • CTurning the ignition off and back on
    • DOpening the front door

    Answer: B. Closing the rear door

    Once the rear door is shut, the interlock lets go of the brakes and the throttle.

    Manual section 4.6 Use of Brake-door Interlocks.
